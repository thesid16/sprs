\begin{abstract}
Floating-point reductions executed across multi-accelerator systems are not
bitwise reproducible under \emph{cluster reshape}: when the number of
processing elements $G$ or the physical topology changes, the underlying
collective's reduction order changes, and so do the last bits of the result.
We measure that premise rather than assume it: reducing identical input
\emph{bytes} at \GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts from $\GpuWorldLo$ to
$\GpuWorldHi$ under PyTorch's collectives returns \GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct
64-bit roots, spreads reaching \GpuUlpMax{}~ulp, while repeats at a fixed
shape are bitwise identical --- the sweep running above $G{=}2$ on the CPU
backend, because NCCL requires one rank per device and the node has two GPUs.
Rerunning the same deterministic computation on a resized cluster therefore
breaks bit-for-bit regression testing and audit-grade replay.  We argue that
reduction
order is an \emph{interface} concern rather than a library concern, and treat
it as a compiler/fabric Application Binary Interface: a size-indexed contract
that fixes the operand binding at every internal node of a canonical full
binary tree $\FBT(N)$.  Bitwise reproducibility under a change of resource
count is not new, and no conjunct below is ours alone; what we claim is the
conjunction.  The order is fixed at the compiler/fabric interface and enforced
in hardware by scoreboard-gated operand binding, so a violated assumption
fail-stops rather than returning a quietly different number; the result is
invariant across network \emph{topologies} and not resource counts alone; the
operator is an unmodified IEEE-754 FP64 addition with no wide accumulator; and
$N$ is arbitrary rather than a power of two.  Given the ABI, invariance is
close to definitional: if every \texttt{COMPUTE} always adds the same
predetermined pair of addresses, neither timing nor placement can reach the
result.  What is not definitional, and what we test, is whether the discipline
survives contact with a real compiler search space and real RTL.  We
instantiate it as \sprs{}: a topology-parametric SystemVerilog
network-on-chip with a four-opcode reduction ISA and a combinational
flush-to-zero/round-to-nearest-even FADD, plus a seven-stage compiler of
which this campaign exercises \StagesRun{}.  We
state two properties under six compiler obligations and nine fabric
invariants --- \emph{cross-topology bitwise invariance}
(Claim~\ref{claim:1}) and \emph{presence-gated fail-stop}
(Property~\ref{prop:2}) --- connected by an explicit \emph{mapping/topology
orthogonality} theorem.  The compiler's algorithmic configuration space spans
$18\times 10\times 122 \approx 22{,}000$ (assignment, mapping, refinement)
triples per instance; the campaign enumerates the refinement-free face of that
space \emph{exhaustively} --- all \SelConfigsTotal{} assignment/mapping pairs
on each of \SuiteSize{} instances, $0.82\%$ of the triples, with refinement
fixed at \sv{None} --- and RTL-validates every distinct program image
produced on the \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances.  Of
\InvAttempted{} images attempted on \TournNInst{}
hardware-validated instances spanning $N\!\in\![8,8192]$,
$G\!\in\![2,2048]$ and six direct-network topologies, \WallTBs{} settled and
every one produced a root bit-identical to the canonical root;
\InvNonSettling{} did not settle, all but one a fabric watchdog fail-stop, and
no run produced a finite root differing from the canonical one.  An
injected-fault campaign establishes oracle non-vacuity on
\MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of \MutTotal{} verdicts, the remainder being
fabric fail-stops rather than value comparisons.  Two defect classes in our own
artifact were nonetheless invisible to that campaign \emph{by construction} ---
the leaves are all positive, so no run asserts the adder's
effective-subtraction path, and the oracle is a transcription of the RTL adder
that carried the identical defect --- and we report that as a finding about
exhaustive evaluation rather than an erratum.  Because the compiler's
search space cannot reach the bit result, we report what it can reach: a
cross-validated portfolio of \SelConfigsRun{} of the \SelConfigsTotal{}
configurations attains the hardware-measured optimum on \SelKFourOpt{} of
\TournNInst{} held-out instances and, refitted with an entire topology
withheld, has mean out-of-sample regret \SelUnseenTopoMean{} on the topology
it never saw (95\% bootstrap interval
$[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$) --- better than a rule that conditions
on topology and does see it in training (\SelCondTopoMean{}).  Every regime map
we could fit over $(N, G, \text{topology})$ is worse out of sample than not
conditioning at all.

reproducibility is the requirement and the collective is not the bottleneck,
and not inside a bandwidth-critical loop.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{D}{istributed} reductions are the connective tissue of modern
parallel computation: gradient all-reduce in synchronous data-parallel
training~\cite{sergeev2018horovod,li2020pytorch}, partial-sum aggregation in
scientific codes~\cite{dongarra2011hpcchallenge}, logits reduction in
pipelined inference. In each case, a multiset of per-worker partial sums is
collapsed into a single scalar or tensor through a collective whose
performance has been the subject of more than a decade of aggressive
engineering~\cite{thakur2005optimization,nccl2018,cai2021synthesizing,shah2023taccl}.
Performance, however, is not the same as determinism, and the two are in
tension. Production implementations of all-reduce --- ring, recursive
halving--doubling, double-binary tree, hierarchical hybrid --- select a
reduction order that maximises throughput for the observed network topology
and worker count~\cite{rabenseifner2004optimization,nccl2018}.  When either
changes, so does the order.

IEEE~754 double-precision addition is non-associative:
$(a+b)+c \neq a+(b+c)$ in the low-order bits whenever alignment shifts a
non-zero mantissa below the round bit. The classical bound~\cite{higham2002accuracy}
gives, for two reduction orders $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ of the same multiset,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\,{\textstyle\sum_\sigma x_i} - {\textstyle\sum_{\sigma'} x_i}\,\bigr|
  \;\leq\; (N-1)\,u\,{\textstyle\sum_i} |x_i| + \mathcal{O}(u^2),
  \label{eq:higham}
\end{equation}
with $u$ the unit in the last place. The classical statement bounds one
summation order against the exact sum; applying it to the difference of two
orders costs a factor of two by the triangle inequality, which we absorb into
the constant since only the scaling matters here. Under cancellation this
bound is catastrophically loose and the actual discrepancy is a function of the
summation tree. The consequence is concrete, and we measure it rather than
assert it (\S\ref{sec:results-premise}). Reducing identical input
\emph{bytes} at \GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts from $\GpuWorldLo$ to
$\GpuWorldHi$ under PyTorch's distributed
collectives~\cite{li2020pytorch} --- same data, same seed, same code, only the
shape of the job changing --- returns \GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct 64-bit
results, with spreads reaching \GpuUlpMax{}~ulp and at least
\GpuElemsMovedMin{} of the \GpuElemsTotal{} elements of the result vector
moving in every case, while repeated runs at any \emph{fixed} shape are
bitwise identical \GpuRepeats{} times out of \GpuRepeats{}. Reshape moves the
answer; noise does not. The rank-count sweep above $G{=}2$ runs on the CPU
backend, because NCCL requires one rank per device and this node carries two
GPUs; \S\ref{sec:results-premise} states that scope in full and draws no
timing claim from the measurement. In a training loop the difference does not
stay local: the reduced gradient becomes the next step's weights, so a
bit-level divergence at the first step is an input difference at the second.
Bit-for-bit regression testing of scientific
codes fails under the same mechanism: a code that ran to acceptance on a
development cluster produces different last bits on the production cluster,
and the reviewer cannot distinguish a new bug from the reshape artefact. In
regulated deployments --- medical-device inference, aerospace controls,
financial model replay --- the inability to reproduce a computation bitwise
across hardware generations is increasingly disqualifying.

\subsection{Two Sources of Non-Determinism, One Bad Outcome}

The cluster-reshape non-determinism we target has two distinct sources,
which current software tools confuse.

\emph{Source 1 --- order-dependence of the reducer.} The collective chooses
a reduction tree or ring shape as a function of the runtime environment. In
NCCL, for instance, the (algorithm, protocol) pair is selected at
communicator initialisation by an internal cost model parameterised by GPU
and CPU architecture, node and rank counts and message
size~\cite{nccl2018}; the data are never consulted. Even with all
randomness suppressed, the algorithm is therefore a function of
$(G,\text{topology})$, not of the input, and reshaping the deployment
changes it deterministically.

\emph{Source 2 --- reassociation inside the reducer.} Within a chosen
algorithm, performance-critical implementations reorder partial sums to
hide latency --- overlapping pipeline segments, exploiting SIMD
lanes, merging small messages. Each micro-optimisation
is an implicit reassociation, and each changes the last bit.

Both sources have been attacked before, and two of the existing answers
reach further than is usually acknowledged. Order-independent accumulators
--- ReproBLAS~\cite{demmel2013reproBLAS}, ExBLAS~\cite{iakymchuk2016exblas},
and the bit-reproducible distributed reductions of Arteaga et
al.~\cite{arteaga2014bitreproducible} --- eliminate both sources at cluster
scale by making the operator effectively associative; their price is that
the answer is no longer the IEEE-754 FP64 sum that any particular addition
order would produce, and that a wide accumulator datatype is required.
Fixed-order schemes keep the FP64 operator and pin the order instead, but
each along one axis inside one implementation: Intel oneMKL's strict
conditional-numerical-reproducibility mode is bitwise identical regardless of
thread count and input partitioning~\cite{onemkl_cnr}, and
TBIK~\cite{tbik2025} is bitwise identical across tensor-parallel size, while
the ``deterministic'' modes of production collectives hold only within one
$(G,\text{topology})$~\cite{nccl2018,rccl2019}. Compensated
summation~\cite{kahan1965summation,rump2009accurate} reduces the error of a
reduction without fixing either source. Section~\ref{sec:related} states each
of these relationships precisely.

What no line of work states is the invariant \emph{at the interface},
enforced below the software that could violate it. What we set out to obtain
is therefore a conjunction rather than a first: reduction order fixed as a
\emph{size-indexed contract} between the collective-scheduling compiler and
the fabric, so that the 64-bit root is invariant not only under worker count
but under network topology, task assignment, physical placement, routing and
schedule; with the operator an unmodified IEEE-754 FP64 addition and no wide
accumulator; enforced in hardware by scoreboard-gated operand binding, so
that a violated assumption fail-stops instead of returning a quietly
different number; and holding for arbitrary, not merely power-of-two, leaf
counts. Every conjunct has a precedent; the conjunction, as far as we have
been able to determine, does not.

\subsection{Position: Reduction Order Is an Interface Concern}

We take the position that bitwise reproducibility under cluster reshape
cannot be achieved inside any single layer of the conventional software
stack, and must instead be moved into the \emph{interface} between the
collective-scheduling compiler and the underlying fabric.  A library-level
flag cannot forbid Source~1, because the library has no authority over
whether a different cluster size will be launched tomorrow.  A fabric-level
guarantee alone cannot forbid Source~2, because the fabric cannot see the
reduction tree a scheduler intends to use. The discipline has to be a
contract shared by both.

The principle is not new; its reach is. IEEE~754-2019 already makes
reproducibility an interface obligation rather than an implementation
property: reproducible results, it states, require cooperation from language
standards, language processors and users, and the reproducible attribute it
specifies forbids the processor from reordering or fusing
operations~\cite{ieee754-2019}. That obligation binds language processors,
within one program; it says nothing about how many workers run a collective
or how they are wired. We extend it to the interface the standard does not
reach --- between a collective-scheduling compiler and the fabric --- and
index it by problem size, so that it survives a change of deployment.

Concretely, let the operation be:
\begin{equation}
  R \;=\; x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus x_{N-1},
  \qquad \oplus \;\triangleq\;\text{IEEE-754 FP64 ADD}.
\end{equation}
We define a size-indexed contract: for every internal node $v$ of a
canonical full binary tree $\FBT(N)$, the compiler emits exactly one
compute instruction whose operand pair is $(\mathrm{left}(v),
\mathrm{right}(v))$, and the fabric's arithmetic unit reads those operands
at a single clock edge after a scoreboard gate confirms both have been
written.  The contract is indexed only by $N$; it says nothing about which
processing element hosts which node, how packets are routed, or when any
instruction fires.  These are left free precisely so that schedulers may
continue to optimise performance without touching the contract.

Under this discipline, the observed 64-bit root result is a pure function
of $N$ and the leaf vector $L$, irrespective of $(G,\text{topology})$,
assignment $\alpha$, mapping $\mu$, refinement $r$, or timing. We call this
property \emph{fabric self-consistency under cluster reshape}. To be
clear, we do not claim that this result agrees with any external reducer's
output --- that would be a different (stronger) claim, requiring a shared
canonical form. We claim that any two executions of our system on the same
$L$ agree bit-for-bit, under any reshape within a declared family $\T$ of
flat direct-network topologies.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper makes three contributions, supported throughout by a
compile-time post-condition on the emitted image, root equality against a
software oracle on every validated run, and a mutation campaign that shows
the oracle is not vacuous (\S\ref{sec:limitations} states what that oracle
can and cannot witness). We use ``mechanised'' nowhere in its
formal-methods sense: the invariance argument is by code inspection and
dynamic checking, not machine-checked proof (\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).

\textbf{C1 --- Reduction order as a size-indexed compiler/fabric ABI, with a
structural invariance argument.} We claim the framing, not the difficulty:
once operand binding is a function of tree-node identity alone, invariance
follows by unwinding the definitions, and we say so rather than dressing it
as a discovery. We define the ABI as post-order operand binding over
$\FBT(N)$ together with a canonical leaf enumeration, and we state two
properties that follow from it. \emph{Claim~\ref{claim:1} (cross-topology
bitwise invariance)} establishes that, under six compiler obligations and
nine fabric invariants, the 64-bit root result is a pure function of
$(N, L)$ --- unchanged by worker count, network topology, assignment,
placement, routing or schedule, with the operator an unmodified IEEE-754
FP64 addition and no wide accumulator, for arbitrary non-power-of-two $N$.
\emph{Property~\ref{prop:2} (presence-gated fail-stop)} establishes that
any assumption violation leaving an operand absent yields no root at all ---
a localised \texttt{P\_ERROR} with diagnostic context, and no completion ---
and we state the boundary of that property rather than claiming the fabric
catches everything. The two are connected by an explicit
\emph{mapping/topology orthogonality} theorem. Bitwise reproducibility under
a change of resource count is not itself new (\S\ref{sec:related}); what C1
claims is the conjunction of that invariant with this enforcement locus,
this failure mode, this range of $N$, and invariance across network
topologies rather than resource counts alone.

\textbf{C2 --- A compiler and fabric that jointly realise the ABI.} The
\sprs{} compiler translates $(N, G, \text{topology})$ into a per-PE
instruction image through the \StagesRun{} stages this campaign exercises: a
composite lower-bound $\Omega$ (S0); $\FBT(N)$ instantiation (S1);
task-to-worker \emph{assignment} selected from eighteen algorithms spanning
list-scheduling, graph partitioning, tree dynamic programming, and
critical-path variants (S2); worker-to-physical-PE \emph{mapping} from ten
algorithms (S3); and code emission with cycle-accurate NOP padding (S6).
Three further passes exist in the compiler but produced no result reported
here: connectivity repair (S2.5), memory enforcement against the
$\DmemDepth$-entry IMEM/DMEM budgets (S5), and refinement (S4, twelve base methods
chainable to $122$ configurations), fixed at the identity throughout.
Operand binding is by node identity, so it is indifferent to whether an
assignment leaves a worker's task set connected, and the invariance result
therefore quantifies over the raw output of all eighteen assignment
algorithms. The fabric is a
topology-parametric SystemVerilog NoC with a four-opcode reduction ISA,
a three-stage IF--DE--EX pipeline with two-level DMEM write-to-read
forwarding, a scoreboard gate at DE, and a combinational IEEE-754 adder
implementing flush-to-zero subnormals and round-to-nearest-even rounding.
Topology is a runtime configuration of adjacency and routing tables, not
an RTL artefact.

\textbf{C3 --- Hardware validation at scale, and a structural pricing of the ABI.}
We evaluate on \SuiteSize{} instances spanning \InvLeafCounts{} leaf counts
$N \in [8,8192]$, requested worker counts $G \in [2,4096]$, and six
direct-network topologies (linear, ring, mesh, torus, fat-tree, hypercube).
The compiler's algorithmic configuration space spans
$\approx\!22{,}000$ (assignment, mapping, refinement) triples per instance;
the campaign enumerates the refinement-free face of that space
\emph{exhaustively} --- all $\SelConfigsTotal$ assignment/mapping pairs on
every instance, \CompileTotal{} compilations, $0.82\%$ of the triples, with
refinement fixed at \sv{None} --- and RTL-validates with cycle-accurate
Vivado xsim every distinct program image produced on the \TournNInst{}
hardware-validated instances. Of \InvAttempted{} images
attempted over \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances, \WallTBs{} settled
and in every one the root PE's final result is bit-identical to the canonical
root $\Rcan(N,L)$ the software oracle predicts for that $N$; \InvNonSettling{}
did not settle, all but one a fabric watchdog fail-stop, and no configuration
produced a finite root differing from $\Rcan(N,L)$. Mutation testing under
injected address-substitution and leaf-corruption faults shows the oracle is
not vacuous, and separates fail-stop from wrong-root outcomes exactly where
Property~\ref{prop:2} places the boundary. We price the ABI structurally
rather than against a non-deterministic baseline: the fabric's ALU is
two-input, so no same-fabric reducer is shallower than the canonical tree, and
what the ABI can cost is leaf-generation serialisation and root-gather fan-in,
which bind any reducer here. Finally, because the compiler's search space is
provably incapable of changing the bit result, we report what it \emph{can}
change: a cross-validated portfolio of \SelConfigsRun{} of the
\SelConfigsTotal{} configurations attains the hardware-measured optimum on
\SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{} held-out instances, while every regime map we
could fit over $(N, G, \text{topology})$ does worse out of sample than not
conditioning at all. Separately, we export the discipline to production
collectives in software --- \emph{pinned-FBT} (\S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt})
--- correcting the index-range construction we published, which is exact only
at power-of-two $N$ and $G$, and measuring the cut-aligned repair for arbitrary
$N$ on both counts that matter: bitwise reshape-invariance against a negative
control that fires, and cost against stock \texttt{ncclAllReduce}.

\subsection{Non-Goals}

To forestall mis-scoping, we state explicitly what this paper does
\emph{not} claim.

\emph{Interoperability.} \sprs{} results are self-consistent under reshape
for a fixed $\sprs{}$/fabric pair. They do not match NCCL, RCCL,
ReproBLAS, ExBLAS, or any external reducer whose canonical order differs.
Interoperability requires either a thin input-permutation adapter (at
zero runtime cost, trading the cross-reshape guarantee) or a shared
canonical form --- a direction we sketch in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\emph{Strict IEEE 754.} The FADD unit implements FTZ subnormals on input
and output, RNE rounding only, a deterministic but non-canonical qNaN
propagation rule, and a deterministic signed-zero rule that returns $+0$
on cancellation. These choices are documented (\S\ref{sec:fadd}) and
strictly narrower than IEEE~754-2019~\cite{ieee754-2019}; a subnormal-aware
alternative inherits Claim~\ref{claim:1} by construction.

\emph{Silicon scalability.} The fabric is a research prototype
characterised by cycle-accurate RTL simulation. We make no claim of timing closure, area, power, or
frequency at production-die scale, and we do not benchmark \sprs{} against
production all-reduce libraries running on NVLink/NVSwitch silicon. The one
head-to-head we do report --- the software export of
\S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt} against stock \texttt{ncclAllReduce} on a PCIe
pair --- prices that export, and not the fabric.

\emph{Novel component algorithms.} Our eighteen assignment algorithms, ten
mappings, twelve refinements, minimal-distance routing, credit-based flow
control, and scoreboarded pipeline are all individually due to
prior work~\cite{topcuoglu2002heft,lukes1974efficient,fiduccia1982linear,%
yang1994dsc,karypis1998multilevel,jeannot2014treematch,%
dally1987deadlock,dally2004principles}. Our contribution is not a new component
but the interface observation: that \emph{if} compiler and fabric agree
on a size-indexed reduction tree as a shared contract, \emph{then} any
choice among those prior-art components inherits cross-topology invariance
by composition. The entire compiler variation space is structurally
orthogonal to the ABI; the campaign searches a space incapable of changing
the bit result.

\section{Background and Threat Model}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{IEEE 754 Non-Associativity}

Let $x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{N-1}$ be $N$ binary64 values and let $\sigma$ be
a summation order induced by a binary reduction tree $\tau$. Denote by
$\Sigma_\tau(x)$ the 64-bit result of evaluating $\tau$ with RNE rounding
at every internal node. For two trees $\tau, \tau'$, the worst-case
componentwise bound~\eqref{eq:higham} is loose in the generic case but
tight in the presence of cancellation: when
$\sum_i x_i = 0$ by exact real arithmetic but intermediate partial sums
differ in magnitude, the last bits of $\Sigma_\tau$ and $\Sigma_{\tau'}$
can differ by an exponent-magnitude of the largest partial sum. The
practical consequence for reductions of noisy tensors is that two trees
executing on identical $x$ will, with overwhelming probability, produce
different last bits of $\Sigma$.

Production collectives choose $\tau$ as a function of their schedule.
Ring all-reduce~\cite{patarasuk2009bandwidth} orders along a physical
worker ring; a recursive halving--doubling all-reduce~\cite{rabenseifner2004optimization}
orders by a power-of-two recursive pattern; double-binary-tree
all-reduce~\cite{sanders2009two} uses two interleaved binary trees.
Runtime-hybrid collectives~\cite{nccl2018,rccl2019} select among these per
message size, using topology discovered at communicator initialisation and
an internal cost model parameterised by architecture and rank count. In each
case $\tau$ is a function of $(G, \text{topology}, \text{message size})$ and
never of the data. Reshaping the cluster reshapes $\tau$ and therefore
$\Sigma_\tau$.

\subsection{Threat Model}

We target a specific, precisely stated reproducibility property.

\begin{definition}[Cross-topology bitwise reproducibility]\label{def:ctbr}
Let $L \in (\mathrm{bits}_{64})^N$ be a fixed leaf-value vector. For every
$(G, \text{topology}) \in \T$ (the declared family of supported direct
networks), every valid \sprs{} compilation, and every valid runtime
execution of the fabric, the observed 64-bit root result is the same
sequence of bits.
\end{definition}

The family $\T$ contains flat direct networks whose adjacency can be
realised by a configuration of the per-router adjacency and routing tables:
linear, ring, 2D mesh, 2D torus, hypercube, and fat-tree. The property is
\emph{structural} --- it is a statement about what the fabric--compiler
pair can be proven to guarantee, not an empirical average over executions.

Out of scope: (i) agreement between \sprs{} and external reducers at
different canonical orders; (ii) tolerance to single-event upsets or
transient hardware faults; (iii) cross-fabric portability of bit patterns
between fabrics with different FADD microarchitectures; (iv) adversarial
traffic patterns affecting cycle counts (these cannot affect the bit
result; head-of-line behaviour is discussed in \S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}).

\subsection{Prior Reproducibility Approaches}

Table~\ref{tab:prior} classifies prior approaches. Two groups already

and fixed-order schemes do it with the operator intact but each within one

\section{The Reduction ABI and the Invariance Claim}\label{sec:abi}
% =====================================================================

This section is the paper's theoretical core. We define the canonical
reduction tree (\S\ref{sec:abi-tree}), state the ABI as a pair of
binding disciplines (\S\ref{sec:abi-def}), and enumerate the fifteen
assumptions the two properties rest on, partitioned into compiler
obligations and fabric invariants (\S\ref{sec:abi-assumptions}). Eleven
of the fifteen are hypotheses of cross-topology bitwise invariance
(Claim~\ref{claim:1}); the other four are liveness invariants used only
by the presence-gated fail-stop property (Property~\ref{prop:2}). Which
assumption belongs where is not a matter of taste --- we state below,
for each one, the failure it does and does not prevent, because that is
what a reader reimplementing the ABI needs. The supporting arguments are
given in \S\ref{sec:abi-args}. A key corollary --- that the mapping and
topology choices are \emph{orthogonal} to the bit result --- is stated
and argued as an explicit proposition in \S\ref{sec:abi-orth}. We close with the FADD policy
(\S\ref{sec:fadd}), edge cases (\S\ref{sec:abi-edge}), and a discussion
of runtime checks and a path to mechanisation
(\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).

\subsection{The Canonical Tree}\label{sec:abi-tree}

\begin{definition}[Canonical full binary tree]\label{def:fbt}
For $N \geq 1$, the \emph{canonical full binary tree} $\FBT(N)$ is the
heap-indexed binary tree with $2N-1$ nodes: root at index $0$, left child of
$i$ at $2i+1$, right child at $2i+2$. The set of internal nodes is
$\{0,1,\ldots,N-2\}$; the set of leaf nodes is $\{N-1,\ldots,2N-2\}$. Since
$2N-1$ is always odd, every internal node has exactly two children: the
single-child edge case is eliminated at the tree level.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical leaf enumeration]\label{def:leafenum}
Let $\mathcal{L}(N) \subset \{0,\ldots,2N-2\}$ be the leaf set of $\FBT(N)$.
The canonical enumeration $\mathrm{idx}: \mathcal{L}(N) \to \{0,\ldots,N-1\}$
assigns index $k$ to the $k$-th smallest \emph{heap index} in
$\mathcal{L}(N)$.
\end{definition}

This is worth stating precisely, because the obvious informal paraphrase is
wrong. Ascending heap index and left-to-right \emph{visual} order coincide
exactly when $N$ is a power of two. When it is not, $\FBT(N)$ is
depth-imbalanced: some leaves sit one level higher than the rest, and a
higher leaf carries a \emph{smaller} heap index than the deeper leaves to its
left. For $N=7$ the leaf set is $\{6,\ldots,12\}$; ascending heap index gives
$\langle 6,7,8,9,10,11,12\rangle$, whereas left-to-right traversal gives
$\langle 7,8,9,10,11,12,6\rangle$ --- node $6$ is a leaf at depth two and
appears \emph{last} visually. Seven of the sixteen leaf counts we evaluate
($10, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1500, 3000$) are of this shape, and at $N=200$ the
two enumerations produce root values differing by one ulp. The ABI is defined
by heap index; an implementation using visual order is a \emph{different}
contract, not an equivalent restatement of this one.

The post-order traversal of $\FBT(N)$ --- left, right, node --- is a pure
function of $N$. We write $\pi_N$ for that traversal restricted to
internal nodes: $\pi_4 = \langle 1,2,0\rangle$, i.e.\ internal node $1$
(children $3,4$) is evaluated first, then internal $2$ (children $5,6$),
then the root $0$ (children $1,2$). The canonical evaluation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rcan}
  \Rcan(N, L) \;=\; \mathrm{evaluate}\bigl(\FBT(N), L, \fadd\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the leaf-value vector indexed by the canonical leaf
enumeration of Definition~\ref{def:leafenum}
and $\fadd$ denotes the combinational semantics of our FP64 adder
(\S\ref{sec:fadd}). Equation~\eqref{eq:Rcan} is the \emph{target} of every
$\sprs{}$ execution on input $(N, L)$.

The canonical leaf enumeration $\mathrm{idx}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:leafenum} is likewise a pure function of $N$, and is the
referent for every use of ``leaf index'' below.

\emph{The canonical order is a constraint, not a tautology.} A reader may
reasonably suspect that bitwise invariance is definitional --- that any
association order would have produced one root value per $N$, so agreement
across topologies measures nothing. It is not, and we measured how much it
is not. On the leaf vectors this paper evaluates
(\S\ref{sec:method-suite}), re-associating the same $N$ addends moves the
64-bit root: right-to-left sequential summation differs from $\Rcan$ by up
to \OrderUlpMax{} ulp at $N = \OrderUlpMaxN$, and left-to-right sequential
summation differs from $\Rcan$ at \OrderLRDiff{} of the sixteen leaf counts
in the suite. The discriminating power is strongly $N$-dependent and is not
monotone in $N$. Exhaustively at $N=8$, all \OrderPermsEight{} leaf
permutations yield only \OrderRootsEight{} distinct roots, of which
\OrderCanonPctEight{} equal the canonical one, so a uniformly random
leaf-order violation is detected with probability \OrderDetectEight{}; at
$N=16$, all \OrderShapesSixteen{} in-order tree shapes yield
\OrderRootsSixteen{} distinct roots spanning two ulp. Sampling association
orders uniformly, the probability that a reassociation moves the root
ranges from \OrderProbMin{} to \OrderProbMax{} across the sixteen leaf
counts. Two consequences follow, and we adopt both. The oracle is
non-vacuous, so an agreeing run is evidence; and the rows of the invariance
table (\S\ref{sec:results-invariance}) are not equally informative --- at
$N=8$ and $N=16$ agreement is close to free, and the weight of the evidence
for Claim~\ref{claim:1} sits at the large leaf counts.

\subsection{The ABI: Operand Binding and Leaf Binding}\label{sec:abi-def}

The ABI is a contract between compiler and fabric comprising two
disciplines.

\begin{definition}[Operand binding]\label{def:opbind}
For every internal node $v$ of $\FBT(N)$, the compiler emits exactly one
\texttt{COMPUTE} instruction on the PE hosting $v$, with
\begin{align}
  \mathtt{addr\_a} &= \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{left}(v)),\\
  \mathtt{addr\_b} &= \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{right}(v)),
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{dmem}(\cdot)$ is a per-PE DMEM address assigned by the
allocator. The binding is a pure function of $v$ and depends on nothing
else.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Leaf binding]\label{def:leafbind}
For every leaf $k \in \mathrm{leaves}(\FBT(N))$, the \texttt{GENERATE}
instruction on the PE hosting $k$ writes, into the DMEM slot bound to $k$,
the 64-bit value $L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$, where $\mathrm{idx}$ is the
canonical leaf enumeration of Definition~\ref{def:leafenum}.
\end{definition}

The fabric's obligation is to execute the two disciplines symmetrically:
\texttt{COMPUTE} reads the two DMEM slots at a single clock edge,
scoreboard-gated, and writes $\fadd(\text{value}_a,
\text{value}_b)$ to the destination slot;  \texttt{GENERATE} delivers
$L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ into its destination slot with the scoreboard set.
Critically, neither discipline references $G$, topology, assignment
$\asgn$, mapping $\map$, refinement $r$, or timing. These are left free
--- and are the object of the tournament's optimisation in
\S\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:abi-assumptions}

Fifteen assumptions carry the two properties: five \emph{compiler
obligations} (\textsf{C-*}), which the compiler or the user must
discharge, and ten \emph{fabric invariants} (\textsf{F-*}), which are
established by inspection of the RTL. Eleven of the fifteen are
hypotheses of Claim~\ref{claim:1}. The four marked~$\dagger$ are not:
violating one of them can stop a run, but it cannot alter a root value
that the fabric does produce, and Claim~\ref{claim:1} is conditional on
normal termination. Those four are the hypotheses of
Property~\ref{prop:2} instead.

\subsubsection*{Compiler obligations}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\textsf{C-sw} (Single-writer allocation for remote destinations).]
  Partition each PE's DMEM into \emph{receive buffers}, the addresses named
  as \texttt{target\_addr} by some \texttt{SEND} from another PE, and
  \emph{local temporaries}, the rest. For every PE $p$ and every receive
  buffer $a \neq 0$, the emitted image contains exactly one write to
  $(p,a)$, and that write is remote. Local temporaries are exempt: the
  allocator reuses them (\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}), and their safety comes
  from in-order issue rather than from single assignment --- see
  Remark~\ref{rem:recycle}. The scoping is load-bearing and every use of
  \textsf{C-sw} below respects it; the two classes are disjoint by
  construction.
\item[\textsf{C-bind} (Operand binding, Def.~\ref{def:opbind}).] Every
  \texttt{COMPUTE} for internal node $v$ binds the operand pair by
  definition. Checked on the emitted image by the post-condition pass
  (\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).
\item[\textsf{C-leaf} (Leaf binding, Def.~\ref{def:leafbind}).] Every
  \texttt{GENERATE} for leaf $k$ delivers $L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ into the
  DMEM slot bound to $k$. Codified by the testbench emitter
  (\sv{emit\_sv\_testbench}, \sv{sprs\_core.py:5533--5549}).
\item[\textsf{C-mem} (Memory feasibility).] For every PE $p$, the emitted
  image names at most $\sv{DMEM\_DEPTH} = \DmemDepth$ distinct DMEM
  addresses on $p$; and every router id in the emitted adjacency table is
  below the disconnected sentinel of the adjacency interface's id field
  (\sv{noc\_system.sv}), which the submitted design fixed at
  \RouterIdBits{} bits, i.e.\ at most \RouterIdMax. Neither bound is
  enforced by the fabric at run time. The fabric truncates DMEM addresses to
  $\log_2 \sv{DMEM\_DEPTH}$ bits (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:82}) and
  \texttt{SEND} target addresses to \SendAddrBits{}
  (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:629}), so an image over budget aliases two
  addresses onto one slot silently; router ids at or above the sentinel
  alias onto low ids, which is what stopped one instance of the suite from
  ever settling (L0). Both halves are the compiler's to discharge, and both
  are cheap: peak occupancy is a quantity the allocator already maintains, so
  the DMEM half is an $O(N)$ read-off checked before emission
  (Remark~\ref{rem:cmem}), and the router-id half holds by construction once
  the emitter sizes the id field to the instance, with an elaboration-time
  range check in the fabric (\sv{gen\_adj\_width\_check},
  \sv{noc\_system.sv}) failing the build rather than aliasing if it does
  not.
\item[$\dagger$\,\textsf{C-route} (Routing-table soundness).] The emitted
  routing tables encode destination-correct, loop-free paths on the
  declared topology. Satisfied by construction for each topology family.
  We claim destination-correctness and loop-freedom only; the emitted
  tables are minimal-distance rather than dimension-ordered, so we do not
  claim an acyclic channel-dependency graph.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[The \textsf{C-mem} margin, and what lies past it]
\label{rem:cmem}
\textsf{C-mem} is the one hypothesis of Claim~\ref{claim:1} that the
campaign came close to violating, so we state the margin rather than the
fact of compliance. Peak DMEM occupancy over the validated configurations
reaches \DmemPeakMax{} of \DmemDepth{} slots, a margin of \DmemMargin{}.
Past the ceiling the claim fails, and it fails silently: on a constructed
$\FBT(\DmemCexLeaves)$ instance with $G=\DmemCexWorkers$ and all leaves on
one PE, peak occupancy is \DmemCexPeak{} slots, \DmemCexOver{} emitted
addresses exceed the depth, and the run terminates normally --- no
\texttt{P\_ERROR}, no deadlock --- returning a wrong root, and two
\emph{different} wrong roots under two different packet-delivery orders.
Aliasing defeats the presence gate rather than tripping it, because the
aliased slot has been written by something. The fabric cannot detect the
condition, which is why \textsf{C-mem} is a hypothesis of Claim~\ref{claim:1}
rather than a property of the machine, and why we report the margin. The
compiler can: the emitter now carries a post-condition over the instruction
words it is about to write out and raises \sv{EmitRefused} rather than emit an
image whose addresses would alias, so the failure above is a recorded compile
failure in the released tool and a wrong root only in the version we measured
it on. No configuration in the campaign trips that post-condition, which is
the same statement as the margin. A
checker (\sv{tests/checkers/\allowbreak test\_emit\_\allowbreak
refuses\_aliasing.py}) guards it, and its own negative control confirms the
refusal is driven by the addresses rather than by a small depth.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why address reuse does not break the gate]
\label{rem:recycle}
The allocator of \S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem} returns a child's slot to a
free list once the parent has consumed it, so a local temporary can be
written more than once per program. Read against a scoreboard bit that is
never cleared, that looks unsafe: a second writer would find the bit already
set, and a reader could not distinguish the new value from the old. Reuse is
also not a corner case that Claim~\ref{claim:1} could be restricted away
from --- over \RecycleConfigs{} compiled configurations we measured
\RecycleAddrs{} recycled addresses and \RecycleEvents{} reuse events, and
\emph{every} configuration recycles.

It is safe, and there are exactly two cases, distinguished by how many
readers of the old value are still outstanding when the slot is rewritten.
(a) In \RecycleZeroReaderPct{} of reuse events there are none: the last
consumer of the old value has already retired and the slot was returned to
the free list, so there is nothing to protect. (b) In the remaining
\RecycleSelfReaderPct{} there is exactly one, and it is the very
\texttt{COMPUTE} that overwrites the slot; under \textsf{F-inorder} that
instruction samples both operands in \textsf{DE} strictly before it writes
back in \textsf{EX}. Over the same configurations no reuse event has a
remaining reader other than the overwriting instruction, and none has two,
so (a) and (b) are exhaustive. The ordering is supplied by program order on
a single PE, not by the scoreboard. The scoreboard exists for the one case
program order cannot cover: a value written by a \emph{different} PE, whose
arrival time is a function of routing and contention. Those destinations are
precisely the receive buffers, and those are never recycled.

Case (b) has a sub-case that program order alone does not cover, and it is
the reason \textsf{F-gci-haz} and \textsf{F-arb} appear in
Claim~\ref{claim:1}'s hypotheses. \texttt{GENERATE} is asynchronous: the
instruction only asserts \sv{gci\_start} in \textsf{EX}, and the GCI agent
commits the leaf value $\GciLatency$ or more cycles later
(\sv{noc\_ni.sv:26}), against a compiler-emitted gap of \GciGapMin{} cycles
at minimum and \GciGapMedian{} at the median. \GenRecycledPct{} of
\texttt{GENERATE}s (\GenRecycled{} of \GenTotal{} in the instances we
instrumented) target a \emph{recycled} slot, and every one of them is
followed by a read of that slot. For those the scoreboard bit is already
$1$, set by the previous writer, so \textsf{F-sb-gate} cannot stall the
consumer and cannot be what makes them safe. The in-flight interlock
\textsf{F-gci-haz} is, and \textsf{F-arb}'s retry latch is what stops a
colliding PIU write from discarding the pending GCI write. Remove either
and the consumer reads the stale value, with no stall and no diagnostic.

The separation of the two address classes is structural rather than
incidental. The allocator assigns receive buffers in a second pass, from
fresh addresses above the local high-water mark, and never from the free
list. We verified both facts directly on the emitted images of the same
\RecycleConfigs{} configurations: the set of recycled addresses and the set
of remotely-written addresses never intersect, and no operand is ever read
after the write that recycles its slot (\RecycleUnsafe{} such events out of
\RecycleEvents{}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Fabric invariants}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\textsf{F-sb-mono} (Scoreboard monotonicity).] For every receive
  buffer $a$, $\texttt{scoreboard[a]}$ transitions $0 \to 1$ at most once
  per program, at the clock edge of the unique remote write to $a$, and
  stays set until the next program-level reset. As with \textsf{C-sw}, the
  scope is receive buffers: a local temporary's bit is set by its first
  writer and stays set across every reuse, which is why local temporaries
  are covered by \textsf{F-inorder} and \textsf{F-gci-haz} below rather
  than by the scoreboard.
\item[\textsf{F-sb-gate} (Scoreboard gating).] \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls
  \textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX} until both $\texttt{scoreboard[addr\_a]}$
  and $\texttt{scoreboard[addr\_b]}$ are $1$; \texttt{SEND} stalls until
  $\texttt{scoreboard[addr\_a]}$ is $1$
  (\sv{scoreboard\_hazard}, \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:514--521}).
\item[\textsf{F-fw} (DMEM write-to-read forwarding).] A DMEM read at cycle
  $t$ returns the value committed by the unique write to that address at
  cycle $\leq t$, even across the adjacent-cycle race. Realised by a
  two-level address-match forwarding network.
\item[\textsf{F-inorder} (In-order single-PE issue).] Each PE issues its
  program in order through \textsf{IF}$\to$\textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX},
  one instruction at a time, and an instruction samples both operands at
  the \textsf{DE} read port strictly before it writes its result back in
  \textsf{EX} (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:548--558} and \sv{:608--621}).
  This is what makes local-temporary reuse safe
  (Remark~\ref{rem:recycle}); the scoreboard cannot, because a recycled
  slot's bit is already set.
\item[\textsf{F-gci-haz} (In-flight \texttt{GENERATE} interlock).] From
  the cycle a \texttt{GENERATE} asserts \sv{gci\_start} until the GCI
  agent commits its value, \textsf{DE} stalls any \texttt{COMPUTE} or
  \texttt{SEND} naming the pending destination as an operand
  (\sv{gci\_data\_hazard}, \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:523--534}). The RTL
  comment calls this belt-and-braces with the scoreboard; it is not. It
  is the sole protection for the \GenRecycled{} \texttt{GENERATE}s that
  target a recycled slot, where the scoreboard bit is already set.
\item[\textsf{F-arb} (Write arbitration).] The DMEM write arbiter is
  strict-priority $\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} > \mathrm{FSM}$ over the
  three write agents, with a GCI retry latch that re-asserts the GCI
  write until accepted (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:364--378}, \sv{:419--421});
  no write is silently dropped. Necessary for the root value, not only
  for liveness, on the recycled-destination path
  (\S\ref{sec:write-arb}).
\item[\textsf{F-fadd} (FADD purity).] \sv{fp64\_add.sv} is combinational
  and stateless (zero \sv{always\_ff}); its output is a pure function of
  the two 64-bit inputs sampled at the EX clock edge.
\item[$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-credit} (Credit conservation).] For every link and every
  interval, $\#\mathrm{credit\_returns} \leq \#\mathrm{buffer\_reads}$
  with a bounded wait. Realised by counter-serialised credit pulses that
  eliminate a multi-VC credit-loss bug present in an earlier design.
\item[$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-misroute} (Misroute drop).] Packets arriving at a PE
  whose \sv{dest\_gpu\_id} header field does not match the local PE are
  dropped, not silently consumed. Converts a \textsf{C-route}
  violation from silent corruption into a liveness failure detected by
  \textsf{F-watchdog}.
\item[$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-watchdog} (Liveness guard).] A stall exceeding
  $\textsf{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = \WatchdogMax$ cycles or an EX stall exceeding
  $\textsf{TIMEOUT\_MAX} = 10{,}000$ cycles raises \texttt{P\_ERROR};
  invalid opcodes raise \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately.
\end{description}

\emph{What the list omits, and why.} Three conditions that a reader might
expect here are absent, and each absence is a decision we can defend.
\emph{Compilation determinism} is not a hypothesis: Claim~\ref{claim:1}
quantifies over every image the compiler can emit, and a statement true of
all of them does not require the compiler to be a pure function of its
seed. Fixed seeds and insertion-ordered dictionaries make the campaign
re-runnable (\S\ref{sec:method}); they do not carry the claim.
\emph{Partition connectedness} is not a hypothesis either, although
\DisconnCount{} of the RTL-validated configurations (\DisconnShare{})
have partitions that are not connected subtrees. Operand binding is by
node identity and every cross-PE child gets a dedicated receive slot, so
the emitted binding is $(\mathrm{left}(v), \mathrm{right}(v))$ for
\emph{any} assignment; we checked this statically on \BindComputes{}
\texttt{COMPUTE} and \BindSends{} \texttt{SEND} instructions across
\BindConfigs{} configurations and found \BindViolations{} violations
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair}). What disconnection actually costs is DMEM
occupancy, and that is what \textsf{C-mem} bounds. Finally, the
\emph{identity slot} \texttt{DMEM[0]} is reserved but unread in this
workload --- $\FBT(N)$ has no single-child internal node
(\S\ref{sec:abi-edge}) --- so no assumption about its contents is
load-bearing here.

\begin{remark}
The partition is not cosmetic. The \textsf{C-*} obligations can be checked
by a compiler-side post-condition on the emitted image, independently of
any fabric execution --- they are compile-time verifiable, and
\textsf{C-mem} joins them precisely because peak occupancy is a property
of the image rather than of a run. The \textsf{F-*} invariants are
properties of the fabric's behaviour, established once by RTL inspection
and independently of any particular compilation. Separating the two means
that a failure of Claim~\ref{claim:1} can be diagnosed precisely: either
the compiler violated an obligation (a bug in \sprs{}) or the fabric
violated an invariant (a bug in the RTL), but not both.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Claim 1 and the Fail-Stop Property}\label{sec:abi-claim}

\begin{claim}[Cross-topology bitwise invariance]\label{claim:1}
Let $N \in \mathbb{N}$, let $L \in (\mathrm{bits}_{64})^N$ be a fixed
leaf-value vector, and let $\T$ be the family of flat direct-network
topologies realisable by adjacency tables consistent with
\sv{noc\_system.sv}. Assume \textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{C-bind},
\textsf{C-leaf}, \textsf{C-mem} and \textsf{F-sb-mono}, \textsf{F-sb-gate},
\textsf{F-fw}, \textsf{F-inorder}, \textsf{F-gci-haz}, \textsf{F-arb},
\textsf{F-fadd}. Let $G$ be a worker count and $\tau \in \T$ a topology for
which \sv{build\_schedule} emits an image satisfying \textsf{C-mem}, let
$\asgn$, $\map$, $r$ be any valid assignment, mapping and refinement, and
let $s$ be the per-PE instruction schedule \sv{build\_schedule} emits for
them. If the run terminates normally --- \sv{all\_done} asserted, no
\texttt{P\_ERROR}, no watchdog --- then the observed 64-bit root, the
result of the root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE}, satisfies
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(N, L, G, \tau, \asgn, \map, r, s)
    \;=\; \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{claim}

Three features of the statement are deliberate and a reimplementer should
read them as binding. \emph{First}, \textsf{C-mem} is a compilability
precondition, not a decoration: the claim asserts nothing about a
configuration whose image exceeds the per-PE DMEM budget or whose router
count exceeds what the adjacency interface can address. One instance in our
suite is in that position, and it is also the one that never settles.
\emph{Second}, the claim is a partial-correctness statement. Liveness is
Property~\ref{prop:2}'s business, which is exactly why \textsf{C-route},
\textsf{F-credit}, \textsf{F-misroute} and \textsf{F-watchdog} are not
hypotheses here: each of them, violated, can prevent a root from being
produced but cannot change one that is. \emph{Third}, the observable is the
root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE} result, the value latched at
\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:806} and the quantity every number in this paper
reports; we do not state the claim on the root DMEM slot, which no emitted
instruction ever reads back.

Under the same assumptions a weaker safety property holds, and its scope is
exactly the set of violations that leave some operand \emph{absent}.

\begin{property}[Presence-gated fail-stop]\label{prop:2}
Suppose an assumption violation causes some \texttt{COMPUTE} to name a DMEM
address whose presence bit is never set --- because a packet was misrouted
and dropped (\textsf{F-misroute}), because a credit was lost and the value
never arrived (\textsf{F-credit}), because a routing table names an
unreachable destination (\textsf{C-route}), or because an operand was bound
to a slot that no agent writes (\textsf{C-bind}). Then no instruction
retires on the affected PE, \textsf{F-watchdog} fires after
$\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = \WatchdogMax$ consecutive non-retiring cycles, and
the PE enters \texttt{P\_ERROR} recording \sv{error\_pc} and
\sv{error\_state}; \sv{all\_done} is never asserted and no root is
produced. An invalid opcode raises \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately. The
fabric therefore never returns a root in these cases.
\end{property}

The converse bound is equally explicit, and we state it because the
property is easy to over-read. The fabric checks presence, not identity: a
\texttt{COMPUTE} whose operands are both present executes, whatever they
contain. Violations that leave every read address present --- an operand
misbound to a live slot (\textsf{C-bind}), a corrupted leaf value
(\textsf{C-leaf}), a duplicate write to a receive buffer (\textsf{C-sw}),
an image over the DMEM budget in which two addresses alias onto one written
slot (\textsf{C-mem}), or a violation of \textsf{F-sb-mono},
\textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw}, \textsf{F-inorder}, \textsf{F-gci-haz},
\textsf{F-arb} or \textsf{F-fadd} --- terminate normally with a wrong root.
The fabric does not detect these, and we do not claim that it does. They
are discharged before or outside it: \textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-sw},
\textsf{C-leaf} and \textsf{C-mem} by the compile-time post-condition on
the emitted image, and all of them by comparison of the final root against
the compiler-computed expected value (\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).

The injected-fault campaign separates along exactly this boundary, and the
separation is evidence we had not previously claimed. Of the \MutAddrSub{}
address-substitution mutants, \MutAddrStall{} repoint an operand at a DMEM
slot that no agent ever writes --- the harness draws a uniformly random
address rather than another node's address --- and those fail-stop: in
every case we re-simulated, the fabric raised \texttt{P\_ERROR} with a
recorded \sv{error\_pc} on each PE, which is Property~\ref{prop:2}'s
outcome observed under injected fault rather than argued from the RTL. The
remaining address substitutions, and all \MutLeaf{} leaf-corruption
mutants, terminate normally with a wrong root and are caught by the
expected-value comparison instead. Oracle non-vacuity therefore rests on
the mutants whose root reached that comparison --- the \MutLeaf{} leaf
corruptions plus at most \MutLiveSlotMax{} address substitutions --- and
not on all \MutTotal{}, and \textsf{C-leaf} is the clean counterexample to
any stronger reading of Property~\ref{prop:2}: all \MutLeaf{}
leaf-corruption mutants terminate normally, one of them one ulp from the
canonical root.

We label these a \emph{claim} and a \emph{property} rather than mechanised
theorems because the supporting argument proceeds by RTL inspection,
a compiler post-condition on the emitted image, end-to-end root comparison
and mutation testing, not by formal proof. We discuss a mechanisation path
in \S\ref{sec:mechanization}; readers who insist on ``theorem'' should
substitute accordingly.

\paragraph{Argument A1 (Operand binding, from \textsf{C-bind},
\textsf{C-mem}).}
\sv{build\_schedule} (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5218--5222}) sets
\sv{child\_addrs = [addr(children[0]), addr(children[1])]}, and the tree's
\sv{children(v)} method returns $[\mathrm{left}(v), \mathrm{right}(v)]$ by
construction (\sv{sprs\_core.py:404--408}). The addresses come from
\sv{addr\_map} keyed on \emph{node identity}, and the allocator gives every
cross-PE child a dedicated receive slot above the host PE's local
high-water mark, so the emitted binding is $(\mathrm{left}(v),
\mathrm{right}(v))$ for any assignment, whether or not the partition is
connected. \textsf{C-mem} is what makes that address-level statement a
value-level one: distinct addresses stay distinct only while the image fits
the DMEM depth, because the fabric truncates every DMEM address to
$\log_2\sv{DMEM\_DEPTH}$ bits. Under A3 the operand \emph{values} read at
those addresses are the final values written.

\paragraph{Argument A2 (Single-writer construction, from \textsf{C-sw},
\textsf{F-sb-mono}).}
The \sv{DMEMAllocator} (\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}) walks \sv{su\_postorder}
and assigns each tree node a DMEM slot, recycling a local slot only after
the node's unique parent has consumed it; receive buffers are allocated in
a second pass and never recycled. Because $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, each node
has exactly one parent and therefore exactly one consumer. This constructs
\textsf{C-sw} \emph{in its scoped form}: every receive buffer has exactly
one writer, and that writer is remote. Under \textsf{F-sb-mono} the
scoreboard bit of a receive buffer therefore transitions $0 \to 1$ exactly
once per program, at the clock edge of that unique write. Neither statement
is made about local temporaries, which are recycled by design; those are
case~(b) of A3.

\paragraph{Argument A3 (Operand-value soundness, from \textsf{F-sb-mono},
\textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw}, \textsf{F-inorder},
\textsf{F-gci-haz}, \textsf{F-arb}).}
\textsf{C-sw} partitions every PE's DMEM into receive buffers and local
temporaries, so every address a \texttt{COMPUTE} reads falls into exactly
one of two cases, and the two together are the whole argument.

\emph{(a) Receive buffers.} Under \textsf{F-sb-mono}, once
$\texttt{scoreboard[a]}$ is set, \texttt{DMEM[$a$]} holds the value of the
unique remote write to $a$. Under \textsf{F-sb-gate} the
\textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX} transition of a \texttt{COMPUTE} requires both
operand bits to be set, so at the EX clock edge both operand slots hold
their committed values. \textsf{F-fw} handles the BRAM read-first race:
two cascaded forwarding registers catch the back-to-back write-then-read
case and the across-one-stall case, which together exhaust the races
possible at this pipeline depth.

\emph{(b) Local temporaries.} Here the scoreboard is silent --- a recycled
slot's bit is already set --- and it does not need to speak. By
Remark~\ref{rem:recycle} a recycled slot has either no remaining reader,
in which case the old value is dead, or exactly one, the \texttt{COMPUTE}
that overwrites it, which under \textsf{F-inorder} samples its operands in
\textsf{DE} strictly before writing back in \textsf{EX}. The single writer
that program order does not order is the asynchronous \texttt{GENERATE}:
\textsf{F-gci-haz} stalls any consumer naming its in-flight destination
until the value commits, and \textsf{F-arb} keeps a colliding PIU write
from discarding it. In both cases the value a \texttt{COMPUTE} reads is
the one its emitted binding names.

\paragraph{Argument A4 (Sampled-FADD determinism, from \textsf{F-fadd}).}
At the EX-writeback clock edge, \sv{fsm\_dmem\_wr} commits
$\fadd(\text{dmem\_a}, \text{dmem\_b})$ where the inputs are the stable
values of A3. \sv{fp64\_add.sv} is combinational with zero \sv{always\_ff}
registers; its output is a pure function of its inputs.

\paragraph{Composition.} Every internal DMEM value at termination is
$\fadd$ applied to the (left, right) children --- A1 for the addresses, A3
for the values, A4 for the function --- evaluated in post-order by data
dependence, with initial values supplied by the \textsf{C-leaf}-enforced
\texttt{GENERATE} writes and address distinctness supplied by
\textsf{C-mem}. The root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE} therefore returns
$\mathrm{evaluate}(\FBT(N), L, \fadd) = \Rcan(N, L)$, a pure function of
$(N, L)$. Each of Claim~\ref{claim:1}'s eleven hypotheses is used at least
once above: \textsf{C-bind} and \textsf{C-mem} in A1, \textsf{C-sw} and
\textsf{F-sb-mono} in A2, \textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw},
\textsf{F-inorder}, \textsf{F-gci-haz} and \textsf{F-arb} in A3,
\textsf{F-fadd} in A4, and \textsf{C-leaf} here.

\begin{remark}[On Property~\ref{prop:2}]
The fail-stop argument is more direct, and it is bounded. A
\textsf{C-route} violation routes a packet to a PE that is not its
destination, where \textsf{F-misroute} drops it; the intended
destination's presence bit is never set, its dependent \texttt{COMPUTE}
stalls, and \textsf{F-watchdog} raises \texttt{P\_ERROR} after
$\WatchdogMax$ cycles. A \textsf{F-credit} violation stalls a link or
overfills a receive FIFO, whose write is ignored when full; either way the
value never arrives and the same chain fires. A \textsf{C-bind} violation
that names a slot no agent writes is the same case once more, and it is
the case we have injected. The bound is that this is \emph{all}: a
violation leaving every read address present --- a \textsf{C-sw}
violation, for instance, whose duplicate write finds the bit already set
and is a no-op to the gate --- produces a wrong root with no
fabric-visible symptom, and is caught, if at all, by the compile-time
post-condition or by the root comparison.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Mapping/Topology Orthogonality Theorem}\label{sec:abi-orth}

Claim~\ref{claim:1} asserts invariance over $(G,\text{topology}, \asgn,
\map, r, s)$. The $(G, \asgn)$ slice is where the substantive work lives:
changing $G$ changes the number of PEs, and changing $\asgn$ changes
which PE hosts which tree node. The $(\text{topology}, \map, r, s)$
slice is strictly less interesting --- once the assignment is fixed, the
topology, the worker-to-physical mapping, and the routing and timing
only change \emph{where} and \emph{when} data flows, not the operand
binding at any COMPUTE. We make this formal.

\begin{proposition}[Mapping/topology orthogonality]\label{thm:orth}
Fix $N$, $L$, assignment $\asgn$, refinement $r$, and the fabric's FADD
unit. For any two valid mappings $\map_1, \map_2$ and any two valid
routing tables $\rho_1, \rho_2$ for (possibly different) topologies in
$\T$, the observed root result satisfies
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_1, \rho_1) \;=\; R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_2, \rho_2)
  \;=\; \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{structuralargument}
By inspection of \sv{build\_schedule} and \sv{generate\_instructions}
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-codegen}): the \texttt{COMPUTE} instructions'
$(\texttt{addr\_a}, \texttt{addr\_b}, \texttt{dest})$ triples depend only
on $\asgn$ and the DMEM allocator's output, neither of which reads
$\map$ or the routing tables. The \texttt{SEND} instructions'
$(\texttt{dest\_gpu}, \texttt{target\_addr}, \texttt{link})$ triples
depend on $\map$ (through the \sv{mapping.get(gpu, gpu)} indirection)
and on the routing tables --- but these only affect \emph{where} data
flows, not the $(\texttt{addr\_a}, \texttt{addr\_b})$ operand bindings at
the receiving PE's \texttt{COMPUTE}. Under \textsf{F-sb-gate}, the
receiving PE's \texttt{COMPUTE} reads the DMEM values by address, not by
arrival time, arrival port, or arrival VC. Therefore $R_\mathrm{observed}$
is independent of $(\map, \rho)$.
\end{structuralargument}

Proposition~\ref{thm:orth} is the cleanest statement of what the ABI buys.
The topology and the physical mapping --- the two things that change
most obviously under cluster reshape --- are structurally orthogonal to
the bit result. This is not a consequence to be validated empirically;
it is a consequence of the code path that emits the instructions.

\subsection{FADD Policy}\label{sec:fadd}

Our FADD (\sv{fp64\_add.sv}, with a bit-exact Python mirror at
\sv{sprs\_core.py::\_fp64\_add\_bits}) implements a \emph{deterministic
subset} of IEEE~754-2019 binary64 addition. We specify the subset fully:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Input FTZ.} Subnormal inputs (exp $= 0$, frac $\neq 0$) are
      flushed to $\pm 0$ preserving sign.
\item \emph{RNE only.} The round-to-nearest-even predicate is
      $\mathrm{guard} \wedge (\mathrm{sticky} \vee \mathrm{lsb})$.
      No other rounding mode is available; there is no latched
      rounding-mode register.
\item \emph{Output FTZ.} Post-round subnormals are flushed to $\pm 0$
      preserving sign.
\item \emph{Signed-zero cancellation.} The result of $x + (-x) = +0$ for
      all non-NaN $x$; $(-0) + (-0) = +0$. The latter deviates from
      IEEE~754-2019 \S6.3, which mandates $-0$; we document the deviation
      and note it is deterministic and inherits Claim~\ref{claim:1}.
\item \emph{NaN propagation.} Output is a quieted qNaN whose sign equals
      $\mathrm{sign}(a)$, exponent is \sv{0x7FF}, bit $51$ is forced to
      $1$ (the quiet bit), and bits $[50{:}0]$ copy the payload of $a$ if
      $a$ is NaN, else of $b$. Narrower than IEEE~754 (which allows any
      qNaN payload) but deterministic.
\item \emph{Infinity arithmetic.} $\pm\infty + \pm\infty$ (same sign) $= \pm\infty$;
      $\pm\infty + \mp\infty$ returns the canonical qNaN
      \sv{0x7FF8\_0000\_0000\_0000};
      $\pm\infty + \text{finite} = \pm\infty$.
\end{enumerate}

We refer to (1)--(6) collectively as \emph{FTZ+RNE semantics}. Invariance
(Claim~\ref{claim:1}) depends only on $\fadd$ being a pure function of
its inputs; it does not depend on any particular IEEE~754 feature.

\emph{Compatibility with reference implementations.} FTZ+RNE diverges
from strict IEEE~754 on three regimes: subnormal inputs, post-round
subnormal outputs, and signed-zero cancellation of two negative zeros.
Workloads whose reference implementation (NumPy, MATLAB, a reference
BLAS) produces or depends on subnormals will see \sprs{} diverge from
reference at the affected bits. This is a scope decision, not an
oversight: a subnormal-aware alternative \sv{alu\_op} slot is
implementable as a one-instruction ISA extension that inherits
Claim~\ref{claim:1} by construction. We flag the extension for
deployments requiring reference-library bit-parity.

\emph{The subset, checked rather than asserted.} Items (1)--(6) are a
specification, and the adder we first submitted did not meet item~(2): on
effective subtraction it was not round-to-nearest-even. We found that by
scoring the adder against references external to the design --- exact
rationals, and the host FPU --- rather than against its own Python mirror,
repaired the hardware and the mirror together, and re-verified.
\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim} gives the defect, the repair and the evidence; L13
gives the two independent reasons why neither the campaign nor the mirror
could have found it. What items (1)--(6) state is therefore what the shipped
adder now measures: \FpSweepErrors{} disagreements with correctly-rounded
IEEE~754 addition over \FpSweepVectors{} vectors inside the documented scope
(normal operands, normal non-zero result).

\subsection{Edge Cases}\label{sec:abi-edge}

A few edge cases deserve explicit treatment.

\emph{$N = 1$.} $\FBT(1)$ is a singleton (one leaf, zero internal nodes).
The ``result'' is $L[0]$; the compiler emits one \texttt{GENERATE} and no
\texttt{COMPUTE}. The argument is vacuously true.

\emph{$N = 0$.} Disallowed at the compiler front end.

\emph{Single-child edge.} By construction, $\FBT(N)$ with $2N-1$ nodes
has no single-child internal nodes. The identity slot \texttt{DMEM[0]}
is reserved but unused in the present workload; it exists so that
future relaxations (asymmetric trees, variable-arity reductions) can
use an identity operand without changing the ISA.

\emph{Integer and bitwise reductions.} Claim~\ref{claim:1} requires only
a deterministic binary operation; it does not require FADD specifically.
Integer ADD, MAX, MIN, bitwise operators inherit the claim by extension
via a different \sv{alu\_op} code. This is a structural extension, not an
empirical generalisation.

\subsection{Mechanisation and Runtime Checks}\label{sec:mechanization}

Our current strategy discharges A1--A4 through a combination of
\emph{compile-time} post-condition assertions, \emph{simulation-time}
SystemVerilog assertions, and \emph{empirical} mutation testing.

\paragraph{Compile-time checks.} \textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-sw},
\textsf{C-leaf} and \textsf{C-mem} are decidable on the emitted IMEM without
executing anything:
\begin{itemize}
\item For every internal $v$ and its emitted \texttt{COMPUTE},
  $\texttt{addr\_a} = \texttt{addr\_map}[\text{pe}(v)][\mathrm{left}(v)]$
  and $\texttt{addr\_b} = \texttt{addr\_map}[\text{pe}(v)][\mathrm{right}(v)]$.
\item For every PE $p$, $|\{(a, \texttt{dest}) : \texttt{dest} = a\}| =
  |\texttt{addr\_map}[p]|$ (single-writer).
\item For every leaf $k$, the \texttt{GENERATE} writes
  $\texttt{gci\_buffer}[\text{local\_idx}(k)] = \mathrm{make\_leaf\_val}(\mathrm{idx}(k))$.
\item For every PE $p$, $\max_a \texttt{addr\_map}[p][a] < \DmemDepth$
  (\textsf{C-mem}).
\end{itemize}
\vspace{-\topsep}
The campaign path did not run these checks inside the compile loop; we ran
them afterwards over every released configuration, and
\S\ref{sec:compiler-codegen} reports the counts. \textsf{C-mem} is the
exception in the released compiler, where it is a precondition of emission
rather than an audit of the output (Remark~\ref{rem:cmem}). \textsf{C-route} has no
companion check beyond destination-correctness and loop-freedom by
construction, and \S\ref{sec:noc-route} states what that does and does not
establish.

\paragraph{Simulation-time checks.} Each emitted testbench carries the
canonical root as a compile-time \sv{EXPECTED} constant and compares the
fabric's \sv{all\_done} result against it
(\sv{sprs\_core.py:5482,\,5573}), which is the \sv{PASS}/\sv{FAIL} verdict
every number in \S\ref{sec:results} is built on. We ship no SystemVerilog
assertion suite, and no claim here rests on one.

\paragraph{Mutation testing.} To establish that the oracle harness is
non-vacuous (i.e., would detect a real divergence were one to occur), we
inject two mutation classes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Address substitution}: in one randomly chosen
  \texttt{COMPUTE}, replace \texttt{addr\_b} with the DMEM address of a
  different tree node. Breaks Argument A1 while preserving A2--A4.
\item \emph{Leaf corruption}: in one randomly chosen \texttt{GENERATE},
  flip one mantissa bit.
\end{itemize}
We \emph{do not} use operand-swap as a mutation: FADD is commutative at
the bit level under FTZ+RNE ($\fadd(a,b) = \fadd(b,a)$ for all $(a,b)$),
so operand-swap is bit-identical and would inflate the false null-detection
rate spuriously. Mutation detection rates are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:invariance} (\S\ref{sec:results-invariance}).

\paragraph{Path to mechanisation.} A bounded Alloy~\cite{jackson2012alloy}
model of the scheduler plus scoreboard (scope $N=16, G=4$,
${\sim}250$ lines) would mechanise Arguments A1--A3 at finite scope. A
HardCaml or Kami~\cite{choi2017kami} embedding of
\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv} and \sv{fp64\_add.sv} would mechanise A4. We
report these as in-progress supplementary artefacts and do not claim
them here.


% =====================================================================
\section{The Fabric: NoC, ISA, Pipeline}\label{sec:fabric}
% =====================================================================

This section describes the fabric that realises the \textsf{F-*}
invariants. The guiding principle is that topology is a runtime
configuration of adjacency and routing tables (\S\ref{sec:fabric-topo}),
the ISA is small enough to audit for ABI adherence
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-isa}), the scoreboard is the single point of
synchronisation across the three agents that write DMEM
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-pipe}), and every assumption violation terminates
the program at a diagnostic state rather than producing silent corruption
(\S\ref{sec:fail-stop}).

\subsection{Design Goals}

The fabric supports a cluster of up to $4096$ PEs in a single Verilog
instantiation whose topology is determined by the adjacency and routing
tables loaded at boot. The binding ceiling is the width of the
configuration interface, not a parameter: adjacency writes carry a
\RouterIdBits-bit router id whose top code is the disconnected sentinel
(\sv{noc\_system.sv:67--70}), so router ids are addressable up to
\RouterIdMax{} and a configuration above that aliases silently
(\S\ref{sec:resource}). \sv{MAX\_ROUTERS} in \sv{noc\_pkg.sv} is declared
once and referenced nowhere, as is \sv{MAX\_PORTS}. Router radix is likewise
not fixed by the design:
it is the per-instance parameter \sv{PORTS\_PER\_RTR}, which the compiler
emits and which ranges $\RadixMin$--$\RadixMax$ across our suite
(\S\ref{sec:resource}). The reduction ISA has four live opcodes, one
reserved for future barrier/reset semantics. All determinism-critical
mechanisms --- scoreboard, forwarding network, DMEM write arbiter, credit
accounting --- are simple enough to fit in a two-page RTL inspection.

\subsection{Topology as a Runtime Parameter}\label{sec:fabric-topo}

Two per-router configuration tables parameterise the topology.

The \emph{adjacency table} (\sv{adj\_entry\_t} in \sv{noc\_pkg.sv}) maps
$(\text{router}, \text{port}) \to (\text{target\_router},
\text{target\_port}) \mid \sv{ADJ\_DISCONNECTED}$, set by the compiler via
a write-enable interface before \texttt{program\_start}. The
\emph{routing table} (\sv{route\_table} in \sv{noc\_router.sv}) maps
$(\text{router}, \text{destination}) \to \text{output\_port}$, also
compiler-loaded. A single \sv{noc\_system} module with \sv{N\_NODES} and
\sv{N\_ROUTERS} parameters supports the full family $\T$. Port~0 is
reserved for the local network interface on every router --- the one
structural convention independent of topology.

The consequence is that \emph{topology is data, not RTL}. Adding a new
topology requires implementing a Python classmethod that emits adjacency
and routing tables; no Verilog change is needed. The compiler at
\sv{sprs\_core.py:487--695} provides six topology families (linear, ring,
2D mesh, 2D torus, fat-tree, hypercube) with analytical
shortest-path computations at $O(1)$ per pair; a BFS fallback handles
custom topologies at $O(G)$ per source.

packet: a 16-bit \texttt{dest\_node\_id}, a 10-bit \texttt{target\_addr},
five reserved bits, the VC-steering bit $[64]$, and the 64-bit payload
(Fig.~\ref{fig:packet}).

(the forwarding network in \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv}), realising \textsf{F-fw}. Level~1
forwards a cycle-$N$ write to a cycle-$(N{+}1)$ read of the same address
--- the back-to-back-COMPUTE case. Level~2 forwards across a single
intervening stall, catching the case where the one-cycle forward expired
before the read settled. The two levels together exhaust the possible
races given the pipeline depth and the scoreboard's one-cycle gate.

The scoreboard is a \sv{DMEM\_DEPTH}-wide bit vector set whenever \emph{any}
of three write ports commits: PIU (an arriving RDMA-push packet), GCI (a
completed leaf fetch, which is also how a \texttt{GENERATE} commits), or the
FSM \texttt{COMPUTE} writeback (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:365--382, 706--721}).
\textsf{F-sb-mono} guarantees each bit transitions $0 \to 1$ at
most once per program; the scoreboard is the single point where
``data present at address $a$'' is decided, independently of which port
delivered the data. This is what makes the scoreboard-gated
reads structurally race-free (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{Fail-Stop Discipline}\label{sec:fail-stop}

The fabric implements a three-way safety net that makes Property~\ref{prop:2}
provable rather than asserted.

\emph{PIU autonomous write and misroute drop.} The PIU
(the PIU block in \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv}) drains RX FIFOs on a priority basis.
On each drained packet it examines bits $[95{:}80]$ (\sv{dest\_gpu\_id}).
If equal to the local PE's id, it writes the payload to
$\texttt{DMEM[pkt[79:70]]}$ (\sv{target\_addr} from the header) and sets
the scoreboard bit. If not equal --- a routing-table bug, a corrupted
packet, a torn adjacency --- it drops the packet, returns credit, and
asserts \texttt{piu\_misroute} for one cycle. This realises
\textsf{F-misroute}. The drop and the credit return are real hardware; the
flag itself is an internal signal with no reader and is not a module port,
so a misroute is observable only through the liveness failure it causes.

The consequence for determinism is stronger than a silent-failure
assumption. A bad routing table causes the destination's scoreboard bit
never to be set; the destination's dependent \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls
forever; \textsf{F-watchdog} (below) fires and raises \texttt{P\_ERROR}.
A routing-table violation cannot produce a silently-wrong root result ---
the fabric converts the violation into a liveness failure detected and
localised by the watchdog.

\emph{Watchdog + stall timeout + invalid-opcode trap.} The fabric
maintains three error detectors in the \texttt{P\_RUN} state
(the stall logic in \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv}):

\begin{itemize}
\item Watchdog: fires after $\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = \WatchdogMax$ cycles with
  no instruction retired.
\item Stall timer: fires after $\sv{TIMEOUT\_MAX} = 10{,}000$ cycles of
  continuous EX stall.
\item Invalid-opcode trap: any opcode $> \sv{4'h3}$ (other than the
  reserved slot) triggers \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately.
\end{itemize}
Each raises \texttt{error\_pc} and \texttt{error\_state} for post-mortem
diagnosis. The termination outcomes are therefore (i) correct result,
(ii) \texttt{P\_ERROR} with diagnostic context, or (iii) watchdog timeout
with the same diagnostic; no configuration in the campaign produced a
fourth. The fabric does not guarantee that a fourth is impossible ---
Remark~\ref{rem:cmem} gives the one construction, an image over the
\textsf{C-mem} budget, that defeats the presence gate instead of tripping
it.

\subsection{Write Arbitration and Retry Latches}\label{sec:write-arb}

The three DMEM write ports demand the same resource; their arbitration
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}, bottom) realises \textsf{F-arb}.

The arbiter is strict-priority $\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} >
\mathrm{FSM}$ (the DMEM write arbiter in \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv}). PIU always wins ---
maintaining the RDMA-push semantic that incoming data is never
blocked. The GCI write carries a retry latch
(\sv{gci\_write\_held} in \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv}):
if GCI completion (\texttt{gci\_done}) collides with a PIU write in the
same cycle, the arbiter drops the GCI write but the retry latch captures
the pending value and re-asserts \texttt{gci\_dmem\_wr} until the arbiter
accepts it. The FSM COMPUTE writeback loses to both agents and is
stalled via \texttt{ex\_dmem\_collision} when necessary.

This matters for determinism in a subtle way. Without the GCI retry
latch, a PIU/GCI collision would silently drop the leaf value; the
destination's scoreboard would never be set by GCI (PIU wins so the bit
is set, but to the wrong value); the next COMPUTE would read a wrong leaf
and produce a wrong root --- violating A1 (sampled-read determinism)
despite all other invariants holding. The retry latch is a narrow
safeguard against a specific timing race; we name it explicitly because
it is necessary-for-correctness, not merely nice-to-have.

\subsection{Flow Control, Routing, and Deadlock}\label{sec:noc-route}

Each input port carries $\sv{NUM\_VCS} = \NumVcs{}$ virtual channels
(\sv{noc\_pkg.sv}), and every emitted testbench sizes router FIFOs at
\BufDepthSim{} flits, overriding the RTL default of \BufDepthRtl{}
(\sv{.RTR\_BUF\_DEPTH(\BufDepthSim)}, \sv{sprs\_core.py:5430}). Per-link
credit-based flow control prevents ingress overflow: \sv{link\_tx}
decrements a per-link credit counter on each transmission and increments it
on each credit-return pulse, with counter conservation $\sum \text{pulses} =
\sum \text{reads}$ (\textsf{F-credit}). Credit return is serialised through a
pending-credit register that accumulates the reads of a cycle and drains one
pulse per cycle (\sv{noc\_router.sv:192,210}); it is \CreditCtrBits{} bits
wide and wraps if a backlog of \CreditCtrWrap{} ever accrues, which requires
two virtual channels to be read in the same cycle. In this campaign it never
does, for the reason the next paragraph gives, and its value is provably
zero in every reported cycle.

\emph{One channel, and no escape.} The router reads bit $[64]$ as a VC
selector, but nothing writes it: the field is a constant in the only
packet-forming assignment in the design, the ISA has no VC field, and the
compiler contains no virtual-channel logic. Every packet in every reported
run travels on \sv{VC0}. Nor could the second channel serve as an escape
channel if the compiler did assign one: the router's egress buffer is a
single FIFO per port shared across VCs (\sv{noc\_router.sv:142--165}), so the
two channels are coupled at the output whatever the header says.

\emph{What discharges \textsf{C-route}, and what does not.} The compiler
computes routing tables by greedy minimal-distance search over the adjacency
list (\sv{sprs\_core.py:667--686}); they are not dimension-ordered. What that
construction gives is what \textsf{C-route} claims
(\S\ref{sec:abi-assumptions}): every emitted path terminates at the
destination named in the packet header and visits no router twice. It does
not give an acyclic channel-dependency graph. Built directly from the
emitted tables, the CDG contains cycles on the ring instances (cycle length
$G$, the textbook bidirectional-ring cycle) and on the torus instances
(cycle length \CdgTorusCycLo--\CdgTorusCycHi); it is acyclic on linear,
mesh, hypercube and fat-tree at every suite size. We therefore claim no
deadlock-freedom property for cyclic topologies. Deadlock is possible in
principle, and the fabric converts it into a fail-stop rather than a wrong
answer: a deadlocked PE retires nothing, so \textsf{F-watchdog} fires and
Property~\ref{prop:2} applies --- \texttt{P\_ERROR} with \texttt{error\_pc}
and \texttt{error\_state}, and no root. The campaign's non-settling runs are
accounted for in \S\ref{sec:results-invariance}; none of them, and none of
the settled runs, produced a finite root differing from the canonical one. A compiler-side
dimension-ordered pass with a VC-escape assignment, and a per-VC egress
buffer to make that escape channel real, are the two changes a production
deployment would need; neither can change a bit result, only whether the
run terminates.

A second silent-drop channel has the same shape. The emitted testbenches
give the compute node's link to the network interface \NiCredits{} credits
against an \NiFifoDepth-entry injection FIFO
(\sv{.NI\_BUF\_DEPTH(\NiFifoDepth)}), so a sufficiently long injection burst
would overflow the FIFO and the packet would be discarded without a flag.
The scoreboard bounds the damage exactly as above: a lost \texttt{SEND}
leaves the destination's presence bit clear, the dependent
\texttt{COMPUTE} stalls, and the watchdog fires. A drop can only cost
termination, never correctness, which is why no passing run can contain one.

Round-robin allocation at each output port advances only on a successful
grant (\sv{noc\_router.sv}): $\texttt{rr\_ptr}[i]$ increments iff
$\texttt{obuf\_wen}[i]$ this cycle. Without this guard, RR advance on empty
cycles would allow unfairness; with it, every contending $(port, vc)$
candidate is served within \texttt{NUM\_CANDIDATES} cycles of becoming
ready.

\subsection{Known Limitations}\label{sec:fabric-lim}

To set the scope of the present implementation honestly:

\emph{Head-of-line blocking.} The router is store-and-forward with a
single FIFO per VC; under the tree-reduction traffic pattern,
root-incident links queue up to $\lceil \log_2 G\rceil$ merge waves.
% The clause deleted here ("the ratio against the non-deterministic baseline is
% more stable because both schedules share the bottleneck") was an empirical
% claim about a baseline this paper states twice it never built (sec:method-nd,
% sec:limitations L1b); no ND measurement exists in the artifact -- the only ND
% generator, scripts/gen_ndmodel.py.notused, is disabled by file extension.
At $G \geq 1024$ on ring and torus, absolute cycle counts slew because of
this. Wormhole forwarding is a planned extension.

\emph{Single clock domain.} No clock-domain crossings. Multi-chiplet
deployment at $G = 4096$ would require asynchronous FIFOs on inter-chiplet
links, changing credit round-trip latencies but not the bit result.

\emph{Validated at one point of a parameterised design.} The fabric
instantiates two virtual channels, two compute-node links, and $k/2$ core
switches per fat-tree pod; the compiler uses one of each. The unused halves
are where the defects are. The credit backlog register of
\S\ref{sec:noc-route} can only overflow on a two-VC read; the network
interface's injection race can only fire on the second link; and for
$k \geq 4$ the fat-tree emitter assigns pod-to-core ports above the declared
radix, which the fabric discards, so the elaborated network uses a single
core switch. Every emitted route is valid on that network and the surplus
cores carry no traffic, but the analytic lower bound $\Omega$
(\S\ref{sec:method-lb}) min-cuts the intended edge set, so the fat-tree
$\Omega$ ratios we report are computed against a more capable network than
the one simulated --- an understatement, not a flattery. This is the
expected consequence of validating a parameterised design at a single point
in its parameter space, and we state it as a general caveat on the RTL
rather than as four separate errata.

\emph{Effective subtraction in \sv{fp64\_add}: a defect, repaired.} The adder
we first submitted did not implement round-to-nearest-even on effective
subtraction. When the operand signs differ the aligned mantissas are
subtracted, and the bits shifted out during alignment form a tail strictly
between zero and one guard unit; the design recorded that tail in the sticky
bit but never borrowed it, so the raw difference was too large by exactly the
tail and the \emph{addition} rounding rule $\mathrm{guard} \wedge
(\mathrm{sticky} \vee \mathrm{lsb})$ was then applied to it, rounding up a
value lying below the midpoint. The error is always \FpCtrlUlp{}~ulp. Scored
against the host's correctly-rounded addition it fires on
\FpBandLo--\FpBandHi{} of opposite-signed pairs whose exponents differ by
\FpBandDeLo{} to \FpBandDeHi{} binades, and on \emph{every} such pair at a
difference of exactly \FpFullDe{}.

The repair takes three coordinated edits, and that is the part worth
reporting. They are: split the alignment tail into its most significant bit
and the rest; borrow the tail from the difference; and rescale the borrowed
remainder across the one-bit renormalising left shift that follows a
cancellation, which can carry it past a whole guard unit. Stopping after the
borrow --- the obvious one-line fix --- leaves a $-1$~ulp residual on
\FpPartialPct{} of effective subtractions (\FpPartialErrors{} of
\FpPartialVectors{} in a three-variant sweep), concentrated at exponent
separations of \FpPartialDeLo{}--\FpPartialDeHi{} binades. A partial repair
here looks plausible and is wrong, which is why what establishes correctness
is the verification rather than the patch.

On a two-directional binade sweep plus a uniform random sample the repaired
adder agrees with correctly-rounded IEEE~754 addition on all
\FpAcceptVectors{} vectors (\FpAcceptErrors{} errors), and the repaired
software oracle agrees with it bit for bit on the same set
(\FpAcceptMismatch{} mismatches); over the wider host-FPU sweep the figure is
\FpSweepErrors{} of \FpSweepVectors{}. The negative control fires: the
as-submitted pair fails the same test on \FpCtrlErrors{} of \FpCtrlVectors{}
vectors, every failure \FpCtrlUlp{}~ulp on the effective-subtraction path.
That control also makes the second half of the finding measurable.
\sv{\_fp64\_add\_bits} is a line-for-line transcription of \sv{fp64\_add.sv}
rather than an independent model, so it carried the identical defect and was
repaired in lockstep --- and on the as-submitted pair the two agree bit for
bit on all \FpCtrlVectors{} vectors while both are wrong on \FpCtrlErrors{} of
them. A differential check between an implementation and its own transcription
passes exactly where it is least informative; L13 draws the lesson, and this
is its measured instance.

No number in this paper moves, and we checked that rather than argued it. Leaf
values come from~\eqref{eq:leaves}, which is strictly positive at every index,
so no FADD in any reported run has operands of differing sign: replaying the
emitted reduction order under the as-submitted and the repaired oracle over
\FpScopeGvals{} worker counts from \FpScopeGLo{} to \FpScopeGHi{} moves
\FpScopeMoved{} roots and encounters \FpScopeMixed{} mixed-sign additions,
while the guard's own injected-adder control detects \FpScopeMutant{}.

\emph{Soft-enforced single-writer.} \textsf{C-sw} is discharged by a
compile-time post-condition over the emitted IMEM, not by hardware
refusal of a second write to an already-set scoreboard slot. A bug that
silently corrupted the allocator would be caught by the static audit of
\S\ref{sec:compiler-codegen} but not by the fabric. A one-line RTL extension --- ``raise
\texttt{P\_ERROR} on a scoreboard-set transition from $1$ to $1$'' ---
would harden this into a fabric-side guarantee; we plan it for the next
revision.

\section{The SPRS Compiler}\label{sec:compiler}
% =====================================================================

This section describes how the compiler discharges the \textsf{C-*}
obligations, and --- because a reviewer of an earlier draft was right to ask
--- which parts of it actually produced the results in this paper. One pass,
SU-ordered DMEM allocation (\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}), does the
load-bearing work: it \emph{constructs} single-writer allocation and operand
binding rather than assuming them. The remaining stages are standard
heuristic ensembles whose algorithmic choices matter for cycle counts but
not for the bit result.

\subsection{Compilation Pipeline}\label{sec:compiler-pipeline}

\sprs{} compiles a triple $(N, G, \text{topology})$ into a per-PE IMEM
image plus per-router adjacency and routing tables. Every image reported in
this paper was produced by \StagesRun{} stages:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{S\arabic*},leftmargin=2.5em]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item Composite lower bound $\Omega$ (\sv{compute\_lower\_bound}) ---
  computed once per instance and cached; a reported metric
  (\S\ref{sec:method-lb}) rather than a step of image construction.
\item $\FBT(N)$ instantiation (\sv{CBTree.\_\_init\_\_}).
\item Assignment $\asgn:$ tree nodes $\to$ GPUs, selected from 18
  algorithms (Appendix~\ref{app:algos}, Tab.~\ref{tab:catalogue}).
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item Mapping $\map:$ GPUs $\to$ physical PEs, from 10 algorithms.
  \emph{Inert on the emitted image}: \texttt{SEND} carries the logical GPU
  id and IMEM is loaded by logical index, so $\map$ reaches the image only
  through the hop counts the cost model uses to place NOPs. Recompiling
  \MapPoints{} representative points under all ten mappings yields
  \MapImagesLo{} to \MapImagesHi{} distinct images, differing only in NOP
  padding.
\setcounter{enumi}{5}
\item ISA emission with cycle-accurate NOP insertion and GCI-gap peephole
  compression, then testbench emission (\sv{build\_schedule},
  \sv{generate\_instructions}, \sv{emit\_sv\_testbench}).
\end{enumerate}

Three further passes exist in the released compiler and produced none of the
results reported here. Connectivity repair (\sv{repair\_connectivity}, S2.5)
and memory enforcement (\sv{enforce\_memory}, S5) are called only from
\sv{sprs\_core.compile()}, which the campaign path does not use; refinement
(S4: 12 base methods chainable to 122 configurations) was not exercised in
the hardware campaign, which fixes refinement at the identity and enumerates
the $(\asgn,\map)$ face exhaustively instead (\S\ref{sec:method-tour}).
\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair} reports what running S2.5 would have cost, and
why we report the unrepaired pipeline.

All three algorithmic search stages preserve the ABI by Argument~A1
(\S\ref{sec:abi-args}) --- including the refinement stage the campaign did
not run, which permutes placement and schedule order but never an operand
pair. Every valid triple $(\asgn, \map, r)$ yields an ABI-compliant IMEM, so
every configuration in the ${\sim}22{,}000$-point space produces the same
64-bit root result by Proposition~\ref{thm:orth}. That is a structural
consequence; \S\ref{sec:results-invariance} verifies \WallTBs{} images of it
directly.

\subsection{Disconnected Partitions and Memory Pressure}\label{sec:compiler-repair}

Twelve of the eighteen assignment algorithms --- HEFT, DSC, DFSseed,
Chr\'etienne, CPOP, CPFD, ClHEFT, MLfm at small $G$, and ALNS-refined
variants of each --- can hand a PE a set of tree nodes that is not a
connected subtree of $\FBT(N)$, and \DisconnCount{} of the \WallTBs{}
RTL-validated configurations (\DisconnShare{}) are of that kind. Operand
binding is unaffected, for the reason given in \S\ref{sec:abi-assumptions}:
\sv{build\_schedule} binds children by node identity and the allocator gives
every cross-PE child a dedicated receive slot above the local high-water
mark, so the emitted binding is $(\mathrm{left}(v), \mathrm{right}(v))$ for
\emph{any} assignment. That is why the invariance result quantifies over the
raw output of all eighteen algorithms rather than over a repaired subset.

What disconnection costs is DMEM. A node whose parent is remote never
returns its slot to the free list, so live values accumulate: over
\BindConfigs{} compiled configurations, peak per-PE occupancy has median
\DmemMedConn{} slots (max \DmemMaxConn) on the \DmemConnConfigs{} connected
partitions and median \DmemMedDisc{} (max \DmemPeakMax) on the
\DmemDiscConfigs{} disconnected ones, against the \DmemDepth-entry budget.
The margin at the tail is \DmemMargin{} slots, and past the ceiling the
failure is silent (Remark~\ref{rem:cmem}) --- which is why the allocator's
$O(N)$ peak-occupancy count is the check that matters, and why
\textsf{C-mem} rather than connectedness is the hypothesis of
Claim~\ref{claim:1}.

The repair pass is therefore available but not used. \sv{repair\_connectivity}
reassigns orphaned nodes to their parent's PE, iterating to a fixed point;
run on the \DmemDiscConfigs{} disconnected campaign assignments it converges
within its \RepairPasses-pass cap every time, and it does remove the
occupancy pressure. It also destroys the load balance the assignment search
exists to produce: it moves a median \RepairMovedMed{} of all tree nodes
(max \RepairMovedMax), inflates the peak per-PE node count by a median
factor of \RepairLoadMed{} and by up to \RepairLoadMax$\times$, and leaves
\RepairImemOver{} of the \DmemDiscConfigs{} with more than \DmemDepth{}
instructions on a single PE against exactly one before repair. It is not
even monotone in the quantity it is invoked for: in \RepairDmemUp{} of the
\DmemDiscConfigs{} it \emph{raises} peak DMEM. Connectedness is a correlate
of memory pressure, not a guarantee of it, so we report the unrepaired
pipeline and check the bound directly.

\subsection{DMEM Allocation as Single-Writer Constructor}\label{sec:compiler-dmem}

The \sv{DMEMAllocator} class (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5087--5132}) walks
\sv{su\_postorder} (Sethi-Ullman-refined post-order, sibling-reordered for
register pressure). It reserves address $0$ on every PE as the identity
slot; at each node $v$ on PE $p = \asgn(v)$ it returns to $p$'s free list
the slot of every child of $v$ already resident on $p$, then gives $v$ the
head of that free list if it is non-empty and otherwise the next fresh
address, advancing $\texttt{peak\_local}[p]$. A final pass gives every
cross-PE edge a persistent receive-buffer slot on the parent's PE, above
that PE's local high-water mark, which is never freed. (Pseudocode is in
the companion.)

Three properties of that walk give \textsf{C-sw} in the scoped form
Argument~A2 uses (\S\ref{sec:abi-args}): $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, so every node
has exactly one consumer; SU-postorder visits children before the parent, so
a local slot is freed only after its consumer has read it; and receive slots
are allocated in a separate pass and never freed, because the
scoreboard-gated \texttt{COMPUTE} needs the address to be stable. Single
writing is therefore \emph{constructed} rather than assumed --- an allocator
working in pre-order, or freeing slots before the consumer ran, would break
it. The same walk yields \textsf{C-mem} at no extra cost: $\texttt{peak\_local}[p]$ plus
the receive slots is exactly the number of distinct addresses the image will
name on $p$, so the capacity obligation is an $O(N)$ read-off of a quantity
the allocator already maintains (\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair}).

\subsection{Schedule Construction}\label{sec:compiler-sched}

\sv{build\_schedule} (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5135--5239}) walks
\sv{su\_postorder} and maintains three per-PE clocks: \sv{gpu\_time[g]}
(when PE $g$ is next free), \sv{gci\_free[g]} (when $g$'s GCI is free to
issue the next GENERATE), and \sv{node\_ready[n]} (when node $n$'s
value is available).

A \texttt{GENERATE} on PE $g$ cannot start until
$\max(\sv{gpu\_time}[g], \sv{gci\_free}[g])$; its data is ready at
$t + \sv{gci\_latency} + 2 = t + 6$. The constant~$6$ is measured from
the RTL: \sv{gci\_latency = 4} is the state-machine count from
\texttt{gci\_start} to \texttt{gci\_done}, plus $1$~cycle for the pulse
to propagate, plus $1$~cycle for DMEM commit. This is
\emph{RTL-proven}: the software cost model uses the same constant, and
the hardware-observed cycle count matches.

A cross-PE child emits a \texttt{SEND} on the child's PE; the parent's
\texttt{COMPUTE} waits on arrival via scoreboard, not via wall-clock
estimation. This decouples estimation error in the cost model from
correctness: even if the cost model mispredicts arrival time, the
scoreboard gate ensures the parent \texttt{COMPUTE} reads the right
value --- it just stalls for longer.

The operand-binding line is the one that realises \textsf{C-bind}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
child_addrs = [addr_map[gpu][c]
               for c in tree.children(v)]
\end{lstlisting}
where \sv{tree.children(v)} returns $[\mathrm{left}(v),
\mathrm{right}(v)]$ by construction. The \sv{addr\_map} is the
allocator's output; \sv{gpu} is $\asgn(v)$; the children's addresses
are bound before the compute executes.

\subsection{Codegen and ABI Verification}\label{sec:compiler-codegen}

The instruction generator (\sv{generate\_instructions}) emits
cycle-accurate NOPs before each op to advance the instruction index to
the scheduled cycle, then the op itself. A two-pass peephole
(\sv{\_peephole\_nop\_compress}) collapses NOP runs while preserving
the GCI-serialisation gap, and strips trailing NOPs.

\textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{C-bind} and \textsf{C-leaf} are post-conditions on
the emitted IMEM (\S\ref{sec:mechanization}), checkable without executing
anything. The campaign path did not run them in the compile loop, so we
discharged them afterwards over the released images: \BindComputes{}
\texttt{COMPUTE} and \BindSends{} \texttt{SEND} instructions across
\BindConfigs{} configurations, \BindViolations{} violations, no missing or
out-of-range address, and no receive buffer with two writers. We report the
check this way --- as a static audit of the artifact rather than as a
compiler feature that never fired --- because that is what was run.

\subsection{Orthogonality of the Compiler Variation Space}\label{sec:compiler-orth}

By Argument A1 and Proposition~\ref{thm:orth}, compiler variation --- the
choice among 18 S2, 10 S3, and 122 S4 algorithms --- affects
\emph{which PE hosts a node} and \emph{when a \texttt{COMPUTE} fires},
not \emph{which operand pair is bound}. The campaign
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}) therefore optimises over a space
structurally incapable of affecting the bit result. For the mapping axis
the statement is stronger and correspondingly less interesting: $\map$ does
not reach the emitted image at all except through NOP padding
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-pipeline}), so invariance across S3 is close to
tautological, and the evidential weight of the campaign sits on the
assignment axis and on reshape across $(G,\text{topology})$. Empirically, every one of
the \WallTBs{} HW-validated configurations produces the same 64-bit root
result at its leaf count (Table~\ref{tab:invariance}); they differ only in
the cycle count. The remainder of the ${\sim}22{,}000$-point space is covered
by the argument above rather than by measurement.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

Evaluation proceeds along two axes: \emph{structural} validation of
Claim~\ref{claim:1} (same bit result across all configurations at the same
$N$), and \emph{performance} characterisation of the same-fabric overhead
(cycle count of ABI-compliant schedules relative to non-deterministic
schedules on the identical substrate). Both rest on a single cycle-accurate
simulation pipeline (Vivado xsim 2025.2).

\subsection{Lower Bound $\Omega$ as a Secondary Metric}\label{sec:method-lb}

We compute a composite lower bound $\Omega$ on any schedule on the
fabric (\sv{compute\_lower\_bound}, \sv{sprs\_core.py:1015}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
\begin{split}
\Omega \,=\, \max \bigl(
    & \text{lb}_{\text{span}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{comm}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{gather}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{bis}}, \\
    & \min_{K \in [K_{\min}, K_{\max}]}
      \max \bigl( \text{lb}_{\text{gen}}(K), \, \text{lb}_{\text{instr}}(K) \bigr)
\bigr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of PEs a schedule actually populates: $K_{\min} = 1$
and $K_{\max} = \min(G, 2N-1)$, since no schedule can use more PEs than the
fabric has or than the tree has nodes. Two of the six terms depend on $K$ ---
both $\text{lb}_{\text{gen}}$ and $\text{lb}_{\text{instr}}$ shrink as work
spreads over more PEs --- so a bound valid against \emph{every} schedule must
take the most favourable $K$ available, hence the inner minimisation. Taking
a maximum there would assert a bound that some legal schedule beats, which is
not a lower bound at all. The four $K$-independent terms bind regardless and
enter the outer maximum directly.

The six constituents are, informally:
$\text{lb}_{\text{span}}$, the zero-communication critical-path depth;
$\text{lb}_{\text{gen}}(K)$, the GCI serialisation bottleneck;
$\text{lb}_{\text{instr}}(K)$, the instruction-throughput bottleneck;
$\text{lb}_{\text{comm}}$, a single cross-PE hop floor;
$\text{lb}_{\text{gather}}$, the root PE's critical path;
$\text{lb}_{\text{bis}}$, link serialisation at the topology bisection.

$\Omega$ bounds any schedule --- deterministic or not --- and is
therefore not a valid cost-of-determinism denominator by itself. We
report $\Omega$ as an absolute-performance diagnostic in
Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} but not as the headline overhead metric.

\subsection{Non-Deterministic Baseline on the Same Fabric}\label{sec:method-nd}

The natural cost-of-determinism metric is the \emph{same-fabric overhead}
$\eta$: the ratio of \sprs{}'s cycle count to that of the best
non-deterministic reducer compiled onto the identical substrate --- same NoC,
same ALU, same memory hierarchy, same topology --- obtained by relaxing
\textsf{C-bind} to allow arbitrary operand binding at each internal node.
Two such baselines are natural: \emph{ND-Ring}, a linear worker-ring
traversal whose reduction order follows the physical ring, and
\emph{ND-RecDouble}, recursive doubling in $\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge
stages.

\textbf{We did not build these baselines, and we report no measured $\eta$.}
We also decline to substitute an analytical one. We built such a model --- the
same cost constants that produce $\Omega$, applied to a ring and to recursive
doubling --- and it returns $\hat\eta \approx 1$ on every instance in the
suite. An earlier version of this paper read that as a modelling artefact, on
the grounds that a cost model indexed by depth and hop count cannot see which
operands pair at each level. That reading was wrong, and the reason is
structural rather than numerical.

The ISA's \texttt{COMPUTE} is two-input: it names $\texttt{addr\_a}$ and
$\texttt{addr\_b}$ and nothing else (Table~\ref{tab:isa}), and the datapath
carries no third operand port. A partial sum of arity $k > 2$ is therefore not
expressible on this fabric at all, so every reducer over $N$ leaves has a
dependence chain of at least $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ combines --- which is
exactly the depth of $\FBT(N)$ (Definition~\ref{def:fbt}). Measured against
the strongest same-fabric alternative, a balanced local tree over each PE's
$\lceil N/G \rceil$ leaves followed by $\lceil \log_2 G \rceil$ merge
rounds, \sprs{} is never deeper on any of the \TournNInst{}
hardware-validated instances and is one level shallower on three of them. The
tree-depth term cannot be positive here.

At power-of-two shapes the statement is stronger than an inequality on depth.
Cutting $\FBT(N)$ at level $\log_2 G$ yields exactly $G$ complete subtrees of
exactly $N/G$ leaves, with exactly $G-1$ internal nodes above the cut --- which
is ND-RecDouble's own decomposition into a local phase and
$\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge stages. The two schedules are then
DAG-isomorphic: the same instruction multiset, the same dependences, the same
PE assignment, differing only in which leaf sits at which position. We verified
that construction directly on all $88$ pairs with $N = 2^a$, $a \in [3,13]$,
and $G = 2^b$, $b \in [1,a]$, with no exceptions. Both $N$ and $G$ are powers
of two on $31$ of the \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances, so on those
$\eta = 1$ is a theorem and no experiment can return anything else. The
analytical model returns $\hat\eta \approx 1$ because that is the answer, not
because it is blind to the difference; we still decline to quote it as a
measurement, because it is the compiler's own cost model and a ratio computed
from it would be circular whatever value it took.

A baseline would say something on the remaining $8$ instances, whose $N$ or $G$
is not a power of two and where the isomorphism does not hold. Building one
there is not a small change: relaxing \textsf{C-bind} means re-deriving the
DMEM allocator's single-writer discipline and the scoreboard gating around a
schedule that no longer has a canonical operand pairing, and the resulting
numbers would only be meaningful if the baselines were optimised with the same
effort we spent on \sprs{} --- otherwise $\eta$ measures our relative
implementation effort rather than the ABI. We flag that residual in
\S\ref{sec:limitations} (L1b) and price the ABI structurally instead
(\S\ref{sec:results-overhead}).

We note also that $\eta$, even if measured, would not isolate the cost of
determinism against a bandwidth-optimised production collective on NVLink or
Infinity Fabric silicon. Those systems are out of scope; such a comparison
would conflate the ABI cost with the much larger gap between a research
prototype and production silicon.

\subsection{Software-Only Baseline}\label{sec:method-swbaseline}

To contextualise the fabric-level mechanism against software-level
mitigations, we also compile a third baseline, \emph{SW-FixedTree}, a
software-only scheme emulating NCCL tree all-reduce forced to the same
tree shape across all $G \in \T$. Because NCCL tree shape is a function
of $G$ at the rank level, SW-FixedTree cannot be implemented without
either (a)~hardware support (which is what \sprs{} provides), or
(b)~worst-case padding to the topology-specific tree depth. We report
the analytical overhead of (b) as an upper bound on this software-only
alternative (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}). A third route --- replacing the
library's tree outright with a fixed one, rather than reshaping NCCL's --- is
not this baseline; it is the software export of
\S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt}, which is built, run against stock
\texttt{ncclAllReduce} and priced there.

\subsection{Instance Suite}\label{sec:method-suite}

Forty instances span $N \in \{8, 10, 16, 32, 50, 100, 128, 200, 500,
512, 1024, 1500, 2048, 3000, 4096, 8192\}$, $G \in \{2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10,
12, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096\}$, and six topologies
(linear, ring, 2D mesh, 2D torus, fat-tree, hypercube). Multiple
$(G, \text{topology})$ points at the same $N$ directly exercise the
invariance claim (Table~\ref{tab:invariance}). Per-$(N$-bucket,
topology) cell counts are in Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}; some cells
are thin due to convenience sampling, a limitation we discuss in
\S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\emph{Feasibility bound.} DMEM is fixed at $\DmemDepth$ entries per PE; IMEM is
not. Each testbench elaborates \sv{CN\_IMEM} to the next power of two above
its own longest per-PE program ($\ImemDepthLo$ to $\ImemDepthHi$ entries
here, \sv{sprs\_core.py:5380}). $\ImemFlagged$ of the $\CompileTotal$
compilations exceed the
$\DmemDepth$-instruction reference budget and are flagged \sv{E\_IMEM\_OF}; they were
emitted and simulated anyway, and $\ImemFlaggedValidated$ of them are among the \WallTBs{}
validated configurations. They cluster at both ends of the $N/G$ range: high
$N/G$ piles leaves onto too few PEs ($120$ flagged configurations each on
\sv{large\_extreme\_1d} and \sv{vlarge\_extreme\_1d}), while at $N/G = 2$ a PE
with few local nodes has many remote children to land. Instruction-memory
depth cannot change the root, so the $\DmemDepth$-entry budget should be read as the
deployment target priced in \S\ref{sec:resource}, not as the depth every
simulation used.

Leaf values are generated by \sv{make\_leaf\_val}
(\sv{make\_leaf\_val} in \sv{sprs\_core.py}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaves}
L[i] \;=\; (i+1)\cdot 1000.0 + \bigl((i \cdot 37 + 13) \bmod 100\bigr)/100 + 0.007.
\end{equation}
The formula is chosen so that every leaf carries a non-trivial fractional
part: the additive $((37i+13) \bmod 100)/100 + 0.007$ term guarantees a
populated mantissa, so each FADD exercises the guard, round and sticky bits
rather than reducing to an exact power-of-two addition. Magnitudes span
$10^3$ to $8\times10^6$ across the suite, so partial sums accumulate across a
wide exponent range and reassociation is observable in the low-order bits.

We deliberately do not claim that reordering \emph{always} changes the
result. It does not: at these magnitudes a sufficiently small perturbation is
absorbed by rounding, which is precisely what the mutation protocol of
\S\ref{sec:method-mutation} has to work around. What the value distribution
buys is that a bit mismatch between fabric and oracle is a determinism
violation rather than an artefact of degenerate inputs; the converse --- that
every violation must show up as a mismatch --- is established by mutation
testing, not by the choice of leaf values.

\subsection{Campaign Protocol}\label{sec:method-tour}

The per-instance configuration space is $18\times10\times122 = 21{,}960$
$(\asgn, \map, r)$ triples; simulating it exhaustively is infeasible
(${\approx}880{,}000$ xsim runs). We fix refinement at the identity and
enumerate the remaining $18\times10$ $(\asgn, \map)$ face \emph{exhaustively}
on every instance --- $\SelConfigsTotal$ of the $21{,}960$ triples per instance, $0.82\%$
of the space, $7{,}200$ compilations, of which $6{,}777$ emit a program image
($390$ size-gated, $33$ evaluation timeouts). Refinement is fixed at
\sv{None} throughout and is covered by the orthogonality argument of
\S\ref{sec:compiler-orth} rather than by measurement. The campaign is one
pass: compile, deduplicate, simulate.

\emph{Testbench-hash dedup.} Distinct $(\asgn, \map)$ pairs frequently produce
identical program images up to PE relabelling. A SHA-256 hash of the emitted
testbench collapses the $6{,}777$ images to $2{,}920$ distinct ones
($56.9\%$); followers inherit the leader's cycle count. This is not merely a
compute saving: identical images \emph{tautologically} produce identical bit
results, so dedup is what makes each remaining xsim run informative.

\emph{No shortlist.} Every distinct image was queued for xsim --- $12$ to
$142$ per instance, median $75$, covering $152$ of the $\SelConfigsTotal$ pairs --- so no
selection of any kind stands between the compiler and the invariance
evidence. The one exception is \sv{maxp\_mod\_fat}, dropped from the
HW-validated suite: five of its $76$ images were attempted and each exhausted
the $8$\,h simulator budget; the other $71$ were not run. The budget was not
the binding constraint, and \S\ref{sec:limitations} (L0) reports what was. A
top-$K$-by-\sv{makespan} shortlist would have missed a median
\TournLeakPctFifteen{} of the true top-$K$ by measured cycles at $K = 15$
(Appendix~\ref{app:bias}), which is why we did not use one.

\emph{Campaign accounting.} On the \TournNInst{} remaining instances $2{,}844$
distinct images were attempted, \WallTBs{} settled and passed, and $22$ did
not settle. Twenty-one of the $22$ are fabric fail-stops --- their retained
per-PE counters stop at a last retirement plus exactly $\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} =
20{,}000$ cycles, far inside the testbench budget --- and are the campaign's
only observation of Property~\ref{prop:2} on unmutated compiler output; the
twenty-second is a simulator wall-clock kill. The campaign cost $1{,}003.3$
core-hours: \WallTotalH{} on passing runs, $122.9$ on runs that did not settle
(retries included), $\MaxpCoreHours$ on \sv{maxp\_mod\_fat}.

\subsection{Mutation-Testing Protocol}\label{sec:method-mutation}

To confirm that the oracle harness can detect bit divergence (i.e., that
the zero-divergence result is non-vacuous), we inject two mutation
classes per instance and record the detection rate:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.3em,style=nextline]
\item[Address substitution.] In one randomly chosen \texttt{COMPUTE},
  repoint \texttt{addr\_a} at a different DMEM address. Breaks Argument A1
  while preserving A2--A4.
\item[Leaf corruption.] Perturb one leaf value by the smallest single-bit
  flip that provably moves the canonical root. Breaks \textsf{C-leaf}.
\end{description}

The escalation in the second class is necessary, not incidental, and it is
the same phenomenon the paper is about. Flipping the mantissa LSB is
frequently \emph{not} observable at the root: at these operand magnitudes the
perturbation falls below the round bit of the running sum and RNE discards
it, leaving the canonical root unchanged. A fabric reporting \sv{PASS} on
such a mutant is correct to do so, and counting it as a missed detection
would understate the oracle. We therefore raise the flipped bit position
until the software oracle confirms the root has actually moved, so that every
counted mutant is a test of detection rather than a test of whether rounding
happened to preserve the value.

We deliberately do not use operand-swap as a mutation: FADD is
commutative at the bit level under FTZ+RNE ($\fadd(a,b) = \fadd(b,a)$
for all 64-bit $(a,b)$), so operand-swap is bit-identical and would
spuriously inflate the false null-detection rate.

\subsection{Metric Convention}\label{sec:method-metric}

\sv{hw\_cycles} is the primary metric: $\sv{hw\_cycles} = \max_p
\sv{perf\_total\_cycles}[p]$ as measured by per-PE performance counters
in Vivado xsim. \sv{makespan}
from the software cost model is a diagnostic only. Summary statistics are
reported as medians with observed ranges rather than as interval estimates:
the per-instance samples are not independent draws from a population --- they
enumerate a deterministic configuration space --- so a resampling interval
would describe our sampling of that space rather than any underlying
uncertainty. Per-topology breakdowns are reported where cell counts permit;
thin cells are labelled.

\subsection{The Premise, Measured}\label{sec:results-premise}

The ABI is worth its cost only if reshaping a production collective really
does move the bits. Earlier versions of this paper argued that from the
arithmetic and from the libraries' own documentation; we have since measured
it. On \GpuHardware{}, we reduced identical input \emph{bytes} at
\GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts from $\GpuWorldLo$ to $\GpuWorldHi$ under
PyTorch~\GpuStack{}'s distributed collectives~\cite{li2020pytorch}, holding
the data, the seed and the code
fixed so that only the shape of the job changed. Leaf $i$'s value is a pure
function of $i$, so reshaping alters who holds which leaf and in what order
the partial sums combine, and nothing else.

The \GpuWorldSizes{} configurations returned \GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct
64-bit results, in every one of \GpuCases{} (leaf count, precision,
value-profile) cases; in the \GpuCanonCases{} \sv{fp64} cases, for which the
single-process canonical reduction was also computed, \GpuEqCanonical{} of the
\GpuWorldSizes{} matched it. Spreads reach \GpuUlpMax{}~ulp, a maximum
relative spread of $\GpuRelMax$, and at least \GpuElemsMovedMin{} of the
\GpuElemsTotal{} elements of the result vector move in every case; pooled over
every configuration swept, \GpuPairDblPct{} of \sv{fp64} configuration pairs
and \GpuPairSglPct{} of \sv{fp32} pairs disagree. Repeated runs at any \emph{fixed} configuration
were bitwise identical, \GpuRepeats{} times out of \GpuRepeats{}, with no
disagreement between ranks inside a run, and an independent re-run of the
sweep under a different bookkeeping harness reproduced \GpuXCheck{} of
\GpuXCheck{} common cells bit for bit. Reshape moves the answer; noise does
not. That decomposition --- determinism at a fixed shape, divergence across
shapes --- is the failure the ABI is built to remove, and
\S\ref{sec:results-invariance} is the same experiment on a fabric that holds
the shape fixed by construction.

Three scope conditions belong with the measurement rather than under it.
The rank-count sweep runs on the CPU backend, because NCCL requires one rank
per device and this node carries two GPUs, so partitions above $G{=}2$ could
not be placed on them. At $G{=}2$ the NCCL algorithm-by-protocol axis
(\GpuLibCfgs{} configurations) and the channel-count axis (\GpuChanCfgs{})
each returned a single root; that is structural and not an empirical null,
because at two ranks the cross-rank reduction is one floating-point addition
per element and no schedule can reassociate it, and we do not generalise it to
larger $G$. At that same partition the CPU and GPU roots differ in all
\GpuCases{} cases, so device placement moves the bits as well as partitioning
does. This measures reduction order on one node; it is not a benchmark of any
library and no timing claim is drawn from it.

\subsection{Cross-Topology Bitwise Invariance}\label{sec:results-invariance}

We state the paper's primary empirical result in the narrowest form the
campaign supports. Across the \InvAttempted{} distinct ABI-compliant program
images obtained by compiling the complete $18\times10$
assignment-by-mapping grid on the \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances
% G range corrected 4096 -> 2048: this apposition attaches to the \TournNInst{}
% HW-validated instances, whose G maximum is 2048.  The suite envelope reaches
% G = 4096 only through maxp_mod_fat, which has no hardware result (L0); the
% \SuiteSize{}-instance statements at C3 and in the conclusion keep [2,4096].
--- \InvLeafCounts{} leaf counts $N \in [8,8192]$, requested worker counts
$G \in [2,2048]$, six direct-network topologies --- \WallTBs{} simulations
terminated, and in every one of them the root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE}
result was bit-identical to the canonical root $\Rcan(N,L)$ the software
oracle predicts for that $N$. The remaining \InvNonSettling{} did not settle
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}); no configuration in the campaign produced a
finite root differing from $\Rcan(N,L)$.

Two scope conditions belong inside that sentence rather than in a footnote.
The campaign evaluates one leaf vector per $N$ and the identity refinement
throughout. And \InvSingletonRows{} of the \InvLeafCounts{} leaf counts are
represented by a single requested $(G,\text{topology})$ point: at those $N$
invariance is witnessed across assignment, mapping and schedule, but not
across reshape. \InvMultiPoint{} of the \WallTBs{} configurations lie at
leaf counts carrying two or more points, and that is where the reshape
evidence sits.

Where reshape is exercised it is not marginal. At $N{=}128$ the same root
survives six $(G,\text{topology})$ points spanning $G{=}4$ on a linear chain
to $G{=}128$ on a torus --- a $32\times$ change in worker count across five
distinct topologies --- and at $N{=}512$ and $N{=}1024$ it survives six and
five points respectively. The \InvAttempted{} images are moreover distinct
\emph{programs}, not repeated runs of one program: the campaign
deduplicates by SHA-256 over the emitted testbench and simulates one
representative per hash class (\S\ref{sec:method-tour}), so two images in
the count differ structurally, not merely in the compiler triple
$(\asgn, \map, r)$ that produced them. Invariance therefore holds
simultaneously under reshape and under complete replacement of the
assignment and mapping algorithms.

The campaign has since been re-executed from source. All \RouteRuns{} passing
configurations at $G \le \RouteGMax$ --- every one of the \RouteReexecInst{}

were recompiled and re-simulated under both route-table loading paths
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L0b), and on the \RouteReexec{} points that
completed under both, the two paths agree with each other and with the
software oracle on the same 64-bit value; each of the \RouteReexecInst{}
instances yields exactly one distinct root across all of its pipelines. The released results file records verdicts and cycle counts but

the exact one that produced that file, so this is a re-execution that

Table~\ref{tab:invariance} reports the result. All HW-validated
configurations at each $N$ produce the same 64-bit root, exactly
matching the software oracle, and the divergence column is zero as
predicted by Claim~\ref{claim:1}. Its rows are not equally informative,
and \S\ref{sec:abi-tree} measures why: at the smallest leaf counts a large
majority of alternative association orders reproduce the canonical root
anyway, so agreement there is close to free and the weight of the evidence
for Claim~\ref{claim:1} sits at the large-$N$ rows.

A zero-divergence column is only meaningful if the oracle can report a
non-zero one, which is what the injected-fault campaign is for
(\S\ref{sec:method-mutation}). Reporting it requires more care than a single
rate. The harness retains one boolean per mutant, and the testbench prints
\sv{[FAIL]} both on a value mismatch and from its \sv{!all\_done} branch on
any run that fails to complete; the two are not distinguishable in the
recorded output. Reconstructing the mutations against regenerated images
separates them. Of the \MutVerdicts{} address-substitution verdicts, in at
least \MutNoCompare{} the value comparison never ran: the substituted
address is one no agent ever writes, so the dependent \texttt{COMPUTE}
stalls on the presence gate and the flag is a fail-stop, not a detection.
Oracle non-vacuity therefore rests on \MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of the
\MutTotal{} verdicts --- every leaf corruption, plus the address
substitutions that happened to reach a live slot --- and not on all
\MutTotal{}, as we previously reported.

Read the other way round, the same fact is an asset rather than a
concession: \MutAddrStall{} of the \MutAddrSub{} address substitutions
fail-stopped instead of corrupting, and in every one we re-simulated the
fabric raised \texttt{P\_ERROR} with a recorded \sv{error\_pc} on every PE.
That is Property~\ref{prop:2}'s halt outcome observed under injected fault
rather than argued from the RTL, and \S\ref{sec:abi-claim} sets out why the
presence gate draws the boundary exactly there.

\emph{Extrapolation beyond the measured range.} Bit-result
invariance at untested $G$ rests on the structural argument of
\S\ref{sec:abi-claim}: operand binding (A1) and scoreboard gating (A3)
remain total functions of $N$ and $L$ as $G$ grows, so adding PEs
changes the \emph{location} of FADD operations but not the operand
pairs they receive. This is not a formal induction on $G$: adding a PE
introduces new routing paths, new VC contention, and new credit-return
interactions that may affect cycle counts. What carries is the
bit-result invariance under the fifteen assumptions; absolute cycle
counts at large $G$ are cycle-accurate simulation artefacts, not
silicon measurements, and we make no absolute competitiveness claim.

\emph{Coverage of the injected-fault campaign.} Mutants were injected across
all \InvLeafCounts{} leaf-count classes, spanning $N$ from $8$ to $8192$, so
no row of Table~\ref{tab:invariance} rests on an oracle that was never
exercised. One further mutant, on the largest instance, produced no verdict
at all and is excluded rather than counted either way. The leaf-corruption
column additionally depends on the bit-escalation protocol of
\S\ref{sec:method-mutation}: without it the column would measure how often
FP64 rounding absorbs a perturbation, not how often the oracle detects one.

\subsection{No Silicon Measurements}\label{sec:results-fpga}

Every cycle figure in this paper comes from cycle-accurate RTL simulation.
We ship Artix-7 (Nexys~A7) bring-up wrappers for $G \in \{2,4\}$, but
synthesising them additionally requires a Vivado VIO IP core and board
constraints that we do not redistribute, and we hold no board logs; we
therefore report no board-measured cycle count and make no silicon-timing
claim. This is a reporting gap, not a soundness gap: the invariance result of
\S\ref{sec:results-invariance} is a statement about bit values, which xsim
resolves exactly, and Claim~\ref{claim:1} is argued structurally rather than
by extrapolation from a boundary point.

\subsection{The Cost of the ABI}\label{sec:results-overhead}

The natural way to price the ABI would be to run, on this same fabric, the
best \emph{non}-deterministic reducer we could construct --- one free to
reassociate and to reshape its tree per $(G,\text{topology})$, though not to
pick $k$-ary partial sums, which this ISA cannot express
(\S\ref{sec:method-nd}) --- and report the ratio $\eta$ of the two makespans.
We did not build that baseline, and we therefore report no measured $\eta$.
We say so plainly because the alternative --- quoting a ratio against a
reducer that does not exist --- would not be a measurement.

One caveat attaches to every cycle number in this subsection and is not
repeated below: all of them come from cycle-accurate RTL simulation of a
single-chip fabric, so they price the schedule rather than the wire. Two
further caveats stood here in an earlier version and have since been
discharged by measurement rather than by argument: the route-table bypass that
every run uses contributes exactly zero uncertainty to these cycle counts
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L0b), and the premise that reshaping a production
collective changes its result is now measured
(\S\ref{sec:results-premise}). What is still not
measured is $\eta$ itself, and that is a decision rather than an omission: the
only quantity obtainable without building the baseline is the compiler's own
cost model, which \S\ref{sec:method-metric} admits only as a diagnostic, so a
ratio computed from it would be circular whatever it said. That the model
returns $\hat\eta \approx 1$ is nonetheless the right answer on most of the
suite, and \S\ref{sec:method-nd} establishes it by construction rather than
by model.

What can be said without a baseline is stronger than it first appears. The
ABI's cost is not an empirical accident to be discovered by benchmarking; it
is a consequence of the contract, and the contract is small enough to price
analytically. We give the decomposition below, then measure the one term of
it that this suite can isolate.

\subsubsection{Structural decomposition}

Four constraints, each a direct consequence of the ABI rather than of our
implementation, separate \sprs{} from an unconstrained reducer.

\begin{enumerate}
% Term 1 previously asserted the premise sec:method-nd refutes ("an
% unconstrained reducer may form partial sums of arbitrary arity ... the
% irreducible core of the ABI's cost").  Arity > 2 is not expressible on this
% fabric (tab:isa; rtl/btree_fsm_fast.sv declares dmem_rd_addr_a and
% dmem_rd_addr_b only), and FBT(N) attains the resulting depth floor on 39/39.
% The item is kept so depth is still accounted for; it now reports a zero.
\item \emph{FBT tree depth --- which costs nothing here.} The ABI fixes
  $\FBT(N)$, so the dependence chain is $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ deep
  regardless of $G$. A reducer free to form partial sums of arity $k$ would be
  $\log_k N$ deep, and off this fabric that is a real saving; on it, arity
  $k > 2$ is not expressible, because \texttt{COMPUTE} names two DMEM
  addresses and the datapath has no third operand port. Every same-fabric
  reducer is therefore at least $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ deep, which is
  exactly $\FBT(N)$'s depth, and this term is at most zero on all
  \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances (\S\ref{sec:method-nd}).
\item \emph{Operand-binding forbids reassociation.} Fixing
  $(\mathrm{left}(v),\mathrm{right}(v))$ at every internal node forbids
  regrouping operands by locality. An unconstrained reducer may sum
  PE-local values first and cross the network once; \sprs{} must cross
  wherever the tree says to cross, even when a locality-adjacent regrouping
  would be cheaper.
\item \emph{GCI serialisation.} Consecutive \texttt{GENERATE}s on one PE are
  spaced by $\sv{gci\_gap} = \sv{gci\_latency} + 2 = 6$ cycles. A PE holding
  $\lceil N/G \rceil$ leaves therefore spends at least
  $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ cycles producing them, and this floor grows with the
  leaves each PE holds, so it binds hardest at \emph{high} $N/G$ --- the
  regime in which the reduction has the most work to overlap it with, and
  still cannot. Unlike term~2, this term is an artefact of
  \emph{our} GCI, not of the ABI, and a multi-channel leaf interface would
  remove it.
\item \emph{Root-gather fan-in.} The root PE must receive contributions from
  the other $G-1$ PEs, giving at least $\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge stages
  plus one traversal of roughly half the network diameter. This is
  $\mathrm{lb}_{\text{gather}}$ in~\eqref{eq:omega} and it binds any reducer,
  deterministic or not.
\end{enumerate}

Term~2 is the price of reproducibility and cannot be engineered away without
abandoning the contract; term~1 is that price in a form this fabric cannot
charge, and reports zero. Terms 3 and 4 are shared with any reducer on
this fabric; term 3 is ours to fix, term 4 is nobody's.

\subsubsection{Where the champion's cycles go}

Term~3 can be measured directly rather than through a ratio, and that is how
we now report it. The GCI floor $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ accounts for at most
\GciShareLowMax{} of the champion makespan on every one of the \GciLowInst{}
instances with $N/G \le 2$, and for at least \GciShareHighMin{} on every one
of the \GciHighInst{} instances with $N/G > 8$, rising to
\GciShareTopLo{}--\GciShareTopHi{}\% on the \GciTopInst{} instances with
$N/G \ge 32$ (Spearman $\rho = \GciShareRho{}$, $n = \TournNInst{}$). The two
groups do not overlap. Since $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ assumes a perfectly balanced
leaf assignment, and any real assignment puts at least $\lceil N/G\rceil$
leaves on the busiest PE, these are \emph{lower} bounds on the true share.
Leaf-generation serialisation is therefore the dominant term at high $N/G$,
which is the quantitative backing for the remark under term~3 that a
multi-channel leaf interface would remove it: on this suite that single
interface change is the highest-value modification available to the fabric,
and it costs nothing in the ABI.

\emph{A prediction we withdraw.} An earlier version of this section tested
term~3 by banding $\mathrm{cycles}/\Omega$ against $N/G$ and read the
resulting decline as a successful prediction of a knee at $N/G \in [6,8]$.
That test is circular and we withdraw it. $\Omega$ equals $6\lceil N/G\rceil$
on \GciOmegaEq{} of the \TournNInst{} instances, so the term under test is
the denominator; the absolute distance $\mathrm{cycles}-\Omega$ carries no
$N/G$ signal at all ($\rho = \GciNullRho{}$, $p = \GciNullP{}$); and a
one-parameter model with no mechanism in it, $\mathrm{cycles} = \Omega +
\SlackMedian{}$, reproduces the published band medians at and above the
predicted knee to within \GciNullTol{}. The banded table, the per-instance
figure and a scan over candidate knee locations are in the companion
document. Nothing is lost by the withdrawal: the direct measurement above
does not involve $\Omega$, and it points the same way term~3 does --- indeed
the opposite way to the withdrawn reading, since GCI serialisation binds
\emph{above} the claimed knee, not below it.

\subsubsection{Distance to the lower bound}

The one absolute statement we can make is how far the compiler lands from
$\Omega$, the composite lower
bound\footnote{As implemented, $\Omega$ additionally evaluates a GCI-aware Hu/HLF
  term and derives $K_{\min}$ from IMEM depth as $\lceil
  N/\OmegaKminDen\rceil$ rather than fixing it at $1$. Neither is the argmax
  on any of the \SuiteSize{} instances, so~\eqref{eq:omega} as printed
  reproduces every $\Omega$ reported here; the discrepancy is documentary,
  not numerical.} of \S\ref{sec:method-lb}.
Because $\Omega$ bounds \emph{any} schedule on this fabric --- including a
non-deterministic one --- the ratio $\text{cycles}/\Omega$ upper-bounds what
an ideal unconstrained reducer could have saved. It is a weaker statement
than $\eta$, but it is a real one, and it is measured rather than asserted.

\subsection{Champion Equivalence Classes}\label{sec:results-classes}

Earlier versions of this paper reported a winner distribution: which
assignment algorithm and which mapping algorithm produced the fastest image
on each instance. We withdraw it, because it was not a measurement, and the
reason it was not is the paper's own subject.

The campaign deduplicates by program image before simulating
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}), so one simulated row stands for a class of
bit-identical programs, and the champion of an instance is a \emph{set} of
configurations rather than a configuration. Expanding those classes back over
the full $18\times10$ grid: the minimum measured cycle count is attained by
more than one configuration on \ClsTied{} of the \TournNInst{} instances, by
a median of \ClsMedian{} configurations and by as many as \ClsMax{}; all ten
mapping algorithms attain it on \ClsAllMappings{} of \TournNInst{}; and
exactly \ClsUnique{} instance in the suite has a unique champion. Which
member of a tie set the published table named was decided by a minimum over
CSV rows in parallel-worker arrival order --- that is, by which simulation
host happened to finish first. A paper about scheduling nondeterminism had its
winner table ordered by scheduling nondeterminism, and saying so is more
useful than quietly re-sorting it: no tie-break repairs the table, because on
\ClsTied{} of \TournNInst{} instances the tied set contains programs that are
bit-identical and therefore cannot be separated by any measurement at all.

What replaces the ranking is stronger, and the data for it was already in the
paper. Proposition~\ref{thm:orth} says the compiler's variation space cannot
reach the bit result. The campaign shows that same space collapsing
structurally before it ever reaches the fabric: of the \ClsCompilations{}
compilations performed, \ClsCollapse{} produce a program image that another
configuration had already produced (\S\ref{sec:method-tour}). At the optimum the collapse is
near-total. The compiler variation space is thus not merely incapable of
changing the answer --- over most of its extent it does not even yield a
different program. The tournament is, from the ABI's point of view,
\WallTBs{} attempts to break Claim~\ref{claim:1}, every one of which failed;
it is not a ranking of algorithms.

\subsection{A Derived Selection Rule}\label{sec:selrule}

The tournament was built to answer a practitioner's question --- given an
instance, which of the $\SelConfigsTotal$ compiler configurations should be
run? --- and champion-class membership lets us answer it in a form that is
invariant to the tie structure just described. For a recommended set of
configurations we report \emph{regret}: the measured cycle count of the best
member of that set, divided by the instance's measured optimum. A regret of
$1.000$ means the set contains a champion.

\emph{The rule is a portfolio, not a regime map.} Exhaustive search over
subsets of the \SelSearchAlgs{} near-universally evaluable assignment
algorithms, minimising mean measured regret and refitted independently in
each of \SelFolds{} leave-one-out folds, returned the \emph{identical} subset
in \SelFolds{} of \SelFolds{} folds at every size. At size \SelKFour{} that
subset is \SelPortfolio{}, and on the held-out instance it attains the exact
hardware-measured optimum in \SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{} folds
($\SelKFourRate$), with mean out-of-sample regret \SelKFourMean{} and worst
case \SelKFourWorst{}. It evaluates \SelConfigsRun{} of the
$\SelConfigsTotal$ configurations, a median $\SelSpeedup\times$ reduction in
compiler time ($\SelCompileBefore$\,s to $\SelCompileAfter$\,s per instance).
Table~\ref{tab:selection_rule} gives it, together with the smaller budgets.

\emph{Conditioning on the instance makes it worse, not better.} This is the
result we did not expect and the one that makes the portfolio finding
non-obvious. Every decision tree we could fit over $(N, G, N/G,
\text{topology})$ is beaten out of sample by the unconditional portfolio: the
best of them reaches an exact-optimum rate of $\SelTreeRate$
(\SelTreeOpt{} of \TournNInst{}) against $\SelKFourRate$, and its
out-of-sample mean regret, \SelTreeMeanLo{}--\SelTreeMeanHi{}, is worse even
than the single-algorithm constant rule ``always \sv{ParSub}''
(\SelConstMean{}). A regime map over these axes is not merely unnecessary
here; it is actively harmful, because it fits partitions of a
\TournNInst{}-instance suite whose cells hold two or three instances each. The
simplest conditioner of all fails the same way: taking whichever algorithm was
best on this instance's topology in training attains the measured optimum on
\SelCondTopoOpt{} of \TournNInst{} held-out instances against \SelKFourOpt{}
for the unconditional portfolio, at mean out-of-sample regret
\SelCondTopoMean{} against \SelKFourMean{}. Topology is the one axis on which
conditioning is most intuitive, and it is the axis on which conditioning most
clearly does not pay.
Against the two pre-specified constant baselines the portfolio is
significant on both confirmatory tests (McNemar $p = \SelMcNemarP$; Wilcoxon
signed-rank on regret $p = \SelWilcoxonP$; both survive Holm correction over
the two comparisons).

\emph{It transfers to a topology the fit never saw.} Leave-one-out holds out
an instance but not its topology, and instances sharing a topology are not
independent, so we also refit the portfolio with an \emph{entire topology}
withheld and score each fold only on the topology it never saw. Across the
\SelTopoGroups{} topologies of the suite the portfolio transfers better than
conditioning generalises: with the topology entirely unseen its mean
out-of-sample regret is \SelUnseenTopoMean{} (95\% bootstrap interval
$[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$ over \SelBootReps{} resamples of the
\TournNInst{} instances), lower than the \SelCondTopoMean{} of a rule that
conditions on topology and does see it in training. The exact measured optimum
is still attained on \SelUnseenTopoOpt{} of \TournNInst{} instances
(\SelUnseenTopoPct{}), with a worst case of \SelUnseenTopoWorst{}. Withholding an entire leaf-count band
instead leaves the leave-one-out figure unchanged (\SelUnseenScaleMean{}).
We quote the interval and not the point estimate deliberately: with only
\SelTopoGroups{} topologies the grouped estimate is genuinely uncertain at the
top end, and a practitioner adopting the rule on an unseen topology should
plan against \SelUnseenTopoHi{} rather than against the mean. The
corresponding leave-one-out interval is $[\SelLooCiLo, \SelLooCiHi]$. Size
\SelKnee{} is a knee rather than a budget we picked: a fifth configuration is
identical to four decimal places under both protocols (\SelKneeNextMean{} with
a topology held out), and only at six does held-out regret reach
\SelKSixMean{}.

\emph{The mapping stage can be deleted.} Assignment is the whole decision.
Conditional on \sv{ParSub}, the ten mapping algorithms give byte-identical
cycle counts on all \TournNInst{} instances. Fixing the mapping at
\sv{Identity} for every instance and choosing only the assignment attains the
exact optimum on \SelIdentityOpt{} of \TournNInst{}, at a mean cost of
\SelIdentityMean{} and a worst case of \SelIdentityWorst{}. We therefore
recommend removing the mapping search from the pipeline rather than
conditioning on it. Note what this does \emph{not} say: \sv{Identity} is not
the best mapping --- its apparent lead in the withdrawn winner table was a
labelling artefact (\S\ref{sec:results-classes}) --- it is indistinguishable
from the others and the cheapest of them to compute. In the other direction,
the spread across assignment algorithms is three orders of magnitude larger.
\SelZeroCount{} of the eighteen are in the champion class on \emph{zero} of
\TournNInst{} instances and cost a median $\SelZeroRegretLo\times$ to
$\SelZeroRegretHi\times$ in cycles, up to $\SelZeroWorst\times$ at worst.
Greedy list scheduling is the block to avoid.

\emph{What the rule does not license.} Nothing here survives Holm correction
over the \SelExpTests{} exploratory association tests we ran between
algorithm families and instance parameters; \SelBHSurvivors{} survive
Benjamini--Hochberg, and we report the portfolio rather than any of them
precisely because the portfolio needs no such association to hold. $N$ and

own cost model --- Appendix~\ref{app:bias} measures exactly how far that
falls short. Every number is a measured cycle count on this pipeline for
tree-structured reduction programs; none of it transfers to another compiler,
another accelerator, or to MPI or NCCL collectives.

\subsection{What the ABI Buys}

The paper's contribution is not the compiler's algorithms or the
fabric's microarchitecture --- both are composed from standard prior art
--- but the interface observation: if compiler and fabric agree on a
size-indexed reduction tree as a shared ABI, then cross-topology bitwise
invariance follows from composition. Every subsequent design decision
in the compiler and fabric amounts to \emph{discharging obligations}
(\textsf{C-*}) or \emph{establishing invariants} (\textsf{F-*}); none of
them changes the shape of the argument.

The practical payoff is that \sprs{} users can reshape their cluster
freely --- from $G=2$ to $G=4096$, from linear to hypercube --- without
touching their code, and still get the same bits out. Regression testing
becomes bit-for-bit across hardware generations. Replay debugging
survives migration to new silicon. ML training, rerun three months
later on a larger cluster, produces the same weights. These are
engineering outcomes that production collectives cannot provide today,
and that single-node techniques cannot address at all.

\subsection{When Not to Use the ABI}\label{sec:discussion-notuse}

Three regimes make \sprs{} a poor fit.

\emph{Interoperability with external reducers.} \sprs{}'s bit result
agrees across any reshape \emph{within} its own fabric-compiler pair,
but does not match NCCL, RCCL, ReproBLAS, ExBLAS, or any reducer using
a different canonical order. A deployment that must consume or produce
bit-compatible results with an external tool needs either (a)~a
one-time canonical-form translation, or (b)~an input permutation adapter
that presents leaves in the external tool's canonical order --- which
sacrifices the cross-reshape guarantee, trading one form of
reproducibility for another.

\emph{Strict IEEE 754.} Workloads whose reference implementation
exercises subnormals, multiple rounding modes, or the signed-zero
cancellation rule of IEEE 754-2019 \S6.3 will see \sprs{} diverge from
reference on those regimes (\S\ref{sec:fadd}). The narrower FTZ+RNE
semantics was a scope decision; a strict-IEEE FADD implementation
inherits Claim~\ref{claim:1} unchanged.

\emph{Bandwidth-critical inner loops.} The structural asymptote determined
by $\log_2 N$ versus $\log_G N$ is a real cost in inner loops where
every cycle is contested. Workloads that can tolerate reshape
non-reproducibility should use a non-deterministic reducer; the ABI
buys invariance at a non-zero price.

\subsection{Generalisation to Other Deterministic Operations}

Claim~\ref{claim:1} requires only (i) a deterministic binary operation
(for us, $\fadd$) and (ii) a size-indexed canonical tree. It does not
require floating-point, associativity, or any particular algebraic
property. Any associative or non-associative binary operation ---
FP32/FP16 ADD, integer ADD with saturating or modular arithmetic,
bitwise OR/AND/XOR, integer MAX/MIN, monoidal aggregations --- inherits
the claim by substituting the \sv{alu\_op} code. Reductions over ordered
types with a non-associative combine (string concatenation, stream
merges with priority) inherit the claim trivially because the canonical
tree \emph{is} the order.

Tensors follow by element-wise extension: reducing a tensor of $D$
elements under an ABI-compliant schedule is $D$ independent scalar
reductions. The fabric does not need per-element indexing; the compiler
emits $D$ parallel IMEM images, one per element, sharing the same
routing and the same PE assignment.

Non-reduction collectives --- all-gather, scatter, broadcast, alltoall
--- are orthogonal to the ABI: they have no intermediate combine, so
their results are bit-identical by construction on any routing
discipline. The ABI is specifically the statement that reductions, the
one collective with an internal combine, can be reproducible too.

\subsection{Exporting the ABI: Pinned-FBT for Production Collectives}\label{sec:discussion-pinfbt}

Can the ABI discipline be adopted without the \sprs{} fabric? It can, in
software: replace a production collective's dynamic $G$-dependent reduction
tree with a library that enforces the $\FBT(N)$ post-order schedule whatever
$G$ and the topology are. We call that \emph{pinned-FBT}. Two things have to
be said before the construction is stated, because we got the first wrong
and the second is not ours.

\emph{The construction we published is incorrect.} It partitioned the
canonical leaf indices into contiguous ranges,
$\mathrm{rank}(i) = \lfloor i \cdot G/N \rfloor$, and asserted that each
rank then reduces exactly the subtree of $\FBT(N)$ rooted at the lowest
common ancestor of its leaves. That assertion is false. Swept over all
\SweepPairs{} pairs with $2 \le N \le 300$ and $2 \le G \le N$ it holds on
\SweepStepOneHolds{} of them (\SweepStepOnePct{}); the smallest counterexample is $N=3$, $G=2$, where rank
$0$ owns canonical leaves $\{0,1\}$ whose lowest common ancestor is the root
of the whole tree; and it fails on $8$ of the \SuiteSize{} instances this
paper evaluates. Nor is it merely a description error. Evaluated literally
in FP64 on our own leaf vector~\eqref{eq:leaves}, the composed construction
returns a $64$-bit root differing from $\Rcan(N,L)$ on \PinIrMismatch{} of
those pairs, on \SweepRootDivergePct{} of the \PinSuitePairs{} pairs the suite's own
leaf counts admit, and on two of our own instances --- $N=10, G=4$ by $2$ ulp
and $N=200, G=12$ by $1$ ulp. The discrepancy is $1$--$\PinIrUlp{}$ ulp
throughout, which is small in magnitude and is nonetheless total failure of a
bitwise claim.
A second, independent error survives any repair of the first: an all-reduce
over sub-roots ``consistent with $\FBT(N)$'s top $\log_2 G$ levels'' is
$\FBT(G)$ only when $N$ and $G$ are both powers of two, so the root differs
even at pairs where the partition does happen to be subtree-aligned
($N=10, G=9$ and $N=32, G=31$ among them). The published construction is
exact precisely when $N$ and $G$ are both powers of two.

\emph{The corrected construction.} Cut $\FBT(N)$ at depth
$d = \lceil \log_2 G \rceil$ instead of partitioning index ranges. The cut
frontier --- the nodes at depth $d$, together with any leaf shallower than
$d$ --- roots disjoint complete subtrees that cover every leaf; assign those
subtrees to the $G$ ranks; each rank evaluates its subtrees by the canonical
recurrence of Def.~\ref{def:fbt} under the enumeration of
Def.~\ref{def:leafenum}; the cross-rank stage evaluates the internal nodes
above the frontier, exactly $2^{d}-1$ combines at depth $d$ --- the
$\log_2 G$-depth all-reduce the published version wanted and did not have.
The proof obligation is one sentence, and we state it rather than assert the
conclusion a second time: every node of $\FBT(N)$ is still computed exactly
once, by the same recurrence and with the same operand binding
(Def.~\ref{def:opbind}), so the multiset of FADD operations and the
dependency order among them are those of $\FBT(N)$ itself, and the root is
$\Rcan(N,L)$ for every $G$. Enumeration discharges it over the same space
that refutes the published version: every one of those \SweepPairs{} pairs is
exact in FP64, with a cross-rank stage of the stated shape at each. The frontier-to-rank assignment does not affect the root --- the tree
fixes the order --- so it is a free load-balancing choice, and the choice
matters. Under a greedy largest-first deal the worst leaf-count ratio over
those pairs is \PinImbLpt{} and none exceeds $2$ (\RepairImbalance{} over the
subset with $G \le \PinWorldHi{}$); under the natural
contiguous deal it reaches \PinImbContig{} and exceeds $2$ on
\PinImbContigOver{} of them. An export must therefore \emph{specify} the
greedy deal, and we report the bound as measured over this space rather than
as proved. The proof itself belongs in the companion.

\emph{Prior art, and the residue.} TBIK~\cite{tbik2025} (arXiv:2511.17826,
21 November 2025) predates this manuscript: it is prior art, not concurrent
work, and we claim no priority against it. Its Appendix~C, Theorem~1 proves
by induction, over the same contiguous-block partition, exactly the theorem
our step~1 asserted without proof, under exactly the power-of-two hypotheses
that make it true, and \S\ref{sec:related} records what it then built and
measured --- a tree all-reduce costing $10\%$ or less (its Appendix~H.1, four
H20 GPUs over NVLink at ${\rm TP}=4$; its larger end-to-end figure bundles
batch-invariant kernels and a custom MatMul for which this work has no
analogue). The summary is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly: the
correct part of pinned-FBT is
what TBIK had already done, and the part TBIK did not do --- arbitrary,
non-power-of-two $N$ --- is the part that was wrong. What remains ours is
the cut-aligned repair for arbitrary $N$ under canonical heap-index leaf
enumeration, together with the properties the export inherits from the ABI
and TBIK does not test: invariance across network \emph{topologies} rather
than across tensor-parallel sizes inside one node, enforcement below the
software stack, and presence-gated fail-stop (Property~\ref{prop:2}). That
residue is why the repaired construction is worth measuring rather than
merely citing.

\emph{Measured: the repair is invariant, and the checker can fail.} We
implemented both constructions on top of PyTorch's collectives and ran them on
\GpuHardware{} --- the node of \S\ref{sec:results-premise} --- with NCCL on the
two GPUs and the CPU backend for larger world sizes. Over \PinCases{} cases
spanning $N$ from \PinLeafLo{} to \PinLeafHi{} (\PinNpowLeaf{} of the leaf
counts not powers of two --- the case TBIK does not cover, and the majority of
the sweep), world sizes \PinWorldLo{}--\PinWorldHi{} (\PinNpowWorld{} not
powers of two), messages from \PinMsgLo{} to \PinMsgHi{}, and \PinDeals{}
frontier deals, every one of the \PinCells{}
(case, world size, rank) cells returned a single $64$-bit root, and that root
equalled the single-process canonical $\Rcan(N,L)$. The result is reportable only because the same checker demonstrably fails: run against the index-range construction as a
pre-registered negative control, \PinNegFired{} of \PinNegCases{} cases broke
as they must --- at $N=\PinNegLeafA{}$ and $N=\PinNegLeafB{}$ it returned
\PinNegDistinct{} distinct roots across world sizes \PinNegWorlds{} and
\emph{none} of its \PinNegCells{} cells equalled $\Rcan$. Where it agrees ---
\PinNegPartial{} of \PinNegCells{} cells at $N=1{,}024$, all of them at
$N=7$ --- it agrees by power-of-two coincidence, which is what the sweep above
says analytically and this says with a running collective.

\emph{Measured: what it costs, and the answer is unfavourable.} The statistic
was fixed before the run: geometric-mean overhead against stock
\texttt{ncclAllReduce} at $G=2$ in FP64 over the swept message sizes, worst
case printed beside it, with a support branch at roughly $1.0$--$1.3\times$ ---
the order of TBIK's published figure --- and an explicit unfavourable branch.
The measurement took the unfavourable branch. Over \PinCostCells{}
configurations the geometric mean is $\PinCostGeo\times$ stock, best
$\PinCostBest\times$, worst $\PinCostWorst\times$. The more flattering
$\PinCostPooled\times$ that also appears in the artifact's cost table is not
this statistic --- it pools \PinCostPooledCells{} rows over both FP64 and FP32,
which geomeans $\PinCostSgl\times$ on its own --- and survives there only as a
recorded deviation from the pre-registration.

Two qualifications pull in opposite directions and neither changes the
verdict. First, the sweep ran while an unrelated job saturated both GPUs, so
the point estimate is \emph{provisional}: substituting the best of the repeats
at each configuration for the median gives $\PinCostRobust\times$, so
contention inflates the reported figure by about $\PinCostInflate\times$. Both
estimators are far outside the pre-registered support band, so the verdict does
not depend on the correction --- the point estimate does, and a re-run on a
quiet machine is owed. Second, the overhead is not one quantity. At $G=2$ the
pinned tree moves $2M$ bytes, one directed send of the full vector plus a
broadcast of it, against a bandwidth-optimal ring's $M$ per rank: a factor of
about two is algorithmic and no engineering removes it. The remainder is an
export artefact --- two dependent library calls issued from Python against one
fused NCCL kernel --- and it dominates at small messages
($\PinSmallCost\times$ at \PinSmallSize{}, where the whole stock call takes
$\PinSmallStock$~$\mu$s) while fading at large ones
($\PinLargeLo$--$\PinLargeHi\times$ between \PinLargeSizeLo{} and
\PinLargeSizeHi{}). A two-chunk bidirectional variant separates the two
terms: at \PinBigSize{} it recovers part of the bandwidth term
($\PinBigChunkTwo\times$ against $\PinBigChunkOne\times$) and below $1$~MiB it
is far worse ($\PinSmallChunkTwo\times$), which is what doubling the number of
dependent calls should do. We report no cost ratio from the CPU backend at
all: at the larger world sizes its stock arm was itself contended and is not
self-consistent --- at $N=\PinGlooBadLeaf{}$, $G=\PinGlooBadWorld{}$ it takes
$\PinGlooBadRatio\times$ longer on \PinGlooBadLo{} than on \PinGlooBadHi{},
which no correct baseline does --- so those cells bound nothing, and the clean
re-run is queued and not reached.

\emph{What that means, and what it does not.} The result contradicts no claim
this paper makes and prices one it already predicts:
\S\ref{sec:discussion-notuse} names bandwidth-critical inner loops as the
regime where the ABI is the wrong choice, and $\PinCostGeo\times$ on a PCIe pair is that prediction with a number
on it. What we claim for pinned-FBT is a correctness property, measured above;
what we do not claim is that it is cheap. It should not be set beside TBIK's
$10\%$: that figure is four H20 GPUs over NVLink on an uncontended node,
a different fabric measured a different way. On this evidence the export is
usable where reshape reproducibility is the requirement and the collective is
not the bottleneck, and not inside a bandwidth-critical loop. Closing the
non-algorithmic part of the gap is an engineering problem; the algorithmic
factor of two at $G=2$ is what the tree costs.

\emph{The broader point.} Fabric-level and software-level enforcement can
discharge the same structural obligation, but the record above is what it
looks like when a construction is believed to discharge it and does not.
Reduction order is the interface; that it is enforced rather than intended
is the part a reader can check.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

Relative to every axis below, what we claim is the conjunction stated in
\S\ref{sec:intro} --- reduction order as a size-indexed compiler/fabric
contract, enforced in hardware with a fail-stop failure mode, invariant
across network topologies, with the IEEE-754 FP64 operator unchanged --- and
not any single conjunct, each of which has a precedent here.

\subsection{Reproducible Arithmetic}

Compensated summation~\cite{kahan1965summation,higham2002accuracy,rump2009accurate}
reduces the error of a single reduction but does not make it reproducible:
the same values compensated in a different order, or with a different number
of lane accumulators, still differ in the last bit, so both sources survive
it.

Order-independent accumulators do give reproducibility, and at cluster
scale. ReproBLAS~\cite{demmel2013reproBLAS} carries a binned accumulator
whose sum does not depend on order and ships MPI reduction operators, so the
result is the same whatever reduction-tree shape MPI chooses;
ExBLAS~\cite{iakymchuk2016exblas} combines a long accumulator with
floating-point expansions, and Collange et al.~\cite{collange2015reproducible}
analyse the parallel reduction directly; Arteaga, Fuhrer and
Hoefler~\cite{arteaga2014bitreproducible} build bit-reproducible distributed
reductions for a production weather code, invariant under process count to
$16{,}384$ processes at under $10\%$ overhead for large arrays; Balaji and
Kimpe~\cite{balaji2013reproducible} study the same question for MPI
reduction operations; Ahrens, Demmel and Nguyen~\cite{ahrens2020reproducible}
give the current algorithms for the binned family. All of them make the
result independent of order by making the operator effectively associative.
That is a strictly stronger guarantee than ours in one respect --- it holds
across implementations, not just across reshapes of one implementation ---
and weaker in another: it changes the arithmetic, so the answer is not the
IEEE-754 FP64 sum any particular order would produce, and it needs a wide
accumulator datatype. Our ABI leaves the arithmetic alone and constrains the
order instead; the two are complementary, and a binned accumulator obeying
the same operand-binding contract would inherit Claim~\ref{claim:1}
unchanged.

In hardware that line becomes exact accumulation: Kulisch's complete
accumulator~\cite{kulisch2013arithmetic}, exact accumulation and dot-product
units in silicon~\cite{dedinechin2008accumulation,koenig2017exactdot}, and
the posit quire~\cite{gustafson2017posit}. It is the obvious alternative to
what we do, and we decline it for three reasons. \emph{Silicon}: an
accumulator covering the FP64 product exponent range is thousands of bits of
architected state per accumulation context, against the $64$ bits an FP64
adder produces, replicated per lane and live across context switches.
\emph{Fidelity}: its intermediates are not IEEE-754 FP64 values and there is
one rounding, at the end, so no partial sum a conventional reducer would
compute is observable. \emph{Interoperability}: the answer is the correctly
rounded sum, which agrees with other exact accumulators and disagrees with
every FP64 reduction tree, ours included. We keep an ordinary adder, and pay
for it with a guarantee that is self-consistency under reshape rather than
agreement with an external canonical form (\S\ref{sec:intro}).

\subsection{Fixed-Order Reproducibility in Deployed Stacks}

The closest prior work keeps the FP64 operator and pins the order. Intel
oneMKL's strict conditional-numerical-reproducibility mode produces
bitwise-identical \sv{?gemm}, \sv{?trsm} and \sv{?symm} regardless of the
number of threads and no matter how the inputs are
partitioned~\cite{onemkl_cnr}: a shipping library with operator-preserving
invariance under a change of resource count, within one node.
TBIK~\cite{tbik2025} imposes one fixed full binary tree shared by intra- and
inter-GPU reduction, proves the resulting invariance for power-of-two
configurations, and demonstrates bitwise-identical inference across
tensor-parallel sizes in production serving stacks, the tree collective
itself costing about $10\%$ or less. RepDL~\cite{repdl2025} pursues
bit-level reproducible training and inference through correct rounding and
order invariance. Our ABI is the same idea --- one fixed tree, indexed by
problem size --- and differs on the axes Table~\ref{tab:prior} records:
arbitrary rather than power-of-two leaf counts, invariance across network
topologies rather than within one node's tensor-parallel dimension,
enforcement in the fabric rather than in a kernel library, and a fail-stop
failure mode. We read TBIK as independent corroboration of the export path
of \S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt}: a fixed reduction tree is deployable in a
production stack at a cost its users accepted.

Production collectives are deterministic only within a deployment. NCCL and
RCCL fix the (algorithm, protocol) pair at communicator initialisation from
architecture, node count, rank count and message
size~\cite{nccl2018,rccl2019}, so pinning them fixes the order for one
$(G,\text{topology})$ and no more. Shanmugavelu et
al.~\cite{shanmugavelu2024} survey the resulting variability across HPC and
deep-learning stacks and measure it on current accelerators.

\subsection{Collective-Scheduling Compilers}

TACCL~\cite{shah2023taccl}, SCCL~\cite{cai2021synthesizing}, and
BLINK~\cite{wang2020blink} compile performance-optimised schedules for
collective operations. They share with \sprs{} the compiler-centric view of
the collective and differ in objective (throughput versus reproducibility)
and in output (a schedule versus an ABI contract); none states a
cross-topology bitwise invariance property, nor could it, since their
schedules are $(G,\text{topology})$-dependent by design. Our compiler is
architecturally closest to this line, but contributes no new search
algorithm: the point is that a size-indexed tree yields the invariance
property \emph{regardless} of which winning schedule the search emits.
Communication-optimal tree
constructions~\cite{huffman1952,jeannot2014treematch} are subject to the
same remark --- they optimise at fixed $(G,\text{topology})$ and do not
survive reshape.

\subsection{In-Network Reduction}

SHARP~\cite{graham2016scalable} reduces inside InfiniBand switches, and pins
operand order to do so: each aggregation node's context includes an order of
calculation, and the reduction fires only once the requests are held in a
predefined deterministic order~\cite{mellanox2019aggregation}. That is our
instinct --- fix the order in the fabric --- with a different index. The
specification is explicit that reproducibility holds ``for a given
application-to-system mapping'', i.e.\ per deployment, where ours is indexed
by $N$ and survives the deployment changing. It also prices the discipline
in hardware, offering a non-reproducible mode that saves aggregation
buffering by a factor of $n/2$ in the number of inputs $n$ --- independent
evidence that reproducibility in a switch is paid for structurally, which is
how we price it in \S\ref{sec:results-overhead}.
SwitchML~\cite{sapio2021scaling} aggregates in programmable switches whose
ALUs are integer-only, carrying gradients in $32$-bit fixed point with
adaptive scaling on the workers; its order-insensitivity therefore comes
from integer associativity, like the accumulators above, and not from
pinning an order.

The novelty here is framing, not results: SHARP already achieves
per-deployment determinism, and we claim no advantage in the speed or cost of
the reduction itself --- only an index that survives a change of deployment,
which a per-mapping order cannot without standardising switch hierarchies
across sites.

\subsection{Deterministic Execution and Deterministic Accelerators}

In the architecture literature ``determinism'' usually means schedule
determinism, not numerical reproducibility, and the gap between them is
exactly our Source-1/Source-2 split. DMP~\cite{devietti2009dmp} makes
multithreaded execution deterministic by constraining the interleaving; the
arithmetic is untouched. Groq's TSP~\cite{abts2022groq} is the closest
fabric-level determinism contract in the literature and a direct precedent
for our framing --- routing is software-scheduled, there is no dynamic
arbitration, and the compiler owns when every packet moves, which is a
shipping demonstration that a compiler/fabric determinism contract is
viable. It is equally the clearest illustration of what schedule determinism
does not buy: the TSP paper makes no bitwise claim, its determinism evidence
is a latency histogram over repeated inferences, and its all-reduce is a
three-stage hierarchical collective keyed to the packaging hierarchy, so the
reduction order is a function of how many chips sit in a node --- Source~1
in full force. Work co-authored at the same vendor lists cross-chip
reproducibility as future work~\cite{shanmugavelu2024}.

The same caution applies to accelerators cited for determinism generally.
The TPU's ``deterministic execution model'' is a statement about
$99$th-percentile response time~\cite{jouppi2017tpu}, and neither that paper
nor Cambricon~\cite{liu2016cambricon} makes a reproducibility claim; where
per-chip determinism is advertised it concerns timing, and chip-to-chip
aggregation remains the collective library's business. Our contribution sits
at that layer: not per-chip determinism, which any accelerator can provide,
but invariance of the chip-to-chip aggregation when the set of chips
changes.

\subsection{NoC Design}

Dally and Towles' text~\cite{dally2004principles} is the foundational
reference for virtual channels, credit flow control and the
deadlock-avoidance discipline our router is built to support;
\S\ref{sec:fabric} states which parts of that discipline the present
compiler discharges. Our NoC is otherwise a textbook design, with one
deliberate choice: topology is a runtime parameter of adjacency and routing
tables, not an RTL artefact, which is what lets a single Verilog
instantiation exercise Claim~\ref{claim:1} across six topologies.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

We enumerate limitations explicitly to set the scope of the work. The last
item is not a limitation of the same kind as the others, and we mark it as
such: it bounds the evaluation \emph{method} rather than the artifact, and it
is the part of this section we would most want a reader building a similar
campaign to take away.

\emph{L0 --- One unbuildable configuration, and why it is a defect rather than
a budget.} Hardware validation covers \TournNInst{} of the \SuiteSize{}
instances. The exception is \sv{maxp\_mod\_fat} ($N{=}8192$, $G{=}4096$,
fat-tree), whose \MaxpAttempts{} attempted testbenches all hit the
$28{,}800$~s wall-clock cap for \MaxpCoreHours{} core-hours and no settled
result --- but wall-clock was not the binding constraint, and we correct an
earlier account that said it was. The instance instantiates \MaxpRouters{}
routers while the adjacency-configuration interface carries a
\RouterIdBits-bit router id (\sv{noc\_system.sv:67--70}) whose top code is the
disconnected sentinel, so routers above \RouterIdMax{} alias onto low ids and
every one of the instance's \MaxpAdjEntries{} adjacency writes touches an
out-of-range id. Replayed through the RTL's own truncation and range guards,
the emitted configuration installs \MaxpLinksInstalled{} links of which
\MaxpLinksCorrect{} match the intended fat-tree and delivers
\MaxpRoutesDelivered{} of \MaxpRoutesSampled{} sampled leaf-to-leaf routes,
all \MaxpRoutesSampled{} of which deliver on the untruncated fabric. No
budget could have completed that configuration, so \MaxpCoreHours{} core-hours
bought a run that could not settle. We have since repaired the truncation ---
the emitter now sizes the router-id field to the instance --- and
re-elaborated, which settles what the earlier account could only assume.
Across \MaxpElabAttempts{} attempts under \MaxpDebugCfgs{} \sv{xelab} debug
configurations, \MaxpElabClean{} ran elaboration to completion and exited zero
reporting the snapshot built --- the larger taking \MaxpElabWall{} at
\MaxpElabRss{}~GiB of peak resident memory and writing \MaxpSnapSize{}~GiB of
snapshot database --- and \MaxpKernels{} of the \MaxpElabAttempts{} wrote a
simulation kernel, so there is nothing for the simulator to load. We do not
assert a root cause: a tool that exits zero having written no output is
misreporting and we have not established why. The exclusion is therefore a
truncation defect in the emitter as shipped and, once that is repaired, a tool
limit at this design point; it is not a wall-clock budget and not a property
of the fabric. The $N{=}8192$ column of
Table~\ref{tab:invariance} rests on the two remaining $(G,\text{topology})$
points.

\emph{L0a --- The denominator, and the runs that are not in it.} We report the
attempted count as well as the passing one. On the \TournNInst{} instances,
\InvAttempted{} distinct ABI-compliant images were attempted, \WallTBs{}
settled and passed, and \InvNonSettling{} did not settle. All but one of the
\InvNonSettling{} are fabric watchdog fail-stops --- their retained per-PE
counters stop at a last retirement plus exactly $\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} =
\WatchdogMax$ cycles, far inside the testbench budget --- and the remaining
one is a simulator wall-clock kill. Non-settling runs are not evidence for
Claim~\ref{claim:1} and we do not count them as such; they are the campaign's
only observation of Property~\ref{prop:2} on unmutated compiler output
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}). Coverage of the configuration space is likewise
narrower than the space we advertise: the \SelConfigsTotal{}
assignment/mapping pairs enumerated on each instance are $0.82\%$ of the
$21{,}960$ $(\asgn,\map,r)$ triples, refinement is fixed at \sv{None} in every
measured configuration, and the remaining axis is covered by the orthogonality
argument of \S\ref{sec:compiler-orth} rather than by measurement. One leaf
vector is evaluated per $N$, and \InvSingletonRows{} of the \InvLeafCounts{}
leaf counts carry a single $(G,\text{topology})$ point, so at those $N$
nothing is witnessed across reshape.

\emph{L0b --- Configuration-load bypass: stated, measured, and withdrawn.}
Route-table initialisation in every run we report is performed by hierarchical
assignment at time zero rather than by the clocked \sv{write\_route()}
protocol, because the latter costs $\mathcal{O}(G^2)$ simulated cycles and
dominates wall-clock at large $G$; every row of every released
hardware-results file carries a \sv{\_BYPASS} error code. An earlier version
of this paper attached a fidelity band to that substitution --- a fraction of
runs bit-exact, the remainder some way lower in cycles --- and, unable to
re-derive it, withdrew the band without putting anything in its place. We have
since measured it. Re-running every passing configuration at $G \le \RouteGMax$
under both paths on the same RTL gives the same \sv{PASS}/\sv{FAIL} verdict, the same
64-bit root and the same cycle count on \RouteRuns{} of \RouteRuns{}
comparable points --- \RouteBitExact{} bit-exact, no exceptions --- and
\RouteBitExact{} again under an alternative rule for choosing among repeated
records (\RouteAltRuns{} points). The only effect of clocked loading is a
deterministic configuration prologue of exactly one clock per route-table
entry (\RouteFillTime{} at our testbench period, with minimum, maximum and
median all equal), which completes before \sv{program\_start} and therefore
enters no reported cycle count. A negative control confirms the clocked path
genuinely exercises routing: corrupting the whole route table flips \sv{PASS}
to \sv{FAIL} on \RouteNegCtrl{} of \RouteNegCtrl{} runs. The uncertainty that
route-table loading contributes to any cycle figure in this paper is therefore
measured, and it is zero. What remains is a scope limit rather than a fidelity
one: the clocked path was exercised up to $G = \RouteGMax$, so the
configuration protocol itself is uncharacterised at the largest $G$ in the
suite.

\emph{L0c --- The FADD is combinational, and we report no timing closure.}
\sv{fp64\_add} is unpipelined: alignment, a 54-bit add, leading-zero
detection, normalisation and rounding all resolve within one cycle. A
stateless adder is what makes the arithmetic a pure function of its operand
pair, which Claim~\ref{claim:1} needs, but it is not a realistic synthesis
target. We have run no synthesis and report no $F_{\max}$, slack or
utilisation, so we make no frequency claim anywhere. Pipelining would change
\emph{when} a \texttt{COMPUTE} releases its scoreboard bit, not which operand
pair it reads: cycle counts would move, the roots in
Table~\ref{tab:invariance} would not. Every cycle figure here belongs to the
one-cycle-ALU model.

\emph{L0d --- One reduction per program.} Scoreboard bits are monotone within
a program and are cleared only at \sv{program\_start}, so the fabric executes
one reduction per reset, which is what our evaluation measures. A workload
running thousands of training steps would need a reset between steps or
epoch-tagged scoreboard bits; the latter does not touch the ABI, but we have
not implemented it and claim no multi-pass throughput numbers.

\emph{L1 --- Research-prototype envelope; no silicon.} All results come from
cycle-accurate RTL simulation. FPGA bring-up wrappers exist for
$G \in \{2,4\}$ but need IP and constraints we do not redistribute, and we
hold no board logs, so no claim in this paper rests on silicon execution and
we make none about timing closure, area or power at production-die scale.

\emph{L1b --- No measured same-fabric overhead at non-power-of-two scale.} We
report no $\eta$: the non-deterministic baselines of \S\ref{sec:method-nd}
were not implemented, so the ABI's cost is priced structurally
(\S\ref{sec:results-overhead}) and anchored only on distance to $\Omega$,
which upper-bounds what any unconstrained reducer could recover. On most of
the suite that is not a gap: \S\ref{sec:method-nd} shows $\eta = 1$ by
construction on the $31$ instances whose $N$ and $G$ are both powers of two,
where the ABI schedule and recursive doubling are the same DAG, so a
measurement there would confirm a theorem rather than test one. The residual
is the $8$ instances whose $N$ or $G$ is not a power of two. There the
isomorphism does not hold, the ABI may cost something we have not priced, and
no baseline was built to price it. An earlier version of this paper called the
missing baseline the largest gap in the evaluation and the first thing it
would close; on the evidence of \S\ref{sec:method-nd} that was an
overstatement, and we withdraw it.

\emph{L2 --- No head-to-head with production collectives.} We do not
benchmark \sprs{} against NCCL, RCCL or oneCCL on GPU silicon; doing so would
conflate the ABI's cost with the cost of a research prototype against
production silicon. The premise that motivates the paper --- that reshaping a
production collective changes its bits --- is measured rather than assumed
(\S\ref{sec:results-premise}), but that measurement is a statement about
reduction order on one two-GPU node and above $G{=}2$ it runs on the CPU
backend; it is not a performance comparison and no number here derives a
timing claim from it. One head-to-head does exist and is reported: the
software export of \S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt} against stock
\texttt{ncclAllReduce} on that node. It prices the export, at $G{=}2$ on
PCIe, and not the fabric, and it is unfavourable.

\emph{L3 --- Synthetic leaf values.} Equation~\eqref{eq:leaves} is a
designed-to-exercise-the-bits distribution, not a workload trace, and it is
strictly positive at every index --- see L13, which is a consequence of that
second property rather than of the first. A workload of real gradients or
partial sums exercises value regimes we do not, especially subnormals, which
we flush.

\emph{L4 --- FTZ+RNE subset, and the deviation that was not intended.}
\S\ref{sec:fadd} documents the intended subset; workloads depending on
subnormals, multiple rounding modes or the IEEE signed-zero rule diverge from
reference at the affected bits. A fourth deviation was not intended: the adder
we first submitted did not round to nearest-even on effective subtraction. It
is repaired and revalidated (\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}) and no number in this
paper moves. We keep the item rather than deleting it because how the defect
escaped an exhaustive campaign is L13's subject and is the more transferable
finding.

\emph{L5 --- What the instance suite cannot support.} The \SuiteSize{}-instance
suite covers the $(N, G, \text{topology})$ space unevenly
(Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}), and three consequences bind every
scale-dependent statement we make. $N$ and $G$ are confounded across the suite
by construction (Spearman $\rho = \SelRhoNG$, $p = \SelRhoNGP$), so no result
here separates the influence of problem size from that of worker count. Of the
\SelPopCells{} populated (size $\times$ topology) cells, \SelThinCells{} hold
two instances or fewer, so no cell-level recommendation is derivable and we
make none. And nothing in the selection-rule analysis survives Holm correction
over the \SelExpTests{} exploratory association tests we ran
(\SelBHSurvivors{} survive Benjamini--Hochberg); we report the unconditional
portfolio precisely because it needs no such association to hold
(\S\ref{sec:selrule}).

\emph{L6 --- The \textsf{C-mem} margin is thin, and past it the failure is
silent.} Peak DMEM occupancy over the validated configurations reaches
\DmemPeakMax{} of a hard \DmemDepth{} slots, a margin of \DmemMargin{}. The
bound is a compile-time obligation, not a fabric check: on a constructed
$\FBT(\DmemCexLeaves)$ instance with $G=\DmemCexWorkers$, peak occupancy is
\DmemCexPeak{} slots, the run terminates normally with no \texttt{P\_ERROR},
and it returns a wrong root --- two \emph{different} wrong roots under two
packet-delivery orders (Remark~\ref{rem:cmem}). Claim~\ref{claim:1} holds only
inside that ceiling, and the campaign came within \DmemMargin{} slots of it.

\emph{L7 --- Head-of-line blocking at large $G$ on low-bisection topologies.}
Store-and-forward with a single FIFO per VC (\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}); wormhole
forwarding is planned.

\emph{L8 --- Validated at one point of a parameterised design.} The fabric
instantiates \NumVcs{} virtual channels per port, two compute-node links and
$k/2$ fat-tree core switches; the compiler uses one of each
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}). Every defect we know of in the RTL is in an unused
half. Results here characterise the parameterisation we exercised, and do not
transfer to a second VC, a second link, or a multi-core-switch fat-tree
without revalidation.

\emph{L9 --- Soft-enforced single-writer.} \textsf{C-sw} is a compile-time
post-condition over the emitted image, not a fabric-level veto on a second
write to a set scoreboard slot (\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}). A one-line RTL
extension would close the gap.

\emph{L10 --- No deadlock-freedom property, and no virtual-channel
discipline.} The emitted routing tables are minimal-distance rather than
dimension-ordered, and the channel-dependency graph built from them is cyclic
on every ring instance (cycle length $G$) and every torus instance (length
\CdgTorusCycLo--\CdgTorusCycHi). No escape channel exists: the compiler
assigns no virtual channel and every packet travels on \sv{VC0}, and the
router's egress buffer is a single FIFO shared across channels, so a second VC
would not be an escape even if one were assigned (\S\ref{sec:noc-route}).
\textsf{C-route} therefore claims destination-correctness and loop-freedom
only. Deadlock is prevented by neither compiler nor fabric; it is converted
into a recorded fail-stop by the watchdog, which is where most of L0a's
non-settling runs come from.

\emph{L11 --- Mutation testing is limited to two classes, and its verdicts are
not all detections.} We inject address substitution and leaf corruption only;
scoreboard-set, forwarding-network and credit-return mutations are untested,
so our figures are a lower bound on the harness's sensitivity. Within the two
classes, oracle non-vacuity rests on \MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of
\MutTotal{} verdicts rather than on all \MutTotal{}: in the remainder the
substituted address is one nobody writes, so the dependent \texttt{COMPUTE}
stalls on the presence gate and the flag is a fail-stop, not a value
comparison (\S\ref{sec:results-invariance}).

\emph{L12 --- No formal proof of Claim~\ref{claim:1}.} Our argument is by code
inspection and dynamic assertion, not mechanised proof; the Alloy/Kami path of
\S\ref{sec:mechanization} is future work.

\emph{L13 --- Two defect classes this evaluation cannot see, and why we report
that rather than patch it quietly.} The items above bound what the campaign
measured. This one bounds what it \emph{could} have measured, and it is a
finding about the method, not an apology for the artifact.

First, the campaign cannot exercise the FADD's effective-subtraction path at
all. Leaf values come from~\eqref{eq:leaves}, which is strictly positive at
every index, so both operands of every FADD in all \WallTBs{} runs carry the
same sign and effective subtraction is never asserted. The defect that
lived on that path (L4) therefore could not have appeared in any number this
paper reports --- not at this
campaign size and not at any other, because the blindness does not lie along
the axis the campaign enumerates: all \CompileTotal{} compilations share one
leaf generator, so exhausting the $(\asgn,\map)$ grid explores none of it.

Second, and closer to the bone, the software oracle against which every one of
the \WallTBs{} roots is compared is not an independent model of IEEE-754
addition. It is a transcription of the same adder
(\sv{sprs\_core.py::\_fp64\_add\_bits}), and it reproduced the same defect bit
for bit. Its agreement with the RTL is therefore true and evidentially empty
on exactly the inputs where the RTL was wrong --- and we can now say how
empty: on the as-submitted pair the two agree on all \FpCtrlVectors{} vectors
of the acceptance test while both disagree with correctly-rounded addition on
\FpCtrlErrors{} of them (\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}). A differential test is only
as strong as the independence of its two sides, and ours were not
independent.

We state these together because the pattern is more useful than either
instance. Both are cases of an evaluation that cannot detect a class of defect
\emph{by construction} --- in the first the stimulus excludes the path, in the
second the reference implements the defect --- and neither is visible from
inside the campaign: no pass rate, coverage figure or additional instance
would have exposed either, and both were found by reading the arithmetic
against an external standard. The transferable lesson, which applies to our
own exhaustiveness argument as much as to anyone's, is that enumerating a
parameter space exhaustively says nothing about the axes that space does not
span, and the two axes that mattered here --- operand sign, and the
independence of the oracle --- were not parameters of the campaign at all. The
discipline we would adopt next time is to state, before the campaign and for
each property under test, which stimulus dimension exercises it and which
reference is independent of the implementation. The adder is repaired and
revalidated in \S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}; the method fix is this item.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have argued that cluster-reshape non-determinism in floating-point
reductions is an \emph{interface} problem rather than a library problem, and
that the place to fix it is the contract between a collective-scheduling
compiler and its fabric. Reduction order, treated as a size-indexed ABI over a
canonical full binary tree, is orthogonal to $G$, topology, assignment,
mapping, refinement and schedule, and yields cross-topology bitwise
invariance: the 64-bit root is a pure function of $N$ and the leaf vector.
What we claim is a conjunction, not a first --- that invariant, enforced at
the compiler/fabric interface with a fail-stop failure mode, across network
topologies rather than resource counts alone, over an unmodified IEEE-754 FP64
operator, for arbitrary non-power-of-two $N$. Six compiler obligations and
nine fabric invariants suffice to argue it: one compiler pass, SU-ordered DMEM
allocation, \emph{constructs} the single-writer discipline the argument needs
rather than assuming it, and three fabric mechanisms --- scoreboard
monotonicity, misroute drop, and the write-arbitration retry latch --- do the
rest. Connectivity repair, which an earlier account of this work called
load-bearing, is available and unused: operand binding is by node identity and
does not care whether a worker's task set is connected
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair}).

We instantiated the ABI as \sprs{}. On \SuiteSize{} instances spanning
$N \in [8,8192]$, $G \in [2,4096]$ and six direct-network topologies, the
campaign enumerated the refinement-free face of the compiler's configuration
space exhaustively and RTL-validated every distinct image it produced: of
\InvAttempted{} attempted on \TournNInst{} hardware-validated instances,
\WallTBs{} settled with a root bit-identical to the canonical one and
\InvNonSettling{} did not settle, all but one a watchdog fail-stop. That face
is $0.82\%$ of the $\approx\!22{,}000$ triples per instance, with refinement
fixed at \sv{None}; the rest is covered by the orthogonality argument, not by
measurement. An injected-fault campaign establishes that the zero-divergence
result is non-vacuous on \MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of \MutTotal{}
verdicts, the remainder being the halt outcome Property~\ref{prop:2} predicts.
We price the ABI structurally rather than against a non-deterministic
baseline, which remains the principal open item in the evaluation.

Two negative results are worth carrying forward alongside the positive one.
The compiler search this campaign was built around is not a ranking problem:
the minimising configuration is a tie set on \ClsTied{} of \TournNInst{}
instances, and what replaces the ranking --- a fixed portfolio of
\SelConfigsRun{} assignment algorithms that reaches the measured optimum on
\SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{} held-out instances, beating every regime map
we could fit over $(N,G,\text{topology})$ --- is both cheaper and better
supported than the distribution it replaces. It also transfers: refitted with
an entire topology withheld and scored only on the topology it never saw, its
mean out-of-sample regret is \SelUnseenTopoMean{} (95\% bootstrap interval
$[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$), lower than that of a rule which
conditions on topology and does see it in training (\SelCondTopoMean{}).
Conditioning on the axis where conditioning looks most obvious is the thing
that loses. And two defect classes in our own
artifact are invisible to this evaluation \emph{by construction}
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L13): we found them by reading the arithmetic, not
by running more configurations. An evaluation that enumerates a space
exhaustively can still be blind along an axis that the space does not span,
and that blindness is not observable from inside the campaign.

The framing suggests a more portable form of the argument: \emph{pinned-FBT},
a software-only export of the ABI discipline onto existing collective
libraries (\S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt}). The construction we published there
is exact only when $N$ and $G$ are both powers of two; the arbitrary-$N$ case
needs the cut-aligned variant given in that section, and both are now measured
on a production collective. The repair is bitwise reshape-invariant --- all
\PinCells{} (case, world size, rank) cells swept returned the canonical root
$\Rcan(N,L)$ --- and the same checker, run against the published construction
as a pre-registered negative control, broke on \PinNegFired{} of
\PinNegCases{} cases. Its cost is unfavourable and the statistic was fixed
before the run: a geometric mean of $\PinCostGeo\times$ stock
\texttt{ncclAllReduce} at $G{=}2$ in FP64 (best $\PinCostBest\times$, worst
$\PinCostWorst\times$), far outside the pre-registered support band and
provisional besides, since the sweep ran under GPU contention. A factor of
about two of it is the algorithmic price of the pinned tree at $G{=}2$ and no
engineering removes it. Reshape reproducibility is therefore available in
software where the collective is not the bottleneck, and is the wrong purchase
inside a bandwidth-critical loop --- which
is what \S\ref{sec:discussion-notuse} predicted before it was priced.
Fabric-level and software-level enforcement discharge the same structural
obligation at very different prices; what makes either one checkable is that
the reduction order is enforced rather than intended.

\section{ISA and Packet Format}\label{app:isa}
% =====================================================================

The instruction word is 64 bits:
\[
  \underbrace{\mathtt{opcode}}_{\text{4 b}}
  \,\|\, \underbrace{\mathtt{aux}}_{\text{4 b}}
  \,\|\, \underbrace{\mathtt{dest}}_{\text{16 b}}
  \,\|\, \underbrace{\mathtt{addr\_a}}_{\text{16 b}}
  \,\|\, \underbrace{\mathtt{addr\_b}}_{\text{16 b}}
  \,\|\, \underbrace{\mathtt{reserved}}_{\text{8 b}}.
\]
The \sv{opcode} field is Table~\ref{tab:isa}. Two source fields are
required: \texttt{COMPUTE} reads $\texttt{DMEM[addr\_a]}$ and
$\texttt{DMEM[addr\_b]}$, which is the operand pair the ABI binds. For
\texttt{SEND}, \sv{dest} is the \emph{destination-side} DMEM address
(\texttt{target\_addr} in the packet), \sv{addr\_a} is the local source, and
\sv{addr\_b} carries the destination PE identifier.

The \sv{aux} field is four bits: $\sv{aux}[3{:}1]$ selects the ALU operation
and $\sv{aux}[0]$ is a legacy link hint. Egress port selection is \emph{not}
an instruction field --- no small fixed field could address a router's egress
ports, which range up to $P = \RadixMax$ across the suite
(Table~\ref{tab:radix}). The port is resolved in hardware by the emitting
router's $\texttt{route\_table}[\sv{dest\_gpu}]$ lookup, which is what makes
topology a configuration input rather than a property of the emitted code.

This 64-bit encoding is what Table~\ref{tab:resource} costs (at the
$\DmemDepth \times 64$ design target; the elaborated depth is per-instance,
\S\ref{sec:resource}) and what the companion document specifies
field-for-field.

The 96-bit packet format is Fig.~\ref{fig:packet}. The
\sv{dest\_node\_id} field is cluster-wide at 16 bits, but the header is not
the binding limit on cluster size: the adjacency-configuration interface
carries a \RouterIdBits-bit router id, so router ids above \RouterIdMax{}
alias silently (\S\ref{sec:resource}, L0).

\section{Worked Invariance Example at $N = 4$}\label{app:worked}
% =====================================================================

We trace a single execution at $N = 4$ on two different topologies to
show the invariance mechanism concretely.

Let $L = \langle 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0\rangle$. The canonical tree
$\FBT(4)$ has internal nodes $\{0,1,2\}$ and leaves $\{3,4,5,6\}$
with $\mathrm{idx}(3)=0, \mathrm{idx}(4)=1, \mathrm{idx}(5)=2,
\mathrm{idx}(6)=3$. The canonical post-order is
$\pi_4^{\text{internal}} = \langle 1, 2, 0\rangle$, so:
\[
\Rcan(4, L) = \fadd(\fadd(1.0, 2.0), \fadd(3.0, 4.0)) = 10.0.
\]

\emph{Configuration A: $G=2$, linear.} $\asgn = \{3\to 0, 4\to 0,
1\to 0, 5\to 1, 6\to 1, 2\to 1, 0\to 0\}$ (PE 0 owns the left subtree,
PE 1 the right subtree and sends its sub-root to PE 0). Emitted
instructions:
\begin{itemize}
\item PE 0: \texttt{GEN(dest=1, val=1.0)}; \texttt{GEN(dest=2, val=2.0)};
  \texttt{COMP(dest=3, addr\_a=1, addr\_b=2)}; receive at addr=4;
  \texttt{COMP(dest=5, addr\_a=3, addr\_b=4)}.
\item PE 1: \texttt{GEN(dest=1, val=3.0)}; \texttt{GEN(dest=2, val=4.0)};
  \texttt{COMP(dest=3, addr\_a=1, addr\_b=2)}; \texttt{SEND(src=3, dest=0, addr=4)}.
\end{itemize}
The operand bindings at PE~0's second \texttt{COMP} are
$(\mathrm{DMEM}_0[3], \mathrm{DMEM}_0[4]) = (\fadd(1.0,2.0),
\fadd(3.0,4.0))$, whose FADD gives $10.0$. \emph{Root result:} $10.0$.

\emph{Configuration B: $G=4$, 2D mesh with PE indices
$\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}$}. $\asgn = \{3\to(0,0), 4\to(0,1),
1\to(0,0), 5\to(1,0), 6\to(1,1), 2\to(1,0), 0\to(0,0)\}$ scatters
every leaf to its own PE; \texttt{SEND}s fan leaves to internal nodes;
\texttt{SEND}s fan internal-node sub-roots to the root. Operand bindings
at internal \texttt{COMP}s are identical by construction: the
\sv{tree.children(v)} function is topology-agnostic.
\emph{Root result:} $10.0$ (the same 64 bits, by Proposition~\ref{thm:orth}).

What changes between A and B: (i) the number and identity of the PEs;
(ii) the number of \texttt{SEND}s; (iii) the cycle count; (iv) which
routers carry which packets. What does not change: the operand pair
bound at each internal \texttt{COMP}, the order in which the
scoreboard-gated adds commit, and therefore the final 64 bits.

\section{Selection-Bias Ablation}\label{app:bias}
% =====================================================================

The campaign simulated every distinct program image
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}). This appendix asks what a cost-constrained
campaign that had instead promoted a top-$K$-by-\sv{makespan} shortlist to
hardware would have missed, since software \sv{makespan} and measured
\sv{hw\_cycles} need not rank configurations alike.

For each HW-validated instance we compute the Spearman correlation between
the software makespan ranking and the measured cycle ranking over all its
configurations, and the \emph{leakage} at cutoff $K$: the fraction of the
true top-$K$-by-cycles that falls outside the top-$K$-by-makespan.

\input{tables/selbias}

The correlation is high ($\rho \approx \TournRhoFifteen$ at the median),
but leakage at the operating point is not negligible: at $K=15$ a median
\TournLeakPctFifteen{} of the genuine top-15 would have been missed by a
makespan-chosen shortlist, and even at the more permissive $K=30$ cutoff
\TournLeakPctThirty{} is still missed. The
practical reading is that software makespan is a good \emph{filter} and a
poor \emph{selector} --- adequate for deciding what to simulate, not for
deciding what won.

None of this touches Claim~\ref{claim:1}, which quantifies over every
HW-validated configuration rather than a selected subset: no shortlist was
applied, and the leakage above is the cost we avoided by not applying one.
