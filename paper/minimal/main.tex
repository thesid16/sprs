\begin{abstract}
Floating-point reductions are not bitwise reproducible under \emph{cluster
reshape}: change the worker count or the network and the collective changes
its reduction order, and with it the last bits. We measure the premise ---
reducing identical input \emph{bytes} at \GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts under
PyTorch's collectives returns \GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct 64-bit roots with
spreads reaching \GpuUlpMax{}~ulp, while repeats at a fixed shape are bitwise
identical. We argue that reduction order is an \emph{interface} concern and
treat it as a compiler/fabric ABI: a size-indexed contract fixing the operand
binding at every internal node of a canonical full binary tree $\FBT(N)$. No
conjunct is ours alone; the conjunction is. The order is fixed at the
interface and enforced in hardware by scoreboard-gated operand binding, so a
violated assumption fail-stops rather than returning a quietly different
number; the result is invariant across network \emph{topologies}, not resource
counts alone; the operator is an unmodified IEEE-754 FP64 addition with no
wide accumulator; and $N$ is arbitrary rather than a power of two. We
instantiate the ABI as \sprs{}, a topology-parametric SystemVerilog NoC plus
compiler, and state two properties --- \emph{cross-topology bitwise
invariance} (Claim~\ref{claim:1}) and \emph{presence-gated fail-stop}
(Property~\ref{prop:2}) --- connected by a mapping/topology orthogonality
proposition. The campaign enumerates the refinement-free face of the
compiler's $\approx\!22{,}000$-point configuration space exhaustively and
RTL-validates every distinct image it produces: of \InvAttempted{} images on
\TournNInst{} of \SuiteSize{} instances spanning $N\!\in\![8,8192]$,
$G\!\in\![2,4096]$ and six topologies, \WallTBs{} settled and every one
produced a root bit-identical to the canonical root; \InvNonSettling{} did not
settle, all but one a fabric watchdog fail-stop, and no run produced a finite
root differing from the canonical one. Injected faults establish oracle
non-vacuity on \MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of \MutTotal{} verdicts. Because
the compiler's search space cannot reach the bit result, we report what it can
reach: a portfolio of \SelConfigsRun{} of \SelConfigsTotal{} configurations
attains the measured optimum on \SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{} held-out
instances and, refitted with an entire topology withheld, has mean
out-of-sample regret \SelUnseenTopoMean{} on the topology it never saw (95\%
bootstrap interval $[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$) --- better than a
rule that conditions on topology and does see it in training
(\SelCondTopoMean{}). Finally we export the discipline to production
collectives in software (\emph{pinned-FBT}), correct the index-range
construction we published, and price the repair: bitwise reshape-invariant on
all \PinCells{} cells swept against a negative control that fires, at a
geometric mean of $\PinCostGeo\times$ stock \texttt{ncclAllReduce}
(provisional, the sweep having run under GPU contention).
\end{abstract}

\section{The Problem}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{P}{roduction} all-reduce implementations --- ring, recursive
halving--doubling, double-binary tree --- pick the reduction order that
maximises throughput for the observed topology and worker
count~\cite{rabenseifner2004optimization,nccl2018}. When either changes, so
does the order. IEEE~754 addition is non-associative, so the order is part of
the answer: for two orders $\sigma,\sigma'$ of the same
multiset~\cite{higham2002accuracy},
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\,{\textstyle\sum_\sigma x_i} - {\textstyle\sum_{\sigma'} x_i}\,\bigr|
  \;\leq\; (N-1)\,u\,{\textstyle\sum_i} |x_i| + \mathcal{O}(u^2),
  \label{eq:higham}
\end{equation}
a bound that is catastrophically loose under cancellation.

The consequence is measured in \S\ref{sec:results-premise}, not asserted:
reducing identical input \emph{bytes} at \GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts under
PyTorch's distributed collectives~\cite{li2020pytorch} --- same data, same
seed, same code, only the shape of the job changing --- returns
\GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct 64-bit results, while repeated runs at any
\emph{fixed} shape are bitwise identical \GpuRepeats{} times out of
\GpuRepeats{}. Reshape moves the answer; noise does not. In a training loop
the divergence compounds: the reduced gradient becomes the next step's
weights. Bit-for-bit regression testing fails the same way --- a code accepted
on a development cluster produces different last bits on the production
cluster, and the reviewer cannot separate a new bug from the reshape
artefact. In regulated deployments, inability to replay a computation bitwise
across hardware generations is increasingly disqualifying.

Two sources produce this. \emph{Order-dependence of the reducer}: NCCL selects
its (algorithm, protocol) pair at communicator initialisation from a cost
model over architecture, node and rank counts and message
size~\cite{nccl2018}; the data are never consulted, so with all randomness
suppressed the algorithm remains a deterministic function of
$(G,\text{topology})$. \emph{Reassociation inside the reducer}: pipeline
overlap, SIMD lanes and message merging are each an implicit reassociation.
Both have been attacked before, along one axis at a time
(\S\ref{sec:related}).

What no line of work states is the invariant \emph{at the interface}, enforced
below the software that could violate it. A library flag cannot forbid
Source~1, because the library has no authority over what cluster size is
launched tomorrow; a fabric guarantee alone cannot forbid Source~2, because
the fabric cannot see the tree a scheduler intends. The discipline has to be a
contract shared by both. The principle is not new --- IEEE~754-2019 already
makes reproducibility an obligation on language processors, forbidding
reordering and fusion under its \emph{reproducible}
attribute~\cite{ieee754-2019} --- but that obligation binds within one
program and says nothing about how many workers run a collective or how they
are wired. We extend it to the interface the standard does not reach, and
index it by problem size so that it survives a change of deployment.

\emph{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)} Reduction order as a size-indexed
compiler/fabric ABI, with a structural invariance argument
(\S\ref{sec:abi}): Claim~\ref{claim:1} (cross-topology bitwise invariance)
and Property~\ref{prop:2} (presence-gated fail-stop), connected by
Proposition~\ref{thm:orth}. We claim the framing, not the difficulty --- once
operand binding is a function of tree-node identity alone, invariance follows
by unwinding definitions, and we say so rather than dressing it as a
discovery. \textbf{(C2)} A fabric (\S\ref{sec:fabric}) and compiler
(\S\ref{sec:compiler}) that jointly realise it, in which topology is a runtime
table rather than an RTL artefact and one compiler pass \emph{constructs} the
single-writer discipline the argument needs. \textbf{(C3)} A hardware campaign
at scale and a structural pricing of the ABI (\S\S\ref{sec:method},
\ref{sec:results}), plus a software export onto production collectives
(\S\ref{sec:pinfbt}). What is \emph{not} claimed: interoperability with any
external reducer, strict IEEE~754 (\S\ref{sec:fadd}), silicon timing, or novel
component algorithms --- our eighteen assignment and ten mapping algorithms
are prior art~\cite{topcuoglu2002heft,lukes1974efficient,dally2004principles};
the observation is that any choice among them inherits invariance by
composition.

\section{The Reduction ABI}\label{sec:abi}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The Canonical Tree}\label{sec:abi-tree}

\begin{definition}[Canonical full binary tree]\label{def:fbt}
For $N \geq 1$, $\FBT(N)$ is the heap-indexed binary tree with $2N-1$ nodes:
root at index $0$, children of $i$ at $2i+1$ and $2i+2$; internal nodes
$\{0,\ldots,N-2\}$, leaves $\{N-1,\ldots,2N-2\}$. Since $2N-1$ is odd, every
internal node has exactly two children: the single-child edge case is
eliminated at the tree level.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical leaf enumeration]\label{def:leafenum}
$\mathrm{idx}$ assigns index $k$ to the $k$-th smallest \emph{heap index} in
the leaf set of $\FBT(N)$.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:leafenum} needs stating precisely because the obvious
paraphrase is wrong. Ascending heap index and left-to-right \emph{visual}
order coincide exactly when $N$ is a power of two. Otherwise $\FBT(N)$ is
depth-imbalanced and a shallower leaf carries a \emph{smaller} heap index than
deeper leaves to its left: for $N=7$ the heap order is
$\langle 6,\ldots,12\rangle$ while visual order is
$\langle 7,\ldots,12,6\rangle$. Seven of the sixteen leaf counts we evaluate
are of this shape, and at $N=200$ the two enumerations give roots differing by
one ulp. An implementation using visual order implements a \emph{different}
contract.

Write $\pi_N$ for the post-order traversal of $\FBT(N)$ restricted to internal
nodes --- a pure function of $N$; $\pi_4 = \langle 1,2,0\rangle$. The
canonical evaluation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rcan}
  \Rcan(N, L) \;=\; \mathrm{evaluate}\bigl(\FBT(N), L, \fadd\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $L$ indexed by $\mathrm{idx}$ and $\fadd$ the combinational semantics of
our FP64 adder (\S\ref{sec:fadd}). Equation~\eqref{eq:Rcan} is the
\emph{target} of every \sprs{} execution on $(N,L)$.

\emph{The canonical order is a constraint, not a tautology.} One might suspect
that any association order yields one root per $N$, so agreement across
topologies measures nothing. We measured how far that is from true. On the
leaf vectors evaluated here, right-to-left sequential summation differs from
$\Rcan$ by up to \OrderUlpMax{} ulp at $N = \OrderUlpMaxN$, and left-to-right
differs at \OrderLRDiff{} of the sixteen leaf counts. The discriminating power
is strongly $N$-dependent and not monotone: exhaustively at $N=8$, all
\OrderPermsEight{} leaf permutations yield only \OrderRootsEight{} distinct
roots, \OrderCanonPctEight{} of them the canonical one, so a uniformly random
leaf-order violation is detected with probability \OrderDetectEight{}; at
$N=16$, all \OrderShapesSixteen{} in-order tree shapes yield
\OrderRootsSixteen{} distinct roots spanning two ulp. Across the sixteen leaf
counts the probability that a sampled reassociation moves the root ranges from
\OrderProbMin{} to \OrderProbMax{}. Two consequences follow and we adopt both:
the oracle is non-vacuous, so an agreeing run is evidence; and the rows of the
invariance table are not equally informative --- at small $N$ agreement is
close to free, and the weight of the evidence sits at the large leaf counts.

\subsection{The ABI}\label{sec:abi-def}

\begin{definition}[Operand binding]\label{def:opbind}
For every internal node $v$ of $\FBT(N)$ the compiler emits exactly one
\texttt{COMPUTE} on the PE hosting $v$, with
$\mathtt{addr\_a} = \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{left}(v))$ and
$\mathtt{addr\_b} = \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{right}(v))$, where
$\mathrm{dmem}(\cdot)$ is a per-PE address assigned by the allocator. The
binding is a pure function of $v$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Leaf binding]\label{def:leafbind}
For every leaf $k$, the \texttt{GENERATE} on the PE hosting $k$ writes
$L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ into the DMEM slot bound to $k$.
\end{definition}

The fabric executes the two disciplines symmetrically: \texttt{COMPUTE} reads
both DMEM slots at one clock edge, scoreboard-gated, and writes
$\fadd(\cdot,\cdot)$; \texttt{GENERATE} delivers $L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ with the
scoreboard set. Neither discipline references $G$, topology, assignment
$\asgn$, mapping $\map$, refinement $r$, or timing. These are left free
precisely so that schedulers may keep optimising without touching the
contract, and they are what the campaign of \S\ref{sec:method} searches over.

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:abi-assumptions}

Fifteen assumptions carry the two properties: five \emph{compiler obligations}
(\textsf{C-*}), discharged by the compiler or the user, and ten \emph{fabric
invariants} (\textsf{F-*}), established by RTL inspection. Eleven are
hypotheses of Claim~\ref{claim:1}; the four marked~$\dagger$ are liveness
invariants that can stop a run but cannot alter a root the fabric does
produce, and are the hypotheses of Property~\ref{prop:2} instead.
Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions} states all fifteen. The partition is not
cosmetic: the \textsf{C-*} obligations are post-conditions on the emitted
image, checkable without executing anything, while the \textsf{F-*} invariants
are properties of the fabric independent of any compilation --- so a failure
of Claim~\ref{claim:1} is diagnosable as a compiler bug or an RTL bug, not
both.

Three conditions a reader might expect are absent. \emph{Compilation
determinism} is not a hypothesis: Claim~\ref{claim:1} quantifies over every
image the compiler can emit, and a statement true of all of them does not need
the compiler to be a function of its seed. \emph{Partition connectedness} is
not either, although \DisconnCount{} of the RTL-validated configurations
(\DisconnShare{}) have partitions that are not connected subtrees: operand
binding is by node identity and every cross-PE child gets a dedicated receive
slot, so the emitted binding is $(\mathrm{left}(v),\mathrm{right}(v))$ for
\emph{any} assignment --- checked statically on \BindComputes{}
\texttt{COMPUTE} and \BindSends{} \texttt{SEND} instructions across
\BindConfigs{} configurations, with \BindViolations{} violations. What
disconnection costs is DMEM occupancy, and that is what \textsf{C-mem} bounds.
The \emph{identity slot} \texttt{DMEM[0]} is reserved but unread here, since
$\FBT(N)$ has no single-child internal node.

\subsection{Claim 1 and the Fail-Stop Property}\label{sec:abi-claim}

\begin{claim}[Cross-topology bitwise invariance]\label{claim:1}
Let $N \in \mathbb{N}$, let $L \in (\mathrm{bits}_{64})^N$ be a fixed
leaf-value vector, and let $\T$ be the family of flat direct-network
topologies realisable by adjacency tables consistent with
\sv{noc\_system.sv}. Assume \textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-leaf},
\textsf{C-mem} and \textsf{F-sb-mono}, \textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw},
\textsf{F-inorder}, \textsf{F-gci-haz}, \textsf{F-arb}, \textsf{F-fadd}. Let
$G$ be a worker count and $\tau \in \T$ a topology for which the compiler
emits an image satisfying \textsf{C-mem}; let $\asgn$, $\map$, $r$ be any
valid assignment, mapping and refinement, and $s$ the emitted per-PE schedule.
If the run terminates normally --- \sv{all\_done} asserted, no
\texttt{P\_ERROR}, no watchdog --- then the observed 64-bit root, the result
of the root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE}, satisfies
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(N, L, G, \tau, \asgn, \map, r, s)
    \;=\; \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{claim}

Three features of the statement bind a reimplementer. \emph{First},
\textsf{C-mem} is a compilability precondition, not a decoration: the claim
asserts nothing about a configuration whose image exceeds the per-PE DMEM
budget or whose router count exceeds what the adjacency interface can address.
One instance in our suite is in that position, and it is also the one that
never settles (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). \emph{Second}, the claim is
partial-correctness; liveness is Property~\ref{prop:2}'s business, which is
exactly why \textsf{C-route}, \textsf{F-credit}, \textsf{F-misroute} and
\textsf{F-watchdog} are not hypotheses here. \emph{Third}, the observable is
the root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE} result, not the root DMEM slot, which no
emitted instruction ever reads back.

\begin{property}[Presence-gated fail-stop]\label{prop:2}
Suppose an assumption violation causes some \texttt{COMPUTE} to name a DMEM
address whose presence bit is never set --- a misrouted and dropped packet
(\textsf{F-misroute}), a lost credit (\textsf{F-credit}), an unreachable
destination (\textsf{C-route}), or an operand bound to a slot no agent writes
(\textsf{C-bind}). Then no instruction retires on the affected PE,
\textsf{F-watchdog} fires after $\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = \WatchdogMax$
consecutive non-retiring cycles, and the PE enters \texttt{P\_ERROR} recording
\sv{error\_pc} and \sv{error\_state}; \sv{all\_done} is never asserted and no
root is produced. An invalid opcode raises \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately.
\end{property}

The converse bound is equally explicit, because the property is easy to
over-read. The fabric checks presence, not identity: a \texttt{COMPUTE} whose
operands are both present executes, whatever they contain. Violations leaving
every read address present --- an operand misbound to a live slot, a corrupted
leaf value, a duplicate write to a receive buffer, an over-budget image in
which two addresses alias onto one written slot, or a violation of any of the
seven safety \textsf{F-*} invariants --- terminate normally with a wrong root.
The fabric does not detect these and we do not claim it does; they are
discharged by the compile-time post-condition on the emitted image and by
comparison of the final root against the oracle.

\emph{How the hypotheses carry the claim.} Four steps, each of the eleven
used at least once. \emph{Addresses} (\textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-mem}):
\sv{build\_schedule} binds a \texttt{COMPUTE}'s operands from an address map
keyed on node identity, and every cross-PE child holds a dedicated receive
slot, so the emitted binding is $(\mathrm{left}(v),\mathrm{right}(v))$ under
any assignment; \textsf{C-mem} is what promotes that address-level statement
to a value-level one, since the fabric truncates addresses and distinct
addresses stay distinct only while the image fits DMEM. \emph{Uniqueness}
(\textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{F-sb-mono}): $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, so each node has one
consumer; the allocator therefore gives each receive buffer exactly one
writer, and its scoreboard bit transitions $0\to1$ exactly once per program.
\emph{Values} (\textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw}, \textsf{F-inorder},
\textsf{F-gci-haz}, \textsf{F-arb}): every address a \texttt{COMPUTE} reads is
a receive buffer or a local temporary. For the first, \textsf{F-sb-gate} holds
the instruction in \textsf{DE} until both bits are set, so at the EX edge both
slots hold their committed remote writes, with \textsf{F-fw} closing the
BRAM read-first race. For the second the scoreboard is silent --- a recycled
slot's bit is already set --- and need not speak: under \textsf{F-inorder} the
overwriting \texttt{COMPUTE} samples its operands strictly before writeback,
and the one writer program order does not sequence, an in-flight
\texttt{GENERATE}, is covered by \textsf{F-gci-haz} and its retry latch
\textsf{F-arb}. \emph{Function} (\textsf{F-fadd}): the committed value is
$\fadd$ of those stable inputs, a pure function. Composing, every internal
DMEM value at termination is $\fadd$ applied to $v$'s two children --- with
leaves supplied by \textsf{C-leaf} --- so the root PE's final \texttt{COMPUTE}
returns $\mathrm{evaluate}(\FBT(N), L, \fadd) = \Rcan(N,L)$, in which no free
axis appears.

We label these a \emph{claim} and a \emph{property} rather than theorems
because the supporting argument proceeds by RTL inspection, a compiler
post-condition, end-to-end root comparison and mutation testing, not by formal
proof (\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L12).

\subsection{Mapping/Topology Orthogonality}\label{sec:abi-orth}

Of the axes Claim~\ref{claim:1} quantifies over, $(G,\asgn)$ is where the
substantive work lives: changing $G$ changes the number of PEs and changing
$\asgn$ changes which PE hosts which node. The $(\text{topology},\map,\rho,s)$
slice admits a sharper statement.

\begin{proposition}[Mapping/topology orthogonality]\label{thm:orth}
Fix $N$, $L$, $\asgn$, $r$ and the FADD unit. For any two valid mappings
$\map_1,\map_2$ and any two valid routing tables $\rho_1,\rho_2$ for
(possibly different) topologies in $\T$,
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_1, \rho_1) = R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_2, \rho_2)
  = \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{structuralargument}
By inspection of \sv{build\_schedule} and \sv{generate\_instructions}: a
\texttt{COMPUTE}'s $(\texttt{addr\_a},\texttt{addr\_b},\texttt{dest})$ triple
depends only on $\asgn$ and the DMEM allocator's output, neither of which
reads $\map$ or the routing tables. A \texttt{SEND}'s
$(\texttt{dest\_gpu},\texttt{target\_addr},\text{link})$ triple does depend on
$\map$ and on routing, but those determine only \emph{where} data flows, not
the operand binding at the receiving PE. Under \textsf{F-sb-gate} the
receiving \texttt{COMPUTE} reads DMEM by address, not by arrival time, port or
VC. Hence $R_\mathrm{observed}$ is independent of $(\map,\rho)$.
\end{structuralargument}

Proposition~\ref{thm:orth} is the cleanest statement of what the ABI buys: the
two things that change most obviously under cluster reshape are structurally
orthogonal to the bit result. This is a consequence of the code path that
emits the instructions, not a hypothesis to be validated empirically.

\subsection{FADD Policy}\label{sec:fadd}

Our adder (\sv{fp64\_add.sv}, with a bit-exact Python mirror) implements a
\emph{deterministic subset} of IEEE~754-2019 binary64 addition, which we
specify fully as \emph{FTZ+RNE}: subnormal inputs flushed to $\pm0$ preserving
sign; round-to-nearest-even only, with no rounding-mode register; post-round
subnormals flushed likewise; $x+(-x) = +0$ and $(-0)+(-0) = +0$ (a documented
deviation from \S6.3 of the standard); a quieted qNaN whose sign and payload
are taken from $a$ when $a$ is NaN and from $b$ otherwise; and the usual
infinity rules with $\pm\infty + \mp\infty$ returning the canonical qNaN.
Invariance depends only on $\fadd$ being a pure function of its inputs, not on
any particular IEEE~754 feature; a subnormal-aware alternative inherits
Claim~\ref{claim:1} by construction. The subset is checked rather than
asserted: the shipped adder shows \FpSweepErrors{} disagreements with
correctly-rounded IEEE~754 addition over \FpSweepVectors{} vectors inside the
documented scope. The adder we first submitted did not meet the RNE clause on
effective subtraction; \S\ref{sec:limitations} (L13) reports why neither the
campaign nor the mirror could have found that, which is a finding about the
evaluation method rather than an erratum.

\section{The Fabric}\label{sec:fabric}
% =====================================================================

The fabric realises the \textsf{F-*} invariants. It supports up to $4096$ PEs
in a single Verilog instantiation, and the guiding principle is that
\emph{topology is data, not RTL}.

\emph{Topology as a runtime parameter.} Two compiler-loaded per-router tables
parameterise the network: an \emph{adjacency table} mapping
$(\text{router},\text{port}) \to (\text{target router},\text{target port})
\mid \sv{ADJ\_DISCONNECTED}$, and a \emph{routing table} mapping
$(\text{router},\text{destination}) \to \text{output port}$. A single
\sv{noc\_system} module supports the whole family $\T$; port~0 is reserved for
the local network interface on every router, the one structural convention
independent of topology. Adding a topology requires a Python classmethod that
emits the two tables and no Verilog change: the compiler provides six families
(linear, ring, 2D mesh, 2D torus, fat-tree, hypercube) with $O(1)$ analytical
shortest paths and a BFS fallback. The binding ceiling is the width of the
configuration interface rather than a design parameter --- adjacency writes
carry a \RouterIdBits-bit router id whose top code is the disconnected
sentinel, so ids are addressable to \RouterIdMax{} and a configuration above
that aliases silently. That is exactly what defeats one instance of the suite
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L0). Router radix is likewise per-instance
(\sv{PORTS\_PER\_RTR}), ranging $\RadixMin$--$\RadixMax$ across the suite;
since the crossbar term is $O(P^2)$, a fat-tree router is
\RouterBlowup$\times$ a mesh router (\RouterBitsFt{} against \RouterBitsLo{}
storage-equivalent bits, \XbarShareFt{} of it crossbar), while per-PE memory
is topology-independent at $\DmemDepth \times 64$ DMEM, \PerPEBits{} bits per
PE. These are RTL-level counts from a research prototype, not synthesis
results; we report no timing closure anywhere.

\emph{ISA and pipeline.} Four opcodes are live: \texttt{NOP};
\texttt{GENERATE}, which fetches a leaf into \texttt{DMEM[dest]} through the
GCI interface and sets \texttt{scoreboard[dest]}; \texttt{COMPUTE}, which
reads \texttt{DMEM[addr\_a]} and \texttt{DMEM[addr\_b]} scoreboard-gated,
writes $\fadd(a,b)$ to \texttt{DMEM[dest]} and sets its bit; and
\texttt{SEND}, which enqueues an RDMA-push packet carrying
\texttt{DMEM[src]}. Opcodes beyond one reserved slot trap to
\texttt{P\_ERROR}. Each PE runs a three-stage pipeline --- \textsf{IF}
(registered IMEM read, decode), \textsf{DE} (scoreboard hazard check, DMEM
read issue), \textsf{EX} (combinational ALU, writeback or TX enqueue) --- with
two-level write-to-read forwarding. The ISA is small enough that the
determinism-critical mechanisms fit in a two-page RTL inspection, which is the
point: the scoreboard is the single synchronisation point across the three
agents that write DMEM. Packets are 96-bit single-flit and carry one FP64
value; the VC-steering bit is written as a literal \VcBitLiteral{} by the only
packet-forming assignment in the design, so every packet travels on \sv{VC0}
and cycle counts are value-agnostic by construction.

\emph{Two mechanisms that are necessary for the value, not merely for
liveness.} First, the DMEM write arbiter is strict-priority
$\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} > \mathrm{FSM}$ with a GCI \emph{retry latch}:
when a GCI completion collides with a PIU write, the arbiter drops the GCI
write and the latch re-asserts it until accepted. Without the latch the leaf
value would be silently lost while PIU still set the scoreboard bit, and the
next \texttt{COMPUTE} would read a wrong leaf and produce a wrong root with no
diagnostic. Second, the in-flight \texttt{GENERATE} interlock stalls any
\texttt{COMPUTE} or \texttt{SEND} naming a pending destination. The RTL
comment calls it belt-and-braces with the scoreboard; it is not.
\GenRecycledPct{} of \texttt{GENERATE}s (\GenRecycled{} of \GenTotal{}) target
a \emph{recycled} slot whose scoreboard bit is already set by the previous
writer, so the scoreboard cannot stall their consumer and the interlock is
their sole protection.

\emph{Fail-stop discipline.} On each drained packet the PIU compares the
header's \sv{dest\_gpu\_id} against the local PE; on mismatch it drops the
packet and returns credit rather than writing it (\textsf{F-misroute}). A bad
routing table therefore leaves the destination's scoreboard bit clear, its
dependent \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls, and the watchdog raises \texttt{P\_ERROR}
after \WatchdogMax{} cycles. Silent corruption is converted into a liveness
failure with a recorded program counter --- which is Property~\ref{prop:2}.

\section{The Compiler}\label{sec:compiler}
% =====================================================================

\sprs{} compiles $(N, G, \text{topology})$ into per-PE IMEM images plus
routing and adjacency tables. Every image reported here was produced by
\StagesRun{} stages: the composite lower bound $\Omega$ (S0, a reported metric
rather than a construction step, \S\ref{sec:method}); $\FBT(N)$ instantiation
(S1); assignment $\asgn$ of tree nodes to workers, from 18 algorithms (S2);
mapping $\map$ of workers to physical PEs, from 10 (S3); and code emission
with cycle-accurate NOP padding (S6). Three further passes exist in the
released compiler and produced none of these results: connectivity repair
(S2.5), memory enforcement (S5), and refinement (S4, twelve base methods
chainable to $122$ configurations), fixed at the identity throughout.

\emph{One pass is load-bearing.} The DMEM allocator walks the
Sethi--Ullman-refined post-order. It reserves address $0$ on every PE; at each
node $v$ on PE $p=\asgn(v)$ it returns to $p$'s free list the slot of every
child of $v$ already resident on $p$, then gives $v$ the head of that list or
else a fresh address. A final pass gives every cross-PE edge a persistent
receive-buffer slot on the parent's PE, above that PE's local high-water mark,
never freed. Three properties of that walk \emph{construct} \textsf{C-sw}
rather than assume it: $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, so every node has exactly one
consumer; post-order visits children before the parent, so a local slot is
freed only after its consumer has read it; and receive slots live in a
separate never-freed pass, because a scoreboard-gated \texttt{COMPUTE} needs
its address stable. An allocator working in pre-order, or freeing before the
consumer ran, would break the discipline. The same walk yields \textsf{C-mem}
free: peak local occupancy plus receive slots is exactly the number of
distinct addresses the image names on $p$, so the capacity obligation is an
$O(N)$ read-off of a quantity the allocator already maintains.

\emph{The repair pass that looks load-bearing is not.} Twelve of the eighteen
assignment algorithms can hand a PE a node set that is not a connected
subtree, and \DisconnCount{} of the \WallTBs{} validated configurations are of
that kind. Operand binding is unaffected (\S\ref{sec:abi-assumptions}); what
disconnection costs is DMEM. Over \BindConfigs{} configurations, peak per-PE
occupancy has median \DmemMedConn{} slots on the \DmemConnConfigs{} connected
partitions and median \DmemMedDisc{} (max \DmemPeakMax) on the
\DmemDiscConfigs{} disconnected ones, against the \DmemDepth-entry budget --- a
tail margin of \DmemMargin{} slots. Running \sv{repair\_connectivity} removes
the pressure and destroys the load balance the assignment search exists to
produce: it moves a median \RepairMovedMed{} of all tree nodes, inflates peak
per-PE node count by a median factor of \RepairLoadMed{} and up to
\RepairLoadMax$\times$, and leaves \RepairImemOver{} of the
\DmemDiscConfigs{} with more than \DmemDepth{} instructions on a single PE
against exactly one before repair. It is not even monotone in the quantity it
is invoked for: in \RepairDmemUp{} of them it \emph{raises} peak DMEM.
Connectedness is a correlate of memory pressure, not a guarantee of it, so we
report the unrepaired pipeline and check the bound directly.

\emph{Mapping barely reaches the image.} \texttt{SEND} carries the logical
worker id and IMEM is loaded by logical index, so $\map$ reaches the emitted
image only through the hop counts the cost model uses to place NOPs:
recompiling \MapPoints{} representative points under all ten mappings yields
\MapImagesLo{}--\MapImagesHi{} distinct images, differing only in NOP padding.
Invariance across S3 is therefore close to tautological, and the evidential
weight of the campaign sits on the assignment axis and on reshape across
$(G,\text{topology})$.

\emph{ABI verification.} \textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{C-bind} and \textsf{C-leaf}
are post-conditions on the emitted IMEM, checkable without executing anything.
The campaign path did not run them in the compile loop, so we discharged them
afterwards over the released images --- \BindComputes{} \texttt{COMPUTE} and
\BindSends{} \texttt{SEND} instructions across \BindConfigs{} configurations,
\BindViolations{} violations, no out-of-range address, no receive buffer with
two writers. We report it as a static audit of the artifact rather than as a
compiler feature, because that is what was run.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

\emph{Instance suite.} \SuiteSize{} instances span $N \in \{8, 10, 16, 32,
50, 100, 128, 200, 500, 512, 1024, 1500, 2048, 3000, 4096, 8192\}$,
$G \in \{2,\ldots,4096\}$, and the six topologies. Multiple
$(G,\text{topology})$ points at the same $N$ are what exercises the invariance
claim. Leaf values are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaves}
L[i] = (i+1)\cdot 1000.0 + \bigl((i \cdot 37 + 13) \bmod 100\bigr)/100 + 0.007,
\end{equation}
chosen so every leaf carries a non-trivial fractional part: each FADD
exercises the guard, round and sticky bits rather than reducing to an exact
power-of-two addition, and magnitudes span $10^3$ to $8\times10^6$ so partial
sums accumulate over a wide exponent range. We do not claim reordering
\emph{always} moves the result --- it does not, which is what the mutation
protocol below must work around. What the distribution buys is that a
fabric/oracle mismatch is a determinism violation rather than an artefact of
degenerate inputs.

\emph{Campaign protocol.} The per-instance configuration space is
$18\times10\times122 = 21{,}960$ $(\asgn,\map,r)$ triples; simulating it
exhaustively would be ${\approx}880{,}000$ xsim runs. We fix refinement at the
identity and enumerate the remaining $18\times10$ face \emph{exhaustively} on
every instance --- \SelConfigsTotal{} triples per instance, $0.82\%$ of the
space, $7{,}200$ compilations of which $6{,}777$ emit an image. Distinct
$(\asgn,\map)$ pairs frequently produce identical images up to PE relabelling;
a SHA-256 hash over the emitted testbench collapses the $6{,}777$ to $2{,}920$
distinct ones. That is not merely a compute saving: identical images
\emph{tautologically} produce identical bit results, so dedup is what makes
each remaining run informative. Every distinct image was then queued for xsim
--- no shortlist of any kind stands between the compiler and the invariance
evidence. A top-$K$-by-\sv{makespan} shortlist would have missed a median
\TournLeakPctFifteen{} of the true top-$K$ by measured cycles at $K = 15$,
which is why we did not use one.

\emph{Mutation protocol.} Two classes are injected per instance.
\emph{Address substitution} repoints \texttt{addr\_a} of one randomly chosen
\texttt{COMPUTE} at a different DMEM address. \emph{Leaf corruption} perturbs
one leaf by the smallest single-bit flip that provably moves the canonical
root. The escalation in the second is necessary and is the same phenomenon the
paper is about: flipping the mantissa LSB frequently falls below the round bit
of the running sum, RNE discards it, and a fabric reporting \sv{PASS} is
correct to do so. We raise the flipped bit until the oracle confirms the root
has moved, so every counted mutant tests detection rather than rounding. We
deliberately do not use operand swap: $\fadd$ is commutative at the bit level
under FTZ+RNE, so it would be bit-identical and would inflate the
null-detection rate.

\emph{Metrics.} \sv{hw\_cycles} $= \max_p \sv{perf\_total\_cycles}[p]$ from
per-PE counters is primary; the cost model's \sv{makespan} is a diagnostic
only. We also compute a composite lower bound $\Omega$ on \emph{any} schedule
on this fabric,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
\begin{split}
\Omega \,=\, \max \bigl(
    & \text{lb}_{\text{span}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{comm}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{gather}}, \;
      \text{lb}_{\text{bis}}, \\
    & \min_{K \in [K_{\min}, K_{\max}]}
      \max \bigl( \text{lb}_{\text{gen}}(K), \, \text{lb}_{\text{instr}}(K) \bigr)
\bigr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of PEs a schedule populates,
$K_{\max} = \min(G, 2N-1)$. The terms are the zero-communication critical
path, GCI serialisation, instruction throughput, a cross-PE hop floor, the
root PE's gather path, and link serialisation at the bisection. Two of the six
shrink as work spreads over more PEs, so a bound valid against \emph{every}
schedule must take the most favourable $K$; a maximum there would assert a
bound some legal schedule beats. Because $\Omega$ bounds non-deterministic
schedules too, it is not a cost-of-determinism denominator but an
absolute-performance diagnostic. Summary statistics are medians with observed
ranges rather than interval estimates: the per-instance samples are not draws
from a population, they enumerate a deterministic configuration space, so a
resampling interval would describe our sampling of that space rather than any
underlying uncertainty.

\emph{The baseline we did not build.} The natural metric is the same-fabric
overhead $\eta$ against the best non-deterministic reducer on the identical
substrate, obtained by relaxing \textsf{C-bind}. \textbf{We did not build it
and report no measured $\eta$}, and we decline to substitute an analytical

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:results}
% =====================================================================

Vivado xsim 2025.2 on Linux; ten SystemVerilog sources ($3{,}738$ lines); a
72-thread Intel Xeon~w9-3475X with 503~GB of RAM; RNG seeds pinned at $42$;
per-simulation wall-clock cap $28{,}800$~s. Every cycle figure comes from a
single campaign of \WallTBs{} passing runs consuming \WallTotalH{} core-hours
at a median of \WallMedianS{}~s per testbench, the five largest instances
accounting for \WallGiantShare{} of that and the longest single run taking
\WallMaxH{}~h. An earlier campaign over the same testbenches is used for no
reported number: it recorded PE~0's counter rather than the maximum across
PEs, which understates end-to-end latency whenever the root lands elsewhere.
That defect moved \ClsFragilePct{} of cycle counts and changed the
\emph{minimising configuration set} on \ClsFragileChanged{} of \TournNInst{}
instances, leaving it disjoint from the corrected set on
\ClsFragileDisjoint{}, so the measurements were repeated in full rather than
corrected in place. Hardware validation covers \TournNInst{} of the
\SuiteSize{} instances (\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L0).

\subsection{The Premise, Measured}\label{sec:results-premise}

The ABI is worth its cost only if reshaping a production collective really
does move the bits. On \GpuHardware{} we reduced identical input \emph{bytes}
at \GpuWorldSizes{} rank counts from $\GpuWorldLo$ to $\GpuWorldHi$ under
PyTorch~\GpuStack{}'s distributed collectives~\cite{li2020pytorch}, holding
data, seed and code fixed so that only the shape of the job changed. Leaf $i$'s
value is a pure function of $i$, so reshaping alters who holds which leaf and
in what order the partial sums combine, and nothing else.

The \GpuWorldSizes{} configurations returned \GpuRootsDistinct{} distinct
64-bit results in every one of \GpuCases{} (leaf count, precision,
value-profile) cases; in the \GpuCanonCases{} \sv{fp64} cases, for which the
single-process canonical reduction was also computed, \GpuEqCanonical{} of the
\GpuWorldSizes{} matched it. Spreads reach \GpuUlpMax{}~ulp and a maximum
relative spread of $\GpuRelMax$; at least \GpuElemsMovedMin{} of the
\GpuElemsTotal{} elements of the result vector move in every case; pooled over
every configuration swept, \GpuPairDblPct{} of \sv{fp64} configuration pairs
and \GpuPairSglPct{} of \sv{fp32} pairs disagree. Repeated runs at any
\emph{fixed} configuration were bitwise identical \GpuRepeats{} times out of
\GpuRepeats{}, with no disagreement between ranks inside a run, and an
independent re-run under a different bookkeeping harness reproduced
\GpuXCheck{} of \GpuXCheck{} common cells bit for bit. Reshape moves the
answer; noise does not.

Three scope conditions belong with the measurement rather than under it. The
rank-count sweep above $G{=}2$ runs on the CPU backend, because NCCL requires
one rank per device and this node carries two GPUs. At $G{=}2$ the NCCL
algorithm-by-protocol axis (\GpuLibCfgs{} configurations) and the
channel-count axis (\GpuChanCfgs{}) each returned a single root; that is
structural rather than an empirical null --- at two ranks the cross-rank
reduction is one addition per element and no schedule can reassociate it ---
and we do not generalise it to larger $G$. At that same partition the CPU and
GPU roots differ in all \GpuCases{} cases, so device placement moves the bits
as well as partitioning does. This measures reduction order on one node; it is
not a benchmark of any library and no timing claim is drawn from it.

supports. Across the \InvAttempted{} distinct ABI-compliant program images

anyway, so agreement there is close to free and the weight of the evidence
sits at the large-$N$ rows.

\emph{Beyond the measured range.} Invariance at untested $G$ rests on the
structural argument, not on induction: operand binding and scoreboard gating
remain total functions of $N$ and $L$ as $G$ grows, so adding PEs changes the
\emph{location} of FADD operations, not the operand pairs they receive. Adding
a PE does introduce new routing paths, VC contention and credit interactions
that may move cycle counts. What carries is the bit result; absolute cycle
counts at large $G$ are simulation artefacts, not silicon measurements, and we
make no absolute competitiveness claim. Indeed we make no silicon claim at
all: we ship Artix-7 bring-up wrappers for $G \in \{2,4\}$, but synthesising
them needs a Vivado VIO IP core and board constraints we do not redistribute,
and we hold no board logs. That is a reporting gap rather than a soundness
gap --- invariance is a statement about bit values, which xsim resolves
exactly.

\subsection{The Oracle Is Not Vacuous, and Where It Stops}

A zero-divergence column means nothing unless the oracle can report a non-zero
one. Reporting the injected-fault campaign requires more care than a single
rate. The harness retains one boolean per mutant, and the testbench prints
\sv{[FAIL]} both on a value mismatch and from its \sv{!all\_done} branch on
any run that fails to complete; the two are not distinguishable in the
recorded output. Reconstructing the mutations against regenerated images
separates them. Of the \MutVerdicts{} address-substitution verdicts, in at
least \MutNoCompare{} the value comparison never ran: the substituted address
is one no agent ever writes --- the harness draws a uniformly random address
rather than another node's --- so the dependent \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls on the
presence gate and the flag is a fail-stop, not a detection. Oracle
non-vacuity therefore rests on \MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of the
\MutTotal{} verdicts --- every leaf corruption, plus the address substitutions
that happened to reach a live slot --- and not on all \MutTotal{}, as we
previously reported.

Read the other way round, the same fact is an asset. \MutAddrStall{} of the
\MutAddrSub{} address substitutions fail-stopped instead of corrupting, and in
every one we re-simulated the fabric raised \texttt{P\_ERROR} with a recorded
\sv{error\_pc} on every PE. That is Property~\ref{prop:2}'s halt outcome
observed under injected fault rather than argued from the RTL, and the split
falls exactly where the presence gate places the boundary: all \MutLeaf{}
leaf-corruption mutants terminate normally with a wrong root --- one of them
one ulp from canonical --- and are caught by the value comparison instead.
Mutants were injected across all \InvLeafCounts{} leaf-count classes, so no
row of Table~\ref{tab:invariance} rests on an oracle that was never exercised.

\subsection{The Cost of the ABI}\label{sec:results-overhead}

We report no measured $\eta$ (\S\ref{sec:method}), and quoting a ratio against
a reducer that does not exist would not be a measurement. But the ABI's cost
is not an empirical accident to be discovered by benchmarking; it is a
consequence of the contract, and the contract is small enough to price
analytically. Four constraints separate \sprs{} from an unconstrained reducer:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
% Term 1 previously asserted the premise the eta theorem refutes.  Arity k > 2
% is not expressible: COMPUTE names two DMEM addresses only.  Kept in the
% enumeration so depth is still accounted for; it now reports a zero.
\item \emph{\FBT{} depth --- zero here.} The ABI fixes $\FBT(N)$, so the
  dependence chain is $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ deep regardless of $G$; a
  reducer forming arity-$k$ partial sums would be $\log_k N$ deep, but $k > 2$
  is not expressible on this fabric, so no same-fabric reducer is shallower
  than $\FBT(N)$ and the term is at most zero (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item \emph{No reassociation.} Fixing $(\mathrm{left}(v),\mathrm{right}(v))$
  forbids regrouping operands by locality. An unconstrained reducer may sum
  PE-local values first and cross the network once; \sprs{} crosses wherever
  the tree says to.
\item \emph{GCI serialisation.} Consecutive \texttt{GENERATE}s on one PE are
  spaced by $\sv{gci\_gap} = 6$ cycles, so a PE holding $\lceil N/G\rceil$
  leaves spends at least $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ cycles producing them. Unlike
  term~2 this is an artefact of \emph{our} GCI, not of the ABI.
\item \emph{Root-gather fan-in.} The root PE must receive from the other $G-1$
  PEs: at least $\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge stages plus roughly half a
  network diameter. This binds any reducer, deterministic or not.
\end{enumerate}

Term~2 is the price of reproducibility and cannot be engineered away without
abandoning the contract; term~1 is that price in a form this fabric cannot
charge; term 3 is ours to fix and term 4 is nobody's.

\emph{Term 3, measured directly.} The GCI floor $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ accounts
for at most \GciShareLowMax{} of the champion makespan on every one of the
\GciLowInst{} instances with $N/G \le 2$, and for at least \GciShareHighMin{}
on every one of the \GciHighInst{} instances with $N/G > 8$, rising to
\GciShareTopLo{}--\GciShareTopHi{}\% on the \GciTopInst{} instances with
$N/G \ge 32$ (Spearman $\rho = \GciShareRho{}$, $n = \TournNInst{}$). The two
groups do not overlap. Since $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ assumes perfectly balanced
leaf assignment while any real assignment puts at least $\lceil N/G\rceil$
leaves on the busiest PE, these are \emph{lower} bounds on the true share.
Leaf-generation serialisation is thus the dominant term at high $N/G$: on this
suite a multi-channel leaf interface is the highest-value modification
available to the fabric, and it costs nothing in the ABI.

\emph{A prediction we withdraw.} An earlier version tested term~3 by banding
$\mathrm{cycles}/\Omega$ against $N/G$ and read the decline as a successful
prediction of a knee at $N/G \in [6,8]$. That test is circular. $\Omega$
equals $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ on \GciOmegaEq{} of the \TournNInst{} instances, so
the term under test is the denominator; the absolute distance
$\mathrm{cycles}-\Omega$ carries no $N/G$ signal at all
($\rho = \GciNullRho{}$, $p = \GciNullP{}$); and a one-parameter model with no
mechanism in it, $\mathrm{cycles} = \Omega + \SlackMedian{}$, reproduces the
published band medians at and above the predicted knee to within
\GciNullTol{}. Nothing is lost: the direct measurement above does not involve
$\Omega$ and points the same way term~3 does --- indeed the opposite way to
the withdrawn reading, since GCI serialisation binds \emph{above} the claimed
knee, not below it.

\emph{Distance to the lower bound.} The one absolute statement available is
how far the compiler lands from $\Omega$. Because $\Omega$ bounds any schedule
here, including a non-deterministic one, $\mathrm{cycles}/\Omega$
upper-bounds what an ideal unconstrained reducer could have saved: weaker than
$\eta$, but real and measured. The informative reading is additive rather than
multiplicative. The compiler's absolute distance from the bound is
near-constant --- median \SlackMedian{} cycles, interquartile range
$[\SlackIqrLo, \SlackIqrHi]$ --- across a range of $\Omega$ from
\SlackOmegaLo{} to \SlackOmegaHi{}, and uncorrelated with $N/G$
($\rho = \GciNullRho{}$, $p = \GciNullP{}$). The schedule carries a roughly
fixed overhead over the analytic floor regardless of problem size, so relative
efficiency improves monotonically with scale and reaches $\SlackBest\times$ at
the largest instance. No instance reaches $\Omega$, which is expected: the
bound is assembled from separately-derived constraints and is not claimed
tight. Table~\ref{tab:omegaratio} is a descriptive per-topology summary and
not a controlled contrast, and we draw no topology ordering from it: the six
cells differ in median $N/G$ by a factor of \TopoNGSpread{}, the suite
contains only \TopoMultiPoint{} $(N,G)$ points carrying more than one
topology, and those \TopoMultiPoint{} disagree in sign. An earlier version
read the column as ordering with bisection width; that reading is
substantially $N/G$ in disguise and we withdraw it.

\subsection{Champion Equivalence Classes}\label{sec:results-classes}

Earlier versions of this paper reported a winner distribution: which
assignment and which mapping algorithm produced the fastest image on each
instance. We withdraw it, because it was not a measurement, and the reason it
was not is this paper's own subject.

The campaign deduplicates by program image before simulating, so one simulated
row stands for a class of bit-identical programs and the champion of an
instance is a \emph{set}. Expanding those classes back over the full
$18\times10$ grid: the minimum measured cycle count is attained by more than
one configuration on \ClsTied{} of \TournNInst{} instances, by a median of
\ClsMedian{} configurations and by as many as \ClsMax{}; all ten mapping
algorithms attain it on \ClsAllMappings{} of \TournNInst{}; and exactly
\ClsUnique{} instance has a unique champion. Which member of a tie set the
published table named was decided by a minimum over CSV rows in
parallel-worker arrival order --- that is, by which simulation host finished
first. A paper about scheduling nondeterminism had its winner table ordered by
scheduling nondeterminism, and saying so is more useful than quietly
re-sorting it: no tie-break repairs the table, because on \ClsTied{} of
\TournNInst{} instances the tied set contains programs that are bit-identical
and therefore cannot be separated by any measurement at all.

What replaces the ranking is stronger, and its data was already in the paper.
Proposition~\ref{thm:orth} says the compiler's variation space cannot reach
the bit result. The campaign shows that same space collapsing structurally
before it ever reaches the fabric: of the \ClsCompilations{} compilations
performed, \ClsCollapse{} produce a program image that another configuration
had already produced. The compiler variation space is thus not merely
incapable of changing the answer --- over most of its extent it does not even
yield a different program. The campaign is, from the ABI's point of view,
\WallTBs{} attempts to break Claim~\ref{claim:1}, every one of which failed;
it is not a ranking of algorithms.

\subsection{A Derived Selection Rule}\label{sec:selrule}

The campaign was built to answer a practitioner's question --- given an
instance, which of the \SelConfigsTotal{} configurations should be run? ---
and champion-class membership answers it in a form invariant to the tie
structure above. For a recommended set we report \emph{regret}: the measured
cycle count of its best member divided by the instance's measured optimum, so
$1.000$ means the set contains a champion.

\emph{The rule is a portfolio, not a regime map.} Exhaustive search over
subsets of the \SelSearchAlgs{} near-universally evaluable assignment
algorithms, minimising mean measured regret and refitted independently in each
of \SelFolds{} leave-one-out folds, returned the \emph{identical} subset in
\SelFolds{} of \SelFolds{} folds at every size. At size \SelKFour{} that
subset is \SelPortfolio{}, and on the held-out instance it attains the exact
hardware-measured optimum in \SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{} folds
($\SelKFourRate$), with mean out-of-sample regret \SelKFourMean{} and worst
case \SelKFourWorst{}. It evaluates \SelConfigsRun{} of the
\SelConfigsTotal{} configurations, a median $\SelSpeedup\times$ reduction in
compiler time ($\SelCompileBefore$\,s to $\SelCompileAfter$\,s per instance).
Table~\ref{tab:selection_rule} gives it with the smaller budgets. Size
\SelKnee{} is a knee rather than a budget we picked: a fifth configuration is
identical to four decimal places under both protocols, and only at six does
held-out regret reach \SelKSixMean{}.

\emph{Conditioning on the instance makes it worse.} This is the result we did
not expect and the one that makes the portfolio finding non-obvious. Every
decision tree we could fit over $(N, G, N/G, \text{topology})$ is beaten out
of sample by the unconditional portfolio: the best reaches an exact-optimum
rate of $\SelTreeRate$ (\SelTreeOpt{} of \TournNInst{}) against
$\SelKFourRate$, and its out-of-sample mean regret,
\SelTreeMeanLo{}--\SelTreeMeanHi{}, is worse even than the single-algorithm
constant rule ``always \sv{ParSub}'' (\SelConstMean{}). A regime map over
these axes is not merely unnecessary; it is actively harmful, because it fits
partitions of a \TournNInst{}-instance suite whose cells hold two or three
instances each. The simplest conditioner fails the same way: taking whichever
algorithm was best on this instance's topology in training attains the optimum
on \SelCondTopoOpt{} of \TournNInst{} held-out instances against
\SelKFourOpt{}, at mean regret \SelCondTopoMean{} against \SelKFourMean{}.
Topology is the axis on which conditioning is most intuitive, and the axis on
which it most clearly does not pay. Against the two pre-specified constant
baselines the portfolio is significant on both confirmatory tests (McNemar
$p = \SelMcNemarP$; Wilcoxon signed-rank on regret $p = \SelWilcoxonP$; both
survive Holm correction over the two comparisons).

\emph{It transfers to a topology the fit never saw.} Leave-one-out holds out
an instance but not its topology, and instances sharing a topology are not
independent, so we also refit with an \emph{entire topology} withheld and
score each fold only on the topology it never saw. Across the
\SelTopoGroups{} topologies the portfolio transfers better than conditioning
generalises: mean out-of-sample regret \SelUnseenTopoMean{} (95\% bootstrap
interval $[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$ over \SelBootReps{} resamples
of the \TournNInst{} instances), lower than the \SelCondTopoMean{} of a rule
that conditions on topology and does see it in training. The exact measured
optimum is still attained on \SelUnseenTopoOpt{} of \TournNInst{} instances
(\SelUnseenTopoPct{}), worst case \SelUnseenTopoWorst{}. Withholding an entire
leaf-count band instead leaves the leave-one-out figure unchanged
(\SelUnseenScaleMean{}). We quote the interval and not the point estimate
deliberately: with only \SelTopoGroups{} topologies the grouped estimate is
genuinely uncertain at the top end, and a practitioner adopting the rule on an
unseen topology should plan against \SelUnseenTopoHi{} rather than against the
mean. The corresponding leave-one-out interval is
$[\SelLooCiLo, \SelLooCiHi]$.

\emph{The mapping stage can be deleted.} Assignment is the whole decision.
Conditional on \sv{ParSub}, the ten mapping algorithms give byte-identical
cycle counts on all \TournNInst{} instances. Fixing the mapping at
\sv{Identity} and choosing only the assignment attains the exact optimum on
\SelIdentityOpt{} of \TournNInst{}, at mean cost \SelIdentityMean{} and worst
case \SelIdentityWorst{}. We therefore recommend removing the mapping search
from the pipeline rather than conditioning on it. Note what this does
\emph{not} say: \sv{Identity} is not the best mapping --- its apparent lead in
the withdrawn winner table was a labelling artefact
(\S\ref{sec:results-classes}) --- it is indistinguishable from the others and
the cheapest to compute. In the other direction the spread across assignment
algorithms is three orders of magnitude larger: \SelZeroCount{} of the
eighteen are in the champion class on \emph{zero} of \TournNInst{} instances
and cost a median $\SelZeroRegretLo\times$ to $\SelZeroRegretHi\times$ in
cycles, up to $\SelZeroWorst\times$ at worst. Greedy list scheduling is the
block to avoid.

\input{tables/selection}

\emph{What the rule does not license.} Nothing here survives Holm correction
over the \SelExpTests{} exploratory association tests we ran between algorithm
families and instance parameters; \SelBHSurvivors{} survive
Benjamini--Hochberg, and we report the portfolio rather than any of them
precisely because the portfolio needs no such association to hold. $N$ and $G$
are confounded across this suite by construction (Spearman
$\rho = \SelRhoNG$, $p = \SelRhoNGP$), so the budget rows of
Table~\ref{tab:selection_rule} that split on scale cannot be attributed to
either variable separately. Of the \SelPopCells{} populated
(size $\times$ topology) cells, \SelThinCells{} hold two instances or fewer,
so no cell-level recommendation is derivable and we make none. The rule is
untested at $G = 4096$, the one design point without a hardware result. And it
is a retrospective statement about measured cycle counts, not a claim that the
compiler can identify the winner from its own cost model. Every number is a
measured cycle count on this pipeline for tree-structured reduction programs;
none of it transfers to another compiler, another accelerator, or to MPI or
NCCL collectives.

\section{Exporting the ABI: Pinned-FBT}\label{sec:pinfbt}
% =====================================================================

Can the discipline be adopted without the \sprs{} fabric? It can, in software:
replace a production collective's dynamic $G$-dependent tree with a library
that enforces the $\FBT(N)$ post-order schedule whatever $G$ and the topology
are. We call this \emph{pinned-FBT}. Two things must be said first, because we
got the first wrong and the second is not ours.

\emph{The construction we published is incorrect.} It partitioned canonical
leaf indices into contiguous ranges, $\mathrm{rank}(i) = \lfloor iG/N \rfloor$,
and asserted that each rank then reduces exactly the subtree rooted at the
lowest common ancestor of its leaves. Swept over all \SweepPairs{} pairs with
$2 \le N \le 300$ and $2 \le G \le N$ that holds on \SweepStepOneHolds{} of
them (\SweepStepOnePct{}); the smallest counterexample is $N=3$, $G=2$, where
rank $0$ owns leaves $\{0,1\}$ whose lowest common ancestor is the whole
tree's root; and it fails on $8$ of the \SuiteSize{} instances evaluated here.
Nor is it merely a description error: evaluated literally in FP64 on our own
leaf vector~\eqref{eq:leaves}, the composed construction returns a root
differing from $\Rcan(N,L)$ on \PinIrMismatch{} of those pairs, on
\SweepRootDivergePct{} of the \PinSuitePairs{} pairs our own leaf counts
admit, and on two of our own instances --- $N=10, G=4$ by $2$ ulp and
$N=200, G=12$ by $1$ ulp. The discrepancy is $1$--$\PinIrUlp{}$ ulp
throughout, small in magnitude and total failure of a bitwise claim. A second,
independent error survives any repair of the first: an all-reduce over
sub-roots ``consistent with $\FBT(N)$'s top $\log_2 G$ levels'' is $\FBT(G)$
only when $N$ and $G$ are both powers of two. The published construction is
exact precisely at power-of-two $N$ and $G$.

\emph{The corrected construction.} Cut $\FBT(N)$ at depth
$d = \lceil \log_2 G \rceil$ instead of partitioning index ranges. The cut
frontier --- nodes at depth $d$ together with any leaf shallower than $d$ ---
roots disjoint complete subtrees covering every leaf; assign those subtrees to
the $G$ ranks; each rank evaluates its subtrees by the canonical recurrence of
Def.~\ref{def:fbt} under the enumeration of Def.~\ref{def:leafenum}; the
cross-rank stage evaluates the internal nodes above the frontier, exactly
$2^{d}-1$ combines --- the $\log_2 G$-depth all-reduce the published version
wanted and did not have. The proof obligation is one sentence: every node of
$\FBT(N)$ is still computed exactly once, by the same recurrence and with the
same operand binding (Def.~\ref{def:opbind}), so the multiset of FADD
operations and the dependency order among them are those of $\FBT(N)$ itself
and the root is $\Rcan(N,L)$ for every $G$. Enumeration discharges it over the
same space that refutes the published version: every one of those
\SweepPairs{} pairs is exact in FP64. The frontier-to-rank assignment does not
affect the root --- the tree fixes the order --- so it is a free
load-balancing choice, and the choice matters: under a greedy largest-first
deal the worst leaf-count ratio is \PinImbLpt{} and none exceeds $2$, while
under the natural contiguous deal it reaches \PinImbContig{} and exceeds $2$
on \PinImbContigOver{} of them. An export must therefore \emph{specify} the
greedy deal.

\emph{Prior art, and the residue.} TBIK~\cite{tbik2025} predates this
manuscript; it is prior art, not concurrent work, and we claim no priority
against it. Its Appendix~C, Theorem~1 proves by induction, over the same
contiguous-block partition, exactly the theorem our step~1 asserted without
proof, under exactly the power-of-two hypotheses that make it true. The
summary is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly: the correct part of
pinned-FBT is what TBIK had already done, and the part TBIK did not do ---
arbitrary, non-power-of-two $N$ --- is the part that was wrong. What remains
ours is the cut-aligned repair for arbitrary $N$ under canonical heap-index
leaf enumeration, together with the properties the export inherits from the
ABI and TBIK does not test: invariance across network \emph{topologies} rather
than across tensor-parallel sizes inside one node, enforcement below the
software stack, and presence-gated fail-stop.

\emph{Measured: the repair is invariant, and the checker can fail.} We
implemented both constructions on PyTorch's collectives and ran them on
\GpuHardware{} with NCCL on the two GPUs and the CPU backend for larger world
sizes. Over \PinCases{} cases spanning $N$ from \PinLeafLo{} to \PinLeafHi{}
(\PinNpowLeaf{} of the leaf counts not powers of two --- the case TBIK does
not cover, and the majority of the sweep), world sizes
\PinWorldLo{}--\PinWorldHi{} (\PinNpowWorld{} not powers of two), messages
from \PinMsgLo{} to \PinMsgHi{}, and \PinDeals{} frontier deals, every one of
the \PinCells{} (case, world size, rank) cells returned a single 64-bit root,
equal to the single-process canonical $\Rcan(N,L)$. The result is reportable
only because the same checker demonstrably fails: run against the index-range
construction as a pre-registered negative control, \PinNegFired{} of
\PinNegCases{} cases broke as they must --- at $N=\PinNegLeafA{}$ and
$N=\PinNegLeafB{}$ it returned \PinNegDistinct{} distinct roots across world
sizes \PinNegWorlds{} and \emph{none} of its \PinNegCells{} cells equalled
$\Rcan$. Where it agrees --- \PinNegPartial{} of \PinNegCells{} cells at
$N=1{,}024$, all of them at $N=7$ --- it agrees by power-of-two coincidence.

\emph{Measured: what it costs, and the answer is unfavourable.} The statistic
was fixed before the run: geometric-mean overhead against stock
\texttt{ncclAllReduce} at $G{=}2$ in FP64 over the swept message sizes, worst
case beside it, with a support branch at roughly $1.0$--$1.3\times$ and an
explicit unfavourable branch. The measurement took the unfavourable branch:
over \PinCostCells{} configurations the geometric mean is $\PinCostGeo\times$
stock, best $\PinCostBest\times$, worst $\PinCostWorst\times$. Two
qualifications pull in opposite directions and neither changes the verdict.
First, the sweep ran while an unrelated job saturated both GPUs, so the point
estimate is \emph{provisional}: substituting the best of the repeats at each
configuration for the median gives $\PinCostRobust\times$, so contention
inflates the reported figure by about $\PinCostInflate\times$. Both estimators
are far outside the pre-registered support band, so the verdict does not
depend on the correction --- the point estimate does, and a re-run on a quiet
machine is owed. Second, the overhead is not one quantity. At $G{=}2$ the
pinned tree moves $2M$ bytes, one directed send of the full vector plus a
broadcast, against a bandwidth-optimal ring's $M$ per rank: a factor of about
two is algorithmic and no engineering removes it. The remainder is an export
artefact --- two dependent library calls issued from Python against one fused
NCCL kernel --- dominating at small messages ($\PinSmallCost\times$ at
\PinSmallSize{}, where the whole stock call takes $\PinSmallStock$~$\mu$s) and
fading at large ones ($\PinLargeLo$--$\PinLargeHi\times$ between
\PinLargeSizeLo{} and \PinLargeSizeHi{}).

The result contradicts no claim this paper makes and prices one it already
predicts. What we claim for pinned-FBT is a correctness property, measured
above; what we do not claim is that it is cheap. It should not be set beside
TBIK's $10\%$: that figure is four H20 GPUs over NVLink on an uncontended
node, a different fabric measured a different way. On this evidence the export
is usable where reshape reproducibility is the requirement and the collective
is not the bottleneck, and not inside a bandwidth-critical loop. Closing the
non-algorithmic part of the gap is an engineering problem; the algorithmic
factor of two at $G{=}2$ is what the tree costs. Fabric-level and
software-level enforcement can discharge the same structural obligation, but
the record above is what it looks like when a construction is believed to
discharge it and does not.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

Relative to every axis here, what we claim is the conjunction of
\S\ref{sec:intro}, not any single conjunct.
\emph{Reproducible arithmetic} --- ReproBLAS~\cite{demmel2013reproBLAS},
ExBLAS~\cite{iakymchuk2016exblas}, Arteaga et
al.~\cite{arteaga2014bitreproducible} --- obtains order-independence by
changing the operator (pre-rounding, long accumulators, error-free
transformations), so the result is reproducible but is not the FP64 sum any
particular order produces, and a wide accumulator is required; compensated
summation~\cite{kahan1965summation} reduces error without fixing order. We
change no operator and add no accumulator, and fix the order instead.
\emph{Fixed-order schemes in deployed stacks} pin one axis inside one
implementation: oneMKL's conditional numerical reproducibility is invariant to
thread count and input partitioning~\cite{onemkl_cnr}; TBIK~\cite{tbik2025} is
invariant across tensor-parallel size and proves the power-of-two case of the
construction \S\ref{sec:pinfbt} repairs, at a measured cost on NVLink far
better than our software export's on PCIe; the ``deterministic'' modes of NCCL
and RCCL hold within one $(G,\text{topology})$ and not across
reshape~\cite{nccl2018,rccl2019}. \emph{In-network reduction} is the closest
precedent for enforcing order in hardware, and we claim no advantage over it in
the speed or cost of the reduction itself. SHARP~\cite{graham2016scalable}
aggregates inside InfiniBand switches and pins operand order to do so, each
aggregation node holding a predefined order of
calculation~\cite{mellanox2019aggregation}; its specification is equally
explicit that reproducibility holds ``for a given application-to-system
mapping'' --- per deployment, where our index is $N$ and survives the
deployment changing, which a per-mapping order cannot without standardising
switch hierarchies across sites. It also prices the discipline structurally, a
non-reproducible mode saving aggregation buffering by $n/2$ in the number of
inputs. SwitchML~\cite{sapio2021scaling} instead carries gradients in $32$-bit
fixed point through integer-only switch ALUs, so its order-insensitivity is
integer associativity rather than a pinned order. Groq's
TSP~\cite{abts2022groq} shows a compiler/fabric determinism contract shipping,
but the determinism is of the schedule: no bitwise claim is made, and its
all-reduce is keyed to the packaging hierarchy, so reduction order is again a
function of how many chips sit in a node. None states the invariant at a
compiler/fabric interface indexed by problem size, enforced below the software
that could violate it, with a fail-stop failure mode.
\emph{Collective-scheduling
compilers}~\cite{cai2021synthesizing,shah2023taccl,thakur2005optimization,%
rabenseifner2004optimization} search schedule space for throughput, treating
reduction order as free; that freedom is exactly what we remove. Our
assignment, mapping, routing and pipeline components are all prior
art~\cite{topcuoglu2002heft,lukes1974efficient,dally2004principles}; the
contribution is that any choice among them inherits invariance by composition.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\emph{L0 --- One unbuildable configuration.} Hardware validation covers
\TournNInst{} of the \SuiteSize{} instances. The exception is
\sv{maxp\_mod\_fat} ($N{=}8192$, $G{=}4096$, fat-tree), whose
\MaxpAttempts{} attempted testbenches all hit the wall-clock cap for
\MaxpCoreHours{} core-hours --- but wall-clock was not the binding constraint,
and we correct an earlier account that said it was. The instance instantiates
\MaxpRouters{} routers while the adjacency interface carries a
\RouterIdBits-bit router id whose top code is the disconnected sentinel, so
every one of its \MaxpAdjEntries{} adjacency writes touches an id that aliases
onto a low one. Replayed through the RTL's own truncation and range guards,
the emitted configuration installs \MaxpLinksInstalled{} links of which
\MaxpLinksCorrect{} match the intended fat-tree and delivers
\MaxpRoutesDelivered{} of \MaxpRoutesSampled{} sampled leaf-to-leaf routes,
all \MaxpRoutesSampled{} of which deliver on the untruncated fabric. No budget
could have completed it. We have since repaired the truncation and
re-elaborated: across \MaxpElabAttempts{} attempts under \MaxpDebugCfgs{}
\sv{xelab} debug configurations, \MaxpElabClean{} ran elaboration to
completion and exited zero reporting the snapshot built, and \MaxpKernels{}
wrote a simulation kernel --- so there is nothing for the simulator to load.
We assert no root cause: a tool that exits zero having written no output is
misreporting and we have not established why. The exclusion is a truncation
defect in the emitter as shipped and, once repaired, a tool limit at this
design point; it is neither a wall-clock budget nor a property of the fabric.

\emph{L0a --- The denominator.} On the \TournNInst{} instances,
\InvAttempted{} distinct images were attempted, \WallTBs{} settled and passed,
\InvNonSettling{} did not settle. All but one of those are watchdog
fail-stops --- their per-PE counters stop at a last retirement plus exactly
\WatchdogMax{} cycles, far inside the testbench budget --- and the remaining
one is a simulator wall-clock kill. Non-settling runs are not evidence for
Claim~\ref{claim:1} and we do not count them as such; they are the campaign's
only observation of Property~\ref{prop:2} on unmutated compiler output.
Coverage is narrower than the space we advertise: the \SelConfigsTotal{} pairs
enumerated per instance are $0.82\%$ of the $21{,}960$ triples, refinement is
\sv{None} in every measured configuration, and the remaining axis is covered
by Proposition~\ref{thm:orth} rather than by measurement.

\emph{L0b --- Configuration-load bypass, measured.} Route-table
initialisation in every reported run is by hierarchical assignment at time
zero rather than the clocked protocol, which costs $\mathcal{O}(G^2)$
simulated cycles. An earlier version attached an unreproducible fidelity band
to that substitution; we have since measured it. Re-running every passing
configuration at $G \le \RouteGMax$ under both paths gives the same verdict,
root and cycle count on \RouteRuns{} of \RouteRuns{} points
(\RouteBitExact{} bit-exact, no exceptions); the only effect of clocked
loading is a deterministic prologue of one clock per route-table entry,
completing before \sv{program\_start}; and a negative control confirms the
clocked path genuinely exercises routing, corrupting the route table flipping
\sv{PASS} to \sv{FAIL} on \RouteNegCtrl{} of \RouteNegCtrl{} runs. The
residual is scope, not fidelity: the clocked path is uncharacterised above
$G = \RouteGMax$.

\emph{L1 --- Scope.} \sv{fp64\_add} is unpipelined. A stateless adder is what
makes the arithmetic a pure function of its operand pair, which
Claim~\ref{claim:1} needs, but it is not a realistic synthesis target; we have
run no synthesis and report no $F_{\max}$, slack or utilisation. Pipelining
would change \emph{when} a \texttt{COMPUTE} releases its scoreboard bit, not
which operand pair it reads, so cycle counts would move and the roots in
Table~\ref{tab:invariance} would not. Beyond that: no silicon, no measured
same-fabric overhead $\eta$ (\S\ref{sec:method}: a theorem on $31$ of
\TournNInst{}, unmeasured on the other $8$), no head-to-head against
production collectives on production interconnect, one leaf vector per $N$,
one reduction per program (scoreboard bits are monotone within a program), and
a suite whose (size $\times$ topology) cells are too thin for cell-level
claims. FTZ+RNE is strictly narrower than IEEE~754-2019. \textsf{C-sw} is
soft-enforced: a compile-time post-condition, not a fabric check. We prove no
deadlock-freedom property, the emitted routes being minimal-distance rather
than dimension-ordered. And Claim~\ref{claim:1} has no formal proof: the
argument is by code inspection and dynamic checking.

\emph{L13 --- Two defect classes this evaluation cannot see.} This one bounds
the evaluation \emph{method} rather than the artifact, and it is the item we
would most want a reader building a similar campaign to take away. First, the
campaign cannot exercise the FADD's effective-subtraction path at all:
Eq.~\eqref{eq:leaves} is strictly positive at every index, so both operands of
every FADD in all \WallTBs{} runs carry the same sign. The defect that lived
on that path could not have appeared in any number reported here --- not at
this campaign size and not at any other, because the blindness does not lie
along the axis the campaign enumerates: all \CompileTotal{} compilations share
one leaf generator. Second, and closer to the bone, the oracle against which
every root is compared is not an independent model of IEEE-754 addition. It is
a transcription of the same adder and it reproduced the same defect bit for
bit; on the as-submitted pair the two agree on all \FpCtrlVectors{} vectors of
the acceptance test while both disagree with correctly-rounded addition on
\FpCtrlErrors{} of them. A differential test is only as strong as the
independence of its two sides, and ours were not independent. Both are cases
of an evaluation that cannot detect a class of defect \emph{by construction},
and neither is visible from inside the campaign: no pass rate, coverage figure
or additional instance would have exposed either, and both were found by
reading the arithmetic against an external standard. Enumerating a parameter
space exhaustively says nothing about the axes that space does not span, and
the two axes that mattered here --- operand sign, and the independence of the
oracle --- were not parameters of the campaign at all.


% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

Cluster-reshape non-determinism in floating-point reductions is an
\emph{interface} problem, and the place to fix it is the contract between a
collective-scheduling compiler and its fabric. Reduction order, treated as a
size-indexed ABI over a canonical full binary tree, is orthogonal to $G$,
topology, assignment, mapping, refinement and schedule, and yields
cross-topology bitwise invariance: the 64-bit root is a pure function of $N$
and the leaf vector. What we claim is a conjunction, not a first --- that
invariant, enforced at the compiler/fabric interface with a fail-stop failure
mode, across network topologies rather than resource counts alone, over an
unmodified IEEE-754 FP64 operator, for arbitrary non-power-of-two $N$.

On \SuiteSize{} instances spanning $N \in [8,8192]$, $G \in [2,4096]$ and six
topologies, the campaign enumerated the refinement-free face of the compiler's
configuration space exhaustively and RTL-validated every distinct image it
produced: of \InvAttempted{} attempted on \TournNInst{} hardware-validated
instances, \WallTBs{} settled with a root bit-identical to the canonical one
and \InvNonSettling{} did not settle, all but one a watchdog fail-stop.
Injected faults establish that the zero-divergence result is non-vacuous on
\MutOracleLo{}--\MutOracleHi{} of \MutTotal{} verdicts, the remainder being
the halt outcome Property~\ref{prop:2} predicts. We price the ABI structurally
rather than against a non-deterministic baseline, which remains the principal
open item in the evaluation.

Two negative results travel with the positive one. The compiler search this
campaign was built around is not a ranking problem: the minimising
configuration is a tie set on \ClsTied{} of \TournNInst{} instances, and what
replaces the ranking --- a fixed portfolio of \SelConfigsRun{} assignment
algorithms reaching the measured optimum on \SelKFourOpt{} of \TournNInst{}
held-out instances, transferring to an entirely unseen topology at mean regret
\SelUnseenTopoMean{} $[\SelUnseenTopoLo, \SelUnseenTopoHi]$ and beating every
regime map we could fit --- is both cheaper and better supported than the
distribution it replaces. And two defect classes in our own artifact are
invisible to this evaluation by construction: we found them by reading the
arithmetic, not by running more configurations. An evaluation that enumerates
a space exhaustively can still be blind along an axis that space does not
span, and that blindness is not observable from inside the campaign.

Finally, \emph{pinned-FBT} exports the discipline onto existing collective
libraries. The construction we published is exact only at power-of-two $N$ and
$G$; the cut-aligned repair given here is bitwise reshape-invariant on all
\PinCells{} cells swept, against a pre-registered negative control that broke
on \PinNegFired{} of \PinNegCases{} cases. Its cost is unfavourable and the
statistic was fixed before the run: a geometric mean of $\PinCostGeo\times$
stock \texttt{ncclAllReduce} at $G{=}2$ in FP64, provisional under GPU
contention, of which a factor of about two is the algorithmic price of the
pinned tree at two ranks. Reshape reproducibility is therefore available in
software where the collective is not the bottleneck, and is the wrong purchase
inside a bandwidth-critical loop. Fabric-level and software-level enforcement
discharge the same structural obligation at very different prices; what makes
either one checkable is that the reduction order is enforced rather than
intended.

\section{The Fifteen Assumptions}\label{app:assumptions}
% =====================================================================

Five compiler obligations, discharged by the compiler or the user; ten fabric
invariants, established by RTL inspection. The four marked~$\dagger$ are
hypotheses of Property~\ref{prop:2} only: violating one can stop a run but
cannot alter a root the fabric does produce.

\emph{Compiler obligations.}
\textsf{C-sw} (single-writer allocation for remote destinations): partitioning
each PE's DMEM into \emph{receive buffers} --- addresses named as
\texttt{target\_addr} by a remote \texttt{SEND} --- and \emph{local
temporaries}, the image contains for every PE $p$ and receive buffer
$a \neq 0$ exactly one write to $(p,a)$, and it is remote. Local temporaries
are exempt: the allocator reuses them and their safety comes from in-order
issue rather than single assignment. The scoping is load-bearing and the two
classes are disjoint by construction; on the emitted images of
\RecycleConfigs{} configurations the recycled and remotely-written address
sets never intersect, and no operand is read after the write that recycles its
slot (\RecycleUnsafe{} such events in \RecycleEvents{}).
\textsf{C-bind} and \textsf{C-leaf} are Defs.~\ref{def:opbind}
and~\ref{def:leafbind}, checked as post-conditions on the emitted image.
\textsf{C-mem} (memory feasibility): every PE names at most
$\sv{DMEM\_DEPTH} = \DmemDepth$ distinct DMEM addresses and every emitted
router id is below the adjacency sentinel (\RouterIdBits{} bits, at most
\RouterIdMax). Neither is enforced at run time --- the fabric truncates DMEM
and \texttt{SEND} target addresses to \SendAddrBits{} bits, so an over-budget
image aliases two addresses onto one slot silently, and out-of-range router
ids alias onto low ids, which is what stopped one instance from ever settling
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, L0) --- but both are cheap to discharge, peak
occupancy being an $O(N)$ read-off the allocator already maintains.
$\dagger$\,\textsf{C-route}: the emitted tables encode destination-correct,
loop-free paths. We claim those two properties only; the tables are
minimal-distance rather than dimension-ordered, so we do not claim an acyclic
channel-dependency graph.

\emph{Fabric invariants.}
\textsf{F-sb-mono}: for every receive buffer $a$, \texttt{scoreboard[a]}
transitions $0\to1$ at most once per program, at the clock edge of the unique
remote write, and stays set until reset.
\textsf{F-sb-gate}: \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls \textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX} until
both operand bits are $1$, \texttt{SEND} until its source bit is.
\textsf{F-fw}: a DMEM read at cycle $t$ returns the value committed by the
unique write at cycle $\leq t$, even across the adjacent-cycle race.
\textsf{F-inorder}: each PE issues in order through
\textsf{IF}$\to$\textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX}, one instruction at a time,
sampling both operands at the \textsf{DE} read port strictly before writing
back in \textsf{EX} --- which is what makes local-temporary reuse safe, since
the scoreboard cannot, a recycled slot's bit being already set.
\textsf{F-gci-haz}: from \sv{gci\_start} until the GCI agent commits,
\textsf{DE} stalls any \texttt{COMPUTE} or \texttt{SEND} naming the pending
destination.
\textsf{F-arb}: strict priority $\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} > \mathrm{FSM}$
with a GCI retry latch; no write is silently dropped.
\textsf{F-fadd}: \sv{fp64\_add.sv} is combinational and stateless, its output
a pure function of the two 64-bit inputs sampled at the EX clock edge.
$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-credit}: per link and interval,
$\#\mathrm{credit\_returns} \leq \#\mathrm{buffer\_reads}$ with bounded wait.
$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-misroute}: packets whose \sv{dest\_gpu\_id} does not
match the local PE are dropped, not silently consumed.
$\dagger$\,\textsf{F-watchdog}: a stall exceeding
$\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = \WatchdogMax$ cycles raises \texttt{P\_ERROR}, as does
an invalid opcode immediately.
