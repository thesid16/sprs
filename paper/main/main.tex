\begin{abstract}
Floating-point reductions executed across multi-accelerator systems are not
bitwise reproducible under \emph{cluster reshape}: when the number of
processing elements $G$ or the physical topology changes, the underlying
collective's reduction order changes, and so do the last bits of the result.
Production collectives optimise throughput, not reproducibility, and therefore
rerunning the same deterministic computation on a resized cluster yields
bit-divergent weights, breaks scientific regression testing, and blocks
audit-grade replay of machine-learning training.  We argue that reduction order
is an \emph{interface} concern rather than a library concern, and treat it as a
compiler/fabric Application Binary Interface: a size-indexed contract that
fixes the operand binding at every internal node of a canonical full binary
tree $\FBT(N)$. Any fabric that enforces scoreboard-gated execution of this
contract, and any compiler that emits an ABI-compliant instruction image,
together yield a 64-bit result that is a pure function of $N$ and the leaf
vector~$L$, independent of~$G$, topology, assignment, mapping, and schedule.
We instantiate the ABI as \sprs{}: a topology-parametric SystemVerilog
network-on-chip with a four-opcode reduction ISA and a combinational
flush-to-zero/round-to-nearest-even FADD; and a seven-stage compiler whose
algorithmic configuration space spans $18\times 10\times 122 \approx 22{,}000$
assignment/mapping/refinement combinations, of which this campaign compiles
$7{,}200$ and validates $2{,}822$ in RTL. We state and
argue two properties under six compiler obligations and nine fabric
invariants: \emph{cross-topology bitwise invariance} (Claim~1) and
\emph{fail-stop under assumption violation} (Property~2), connected by an
explicit \emph{mapping/topology orthogonality} theorem. On 40 instances
spanning $N\!\in\![8,8192]$, $G\!\in\![2,4096]$, and six direct-network
topologies, $2{,}822$ RTL-validated configurations produce bit-identical
roots with zero divergence, and an injected-fault campaign confirms the
oracle is non-vacuous at $84/84$ detection. We price the ABI structurally
rather than empirically: its cost decomposes into a $\log_2 N$ dependence
chain, a prohibition on locality-driven reassociation, leaf-generation
serialisation, and root-gather fan-in. The RTL is
\ifdefined\fpgadata FPGA-validated at small scale and \fi
cycle-accurately simulated across the suite. We further sketch
\emph{pinned-FBT}, a software-only export of the ABI discipline onto existing
collective libraries (NCCL, MPI, oneCCL) that inherits the invariance property
without adopting our fabric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{D}{istributed} reductions are the connective tissue of modern
parallel computation: gradient all-reduce in synchronous data-parallel
training~\cite{sergeev2018horovod,li2020pytorch}, partial-sum aggregation in
scientific codes~\cite{dongarra2011hpcchallenge}, logits reduction in
pipelined inference. In each case, a multiset of per-worker partial sums is
collapsed into a single scalar or tensor through a collective whose
performance has been the subject of more than a decade of aggressive
engineering~\cite{thakur2005optimization,nccl2018,cai2021synthesizing,shah2023taccl}.
Performance, however, is not the same as determinism, and the two are in
tension. Production implementations of all-reduce --- ring, recursive
halving--doubling, double-binary tree, hierarchical hybrid --- select a
reduction order that maximises throughput for the observed network topology
and worker count~\cite{rabenseifner2004optimization,nccl2018}.  When either
changes, so does the order.

IEEE~754 double-precision addition is non-associative:
$(a+b)+c \neq a+(b+c)$ in the low-order bits whenever alignment shifts a
non-zero mantissa below the round bit. The classical bound~\cite{higham2002accuracy}
gives, for two reduction orders $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ of the same multiset,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\,{\textstyle\sum_\sigma x_i} - {\textstyle\sum_{\sigma'} x_i}\,\bigr|
  \;\leq\; (N-1)\,u\,{\textstyle\sum_i} |x_i| + \mathcal{O}(u^2),
  \label{eq:higham}
\end{equation}
with $u$ the unit of last place; under cancellation this bound is
catastrophically loose and the actual discrepancy is a function of the
summation tree. The consequence is concrete. A training job rerun on a
cluster resized from $G{=}128$ to $G{=}256$ GPUs --- identical seed,
identical dataset, identical code --- produces bit-divergent weights by the
end of the first iteration. The divergence propagates through every
subsequent step and compounds. Bit-for-bit regression testing of scientific
codes fails under the same mechanism: a code that ran to acceptance on a
development cluster produces different last bits on the production cluster,
and the reviewer cannot distinguish a new bug from the reshape artefact. In
regulated deployments --- medical-device inference, aerospace controls,
financial model replay --- the inability to reproduce a computation bitwise
across hardware generations is increasingly disqualifying.

\subsection{Two Sources of Non-Determinism, One Bad Outcome}

The cluster-reshape non-determinism we target has two distinct sources,
which current software tools confuse.

\emph{Source 1 --- order-dependence of the reducer.} The collective chooses
a reduction tree or ring shape as a function of the runtime environment:
the number of ranks $G$, the inter-node bandwidth matrix, and in some
implementations (NCCL, RCCL) a per-run bandwidth probe that selects the
algorithm dynamically.  Even with all randomness suppressed, the algorithm
itself is a function of $(G,\text{topology})$, not of the input.

\emph{Source 2 --- reassociation inside the reducer.} Within a chosen
algorithm, performance-critical implementations reorder partial sums to
hide latency --- overlapping pipeline segments, exploiting SIMD
lanes, merging small messages. Each micro-optimisation
is an implicit reassociation, and each changes the last bit.

Existing reproducibility tools address at most one of these sources.
ReproBLAS~\cite{demmel2013reproBLAS} and ExBLAS~\cite{iakymchuk2016exblas}
eliminate Source~2 within a single node via order-independent
binned accumulation; they do not constrain the inter-node schedule and
therefore inherit Source~1 from whatever collective composes them.  The
``deterministic'' modes of production collectives
(\texttt{NCCL\_ALGO=Tree} with a single channel; Horovod-det; oneCCL-det)
pin the algorithm within a fixed $(G,\text{topology})$ configuration;
reshape the cluster and the tree shape changes by design.
Vendor-deterministic accelerators~\cite{jouppi2017tpu,liu2016cambricon}
guarantee per-chip reproducibility and inherit the vendor library's
cross-chip non-reshape guarantee --- effectively, a stronger form of the
fixed-configuration mode. Compensated-summation
techniques~\cite{kahan1965summation,rump2009accurate} address Source~2 at
the per-reduction level but carry no statement about inter-worker order.

No existing approach guarantees that two executions of the same reduction on
the same leaf values yield bit-identical results when the cluster size,
topology, or schedule has changed.  This is the gap we close.

\subsection{Position: Reduction Order Is an Interface Concern}

We take the position that bitwise reproducibility under cluster reshape
cannot be achieved inside any single layer of the conventional software
stack, and must instead be moved into the \emph{interface} between the
collective-scheduling compiler and the underlying fabric.  A library-level
flag cannot forbid Source~1, because the library has no authority over
whether a different cluster size will be launched tomorrow.  A fabric-level
guarantee alone cannot forbid Source~2, because the fabric cannot see the
reduction tree a scheduler intends to use. The discipline has to be a
contract shared by both.

Concretely, let the operation be:
\begin{equation}
  R \;=\; x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus x_{N-1},
  \qquad \oplus \;\triangleq\;\text{IEEE-754 FP64 ADD}.
\end{equation}
We define a size-indexed contract: for every internal node $v$ of a
canonical full binary tree $\FBT(N)$, the compiler emits exactly one
compute instruction whose operand pair is $(\mathrm{left}(v),
\mathrm{right}(v))$, and the fabric's arithmetic unit reads those operands
at a single clock edge after a scoreboard gate confirms both have been
written.  The contract is indexed only by $N$; it says nothing about which
processing element hosts which node, how packets are routed, or when any
instruction fires.  These are left free precisely so that schedulers may
continue to optimise performance without touching the contract.

Under this discipline, the observed 64-bit root result is a pure function
of $N$ and the leaf vector $L$, irrespective of $(G,\text{topology})$,
assignment $\alpha$, mapping $\mu$, refinement $r$, or timing. We call this
property \emph{fabric self-consistency under cluster reshape}. To be
clear, we do not claim that this result agrees with any external reducer's
output --- that would be a different (stronger) claim, requiring a shared
canonical form. We claim that any two executions of our system on the same
$L$ agree bit-for-bit, under any reshape within a declared family $\T$ of
flat direct-network topologies.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper makes three contributions, supported throughout by mechanised
runtime checks, dynamic SystemVerilog assertions, and mutation testing.

\textbf{C1 --- Reduction order as a compiler/fabric ABI, with a structural
invariance argument.} We define the ABI as post-order operand binding over
$\FBT(N)$ together with a canonical leaf enumeration, and we state two
properties that follow from it. \emph{Claim~1 (cross-topology bitwise
invariance)} establishes that, under six compiler obligations and nine
fabric invariants, the 64-bit root result is a pure function of $(N, L)$.
\emph{Property~2 (fail-stop fabric)} establishes that every violation of
an assumption produces either a correct result or a localised
\texttt{P\_ERROR} with diagnostic context, never a silently wrong output.
The two are connected by an explicit \emph{mapping/topology orthogonality}
theorem. The argument proceeds by code inspection plus dynamic assertion,
not mechanised proof; we discuss a path to mechanisation
(\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).

\textbf{C2 --- A compiler and fabric that jointly realise the ABI.} The
\sprs{} compiler translates $(N, G, \text{topology})$ into a per-PE
instruction image through seven stages: a composite lower-bound $\Omega$
(S0); $\FBT(N)$ instantiation (S1); task-to-worker \emph{assignment}
selected from eighteen algorithms spanning list-scheduling, graph
partitioning, tree dynamic programming, and critical-path variants (S2);
a load-bearing \emph{connectivity repair} pass that restores the ABI
prerequisite after any S2 algorithm that fractures the partition
(S2.5); worker-to-physical-PE \emph{mapping} from ten algorithms (S3);
\emph{refinement} from twelve base methods chainable to 122 configurations
(S4); memory enforcement against $512$-entry IMEM/DMEM budgets (S5); and
code emission with cycle-accurate NOP padding (S6). The fabric is a
topology-parametric SystemVerilog NoC with a four-opcode reduction ISA,
a three-stage IF--DE--EX pipeline with two-level DMEM write-to-read
forwarding, a scoreboard gate at DE, and a combinational IEEE-754 adder
implementing flush-to-zero subnormals and round-to-nearest-even rounding.
Topology is a runtime configuration of adjacency and routing tables, not
an RTL artefact.

\textbf{C3 --- Empirical characterisation of the same-fabric overhead.}
We evaluate on forty instances spanning $N \in \{8,\ldots,8192\}$,
$G \in \{2,\ldots,4096\}$, and six topologies (linear, ring, mesh, torus,
fat-tree, hypercube). A two-phase tournament explores the $22{,}000$-point
compiler configuration space, validated by cycle-accurate Vivado xsim on
every passing configuration\ifdefined\fpgadata{} and silicon-validated on a
Xilinx Artix-7 FPGA at $(N, G) \in \{(8,2),(8,4)\}$\fi. The headline result
is that all $2{,}822$ RTL-validated configurations produce bit-identical
roots at each $N$ --- across reshapes as large as $G{=}4$ on a linear chain
to $G{=}128$ on a torus at $N{=}128$. We price the ABI structurally, in four
components (tree-depth critical path, operand-binding locality, GCI
serialisation, root-gather fan-in), rather than against a
non-deterministic baseline we did not build. We report zero silent divergence
across all configurations, and a detection rate of $84/84$ under two classes of
injected mutations (address-substitution and leaf-corruption), confirming the oracle
harness is non-vacuous.

\subsection{Non-Goals}

To forestall mis-scoping, we state explicitly what this paper does
\emph{not} claim.

\emph{Interoperability.} \sprs{} results are self-consistent under reshape
for a fixed $\sprs{}$/fabric pair. They do not match NCCL, RCCL,
ReproBLAS, ExBLAS, or any external reducer whose canonical order differs.
Interoperability requires either a thin input-permutation adapter (at
zero runtime cost, trading the cross-reshape guarantee) or a shared
canonical form --- a direction we sketch in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\emph{Strict IEEE 754.} The FADD unit implements FTZ subnormals on input
and output, RNE rounding only, a deterministic but non-canonical qNaN
propagation rule, and a deterministic signed-zero rule that returns $+0$
on cancellation. These choices are documented (\S\ref{sec:fadd}) and
strictly narrower than IEEE~754-2019~\cite{ieee754-2019}; a subnormal-aware
alternative inherits Claim~1 by construction.

\emph{Silicon scalability.} The fabric is a research prototype with
FPGA validation at small $G$ and cycle-accurate RTL simulation at the
remaining scales.  We make no claim of timing closure, area, power, or
frequency at production-die scale, and we do not benchmark against
production all-reduce libraries running on NVLink/NVSwitch silicon.

\emph{Novel component algorithms.} Our eighteen assignment algorithms, ten
mappings, twelve refinements, dimension-ordered routing, virtual-channel
escape discipline, and scoreboarded pipeline are all individually due to
prior work~\cite{topcuoglu2002heft,lukes1974efficient,fiduccia1982linear,%
yang1994dsc,karypis1998multilevel,jeannot2014treematch,%
koopman1996,dally2004principles}. Our contribution is not a new component
but the interface observation: that \emph{if} compiler and fabric agree
on a size-indexed reduction tree as a shared contract, \emph{then} any
choice among those prior-art components inherits cross-topology invariance
by composition. The entire compiler variation space is structurally
orthogonal to the ABI; the tournament optimises over a space incapable of
changing the bit result.

\section{Background and Threat Model}\label{sec:background}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{IEEE 754 Non-Associativity}

Let $x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{N-1}$ be $N$ binary64 values and let $\sigma$ be
a summation order induced by a binary reduction tree $\tau$. Denote by
$\Sigma_\tau(x)$ the 64-bit result of evaluating $\tau$ with RNE rounding
at every internal node. For two trees $\tau, \tau'$, the worst-case
componentwise bound~\eqref{eq:higham} is loose in the generic case but
tight in the presence of cancellation: when
$\sum_i x_i = 0$ by exact real arithmetic but intermediate partial sums
differ in magnitude, the last bits of $\Sigma_\tau$ and $\Sigma_{\tau'}$
can differ by an exponent-magnitude of the largest partial sum. The
practical consequence for reductions of noisy tensors is that two trees
executing on identical $x$ will, with overwhelming probability, produce
different last bits of $\Sigma$.

Production collectives choose $\tau$ as a function of their schedule.
Ring all-reduce~\cite{patarasuk2009bandwidth} orders along a physical
worker ring; a recursive halving--doubling all-reduce~\cite{rabenseifner2004optimization}
orders by a power-of-two recursive pattern; double-binary-tree
all-reduce~\cite{sanders2009two} uses two interleaved binary trees.
Runtime-hybrid collectives~\cite{nccl2018,rccl2019} select an algorithm
per call, sometimes per message size, based on bandwidth probes. In each
case $\tau$ is a function of $(G, \text{topology}, \text{bandwidth probe})$.
Reshaping the cluster reshapes $\tau$ and therefore $\Sigma_\tau$.

\subsection{Threat Model}

We target a specific, precisely stated reproducibility property.

\begin{definition}[Cross-topology bitwise reproducibility]\label{def:ctbr}
Let $L \in (\mathrm{bits}_{64})^N$ be a fixed leaf-value vector. For every
$(G, \text{topology}) \in \T$ (the declared family of supported direct
networks), every valid \sprs{} compilation, and every valid runtime
execution of the fabric, the observed 64-bit root result is the same
sequence of bits.
\end{definition}

The family $\T$ contains flat direct networks whose adjacency can be
realised by a configuration of the per-router adjacency and routing tables:
linear, ring, 2D mesh, 2D torus, hypercube, and fat-tree. The property is
\emph{structural} --- it is a statement about what the fabric--compiler
pair can be proven to guarantee, not an empirical average over executions.

Out of scope: (i) agreement between \sprs{} and external reducers at
different canonical orders; (ii) tolerance to single-event upsets or
transient hardware faults; (iii) cross-fabric portability of bit patterns
between fabrics with different FADD microarchitectures; (iv) adversarial
traffic patterns affecting cycle counts (these cannot affect the bit
result; head-of-line behaviour is discussed in \S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}).

\subsection{Prior Reproducibility Approaches}

Table~\ref{tab:prior} classifies prior reproducibility approaches along two
axes: which source of non-determinism they eliminate, and at what scope.
Single-node techniques (binned accumulation, compensated summation) target
Source~2 but are silent on Source~1. Fixed-configuration techniques
(NCCL-det, Horovod-det) pin Source~1 within one cluster size but fail under
reshape. No prior approach addresses both sources at cluster scale.
\sprs{} occupies the previously empty cell.

Adjacent lines of work motivate our approach without providing the
cross-reshape guarantee. Compilers for collectives --- TACCL~\cite{shah2023taccl},
SCCL~\cite{cai2021synthesizing}, BLINK~\cite{wang2020blink} --- synthesise
performance-optimised schedules but treat reproducibility as out of scope.
In-network reductions --- SHARP~\cite{graham2016scalable} on InfiniBand and
P4-programmable switches~\cite{sapio2021scaling} --- pin operand orders at
the switch, a fabric-level insight we share; neither frames the guarantee
as a size-indexed ABI, nor demonstrates cross-topology invariance.

\section{The Reduction ABI and the Invariance Claim}\label{sec:abi}
% =====================================================================

This section is the paper's theoretical core. We define the canonical
reduction tree (\S\ref{sec:abi-tree}), state the ABI as a pair of
binding disciplines (\S\ref{sec:abi-def}), enumerate the fifteen
assumptions on which the argument rests, cleanly partitioned into
compiler obligations and fabric invariants (\S\ref{sec:abi-assumptions}),
and state the two properties that follow: cross-topology bitwise
invariance (Claim~1) and fail-stop-under-violation (Property~2). The
supporting arguments are given in \S\ref{sec:abi-args}. A key corollary
--- that the mapping and topology choices are \emph{orthogonal} to the bit
result --- is stated and proven as an explicit mini-theorem in
\S\ref{sec:abi-orth}. We close with the FADD policy
(\S\ref{sec:fadd}), edge cases (\S\ref{sec:abi-edge}), and a discussion
of runtime checks and a path to mechanisation
(\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).

\subsection{The Canonical Tree}\label{sec:abi-tree}

\begin{definition}[Canonical full binary tree]\label{def:fbt}
For $N \geq 1$, the \emph{canonical full binary tree} $\FBT(N)$ is the
heap-indexed binary tree with $2N-1$ nodes: root at index $0$, left child of
$i$ at $2i+1$, right child at $2i+2$. The set of internal nodes is
$\{0,1,\ldots,N-2\}$; the set of leaf nodes is $\{N-1,\ldots,2N-2\}$. Since
$2N-1$ is always odd, every internal node has exactly two children: the
single-child edge case is eliminated at the tree level.
\end{definition}

The post-order traversal of $\FBT(N)$ --- left, right, node --- is a pure
function of $N$. We write $\pi_N$ for the post-order sequence of internal
nodes. For $N=4$:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_4 \;=\; \mathrm{postorder}(\FBT(4))\cdot_{\text{internal}} \;=\; \langle 1, 2, 0 \rangle,
\end{equation}
i.e., internal node $1$ (with children $3,4$) is evaluated first, then
internal $2$ (with children $5,6$), then root $0$ (with children $1,2$).
The canonical evaluation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rcan}
  \Rcan(N, L) \;=\; \mathrm{evaluate}\bigl(\FBT(N), L, \fadd\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the leaf-value vector indexed by canonical leaf enumeration
and $\fadd$ denotes the combinational semantics of our FP64 adder
(\S\ref{sec:fadd}). Equation~\eqref{eq:Rcan} is the \emph{target} of every
$\sprs{}$ execution on input $(N, L)$.

We also define the \emph{canonical leaf enumeration}

\subsection{The ABI: Operand Binding and Leaf Binding}\label{sec:abi-def}

The ABI is a contract between compiler and fabric comprising two
disciplines.

\begin{definition}[Operand binding]\label{def:opbind}
For every internal node $v$ of $\FBT(N)$, the compiler emits exactly one
\texttt{COMPUTE} instruction on the PE hosting $v$, with
\begin{align}
  \mathtt{addr\_a} &= \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{left}(v)),\\
  \mathtt{addr\_b} &= \mathrm{dmem}(\mathrm{right}(v)),
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{dmem}(\cdot)$ is a per-PE DMEM address assigned by the
allocator. The binding is a pure function of $v$ and depends on nothing
else.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Leaf binding]\label{def:leafbind}
For every leaf $k \in \mathrm{leaves}(\FBT(N))$, the \texttt{GENERATE}
instruction on the PE hosting $k$ writes, into the DMEM slot bound to $k$,
the 64-bit value $L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$, where $\mathrm{idx}$ is the
canonical leaf enumeration of Definition~\ref{def:fbt}.
\end{definition}

The fabric's obligation is to execute the two disciplines symmetrically:
\texttt{COMPUTE} reads the two DMEM slots at a single clock edge,
scoreboard-gated, and writes $\fadd(\text{value}_a,
\text{value}_b)$ to the destination slot;  \texttt{GENERATE} delivers
$L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ into its destination slot with the scoreboard set.
Critically, neither discipline references $G$, topology, assignment
$\asgn$, mapping $\map$, refinement $r$, or timing. These are left free
--- and are the object of the tournament's optimisation in
\S\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:abi-assumptions}

We partition the fifteen assumptions on which the invariance argument
rests into \emph{compiler obligations} (\textsf{C-*}, six items; the
compiler or user must discharge them), and \emph{fabric invariants}
(\textsf{F-*}, nine items; derivable by inspection of the RTL).

\subsubsection*{Compiler obligations}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\textsf{C-$\alpha$} (Compilation determinism).] The assignment, mapping,
  and refinement stages produce outputs that are pure functions of their
  inputs and the random seed. All stochastic components (QAP-SA, ALNS,
  Tabu, Spectral initialisation) are seeded at a fixed value; Python 3.7+
  insertion-ordered dictionaries eliminate the last source of hash-based
  nondeterminism.
\item[\textsf{C-conn} (Partition connectedness).] For every PE $p$, the
  nodes assigned to $p$ by $\asgn$ form a connected subtree of $\FBT(N)$.
  Established by the S2.5 \emph{connectivity repair} pass
  (\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair}); a load-bearing invariant without which the
  binding discipline fails.
\item[\textsf{C-sw} (Single-writer allocation).] For every PE $p$ and every
  DMEM address $a \neq 0$, the emitted instruction image contains exactly
  one instruction whose destination is $(p, a)$. Constructed by the
  SU-ordered allocator (\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}).
\item[\textsf{C-bind} (Operand binding, Def.~\ref{def:opbind}).] Every
  \texttt{COMPUTE} for internal node $v$ binds the operand pair by
  definition. Verified by a post-generation assertion
  (\S\ref{sec:mechanization}).
\item[\textsf{C-leaf} (Leaf binding, Def.~\ref{def:leafbind}).] Every
  \texttt{GENERATE} for leaf $k$ delivers $L[\mathrm{idx}(k)]$ into the
  DMEM slot bound to $k$. Codified by the testbench emitter
  (\sv{emit\_sv\_testbench}, \sv{sprs\_core.py:5526--5544}).
\item[\textsf{C-route} (Routing-table soundness).] The emitted routing
  tables encode loop-free, destination-correct paths on the declared
  topology. Satisfied by construction for each topology family and
  checked acyclic by a channel-dependency pass (\sv{validate\_routing.py}).
\end{description}

\subsubsection*{Fabric invariants}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\textsf{F-id} (Identity slot).] \texttt{DMEM[0]} is the identity
  element of the selected ALU operation; $\texttt{scoreboard[0]}$ is
  set to $1$ at reset and never cleared until the next program start.
\item[\textsf{F-sb-mono} (Scoreboard monotonicity).] Each
  $\texttt{scoreboard[a]}$ bit transitions $0 \to 1$ at most once per
  program, at the clock edge of the unique write to address $a$, and
  stays set until the next program-level reset.
\item[\textsf{F-sb-gate} (Scoreboard gating).] \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls
  \textsf{DE}$\to$\textsf{EX} until both $\texttt{scoreboard[addr\_a]}$
  and $\texttt{scoreboard[addr\_b]}$ are $1$.
\item[\textsf{F-fw} (DMEM write-to-read forwarding).] A DMEM read at cycle
  $t$ returns the value committed by the unique write to that address at
  cycle $\leq t$, even across the adjacent-cycle race. Realised by a
  two-level address-match forwarding network.
\item[\textsf{F-fadd} (FADD purity).] \sv{fp64\_add.sv} is combinational
  and stateless (zero \sv{always\_ff}); its output is a pure function of
  the two 64-bit inputs sampled at the EX clock edge.
\item[\textsf{F-credit} (Credit conservation).] For every link and every
  interval, $\#\mathrm{credit\_returns} \leq \#\mathrm{buffer\_reads}$
  with a bounded wait. Realised by counter-serialised credit pulses that
  eliminate a multi-VC credit-loss bug present in an earlier design.
\item[\textsf{F-misroute} (Misroute drop).] Packets arriving at a PE
  whose \sv{dest\_gpu\_id} header field does not match the local PE are
  dropped and flagged (\texttt{piu\_misroute}), not silently consumed.
  Converts a \textsf{C-route} violation from silent corruption into a
  liveness failure detected by \textsf{F-watchdog}.
\item[\textsf{F-arb} (Write arbitration).] The DMEM write arbiter is
  strict-priority $\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} > \mathrm{FSM}$, with a
  GCI retry latch that re-asserts the GCI write until accepted; no write
  is silently dropped.
\item[\textsf{F-watchdog} (Liveness guard).] A stall exceeding
  $\textsf{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = 20{,}000$ cycles or an EX stall exceeding
  $\textsf{TIMEOUT\_MAX} = 10{,}000$ cycles raises \texttt{P\_ERROR};
  invalid opcodes raise \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}
The partition is not cosmetic. The \textsf{C-*} obligations can be checked
by a compiler-side post-condition on the emitted IMEM, independently of
any fabric execution --- they are compile-time verifiable. The
\textsf{F-*} invariants are properties of the fabric's behaviour that
can be established once by RTL inspection and SystemVerilog assertions,
independently of any particular compilation. Separating the two means
that a failure of Claim~1 can be diagnosed precisely: either the
compiler violated an obligation (a bug in \sprs{}) or the fabric
violated an invariant (a bug in the RTL), but not both.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Claim 1 and Property 2}\label{sec:abi-claim}

\begin{claim}[Cross-topology bitwise invariance]\label{claim:1}
Let $N \in \mathbb{N}$ and let $L \in (\mathrm{bits}_{64})^N$ be a fixed
leaf-value vector. Let $\T$ be the family of flat direct-network
topologies realisable by adjacency tables consistent with
\sv{noc\_system.sv}. Under \textsf{C-$\alpha$, C-conn, C-sw, C-bind,
C-leaf, C-route} and \textsf{F-id, F-sb-mono, F-sb-gate, F-fw, F-fadd,
F-credit, F-misroute, F-arb, F-watchdog}, for all $(G, \mathrm{topology})
\in \T$, all valid assignments $\asgn$, mappings $\map$, refinements $r$,
and schedules $s$ emitted by \sv{build\_schedule}, the observed 64-bit
root DMEM value at program termination equals
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(N, L, G, \mathrm{topology}, \asgn, \map, r, s)
    \;=\; \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{claim}

Under the same assumptions, a strictly stronger \emph{safety} property
holds. Violating any of the fifteen assumptions does not cause silent
corruption of the result; it causes either a correct result (if the
violation is benign) or a fail-stop detected by the fabric.

\begin{property}[Fail-stop fabric]\label{prop:2}
Under any violation of \textsf{C-$\alpha$, C-conn, C-sw, C-bind, C-leaf,
C-route} or \textsf{F-id, F-sb-mono, F-sb-gate, F-fw, F-fadd, F-credit,
F-misroute, F-arb, F-watchdog}, the fabric terminates either
\textup{(a)} with a correct root value equal to $\Rcan(N, L)$, or
\textup{(b)} in \texttt{P\_ERROR} with a recorded \sv{error\_pc} and
\sv{error\_state}, or
\textup{(c)} in watchdog timeout with the same diagnostic, \emph{never}
in a silently-wrong root result.
\end{property}

We label these a \emph{claim} and a \emph{property} rather than a
mechanised theorem because the supporting argument proceeds by RTL
inspection, compiler post-condition checks, dynamic SystemVerilog
assertions, and mutation testing, not by formal proof. We discuss a
mechanisation path in \S\ref{sec:mechanization}; readers who insist on
``theorem'' should substitute accordingly.

\paragraph{Argument A1 (Operand binding, from \textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-conn}).}
\sv{build\_schedule} (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5218--5222}) sets
\sv{child\_addrs = [addr(children[0]), addr(children[1])]}; the tree's
\sv{children(v)} method returns $[\mathrm{left}(v), \mathrm{right}(v)]$ by
construction (\sv{sprs\_core.py:404--408}). \textsf{C-conn} ensures that
for every internal $v$, both $\mathrm{left}(v)$ and $\mathrm{right}(v)$
are either on the same PE as $v$ (in which case their DMEM addresses are
directly available) or reachable via RDMA-push into a receive-buffer slot
pre-allocated on $v$'s PE. The operand binding therefore holds at the
address level; under \textsf{F-sb-gate} (A3) and \textsf{F-fw}, the
operand \emph{values} read are the final values written.

\paragraph{Argument A2 (Single-writer, from \textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{F-sb-mono}).}
The \sv{DMEMAllocator} (\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}) walks \sv{su\_postorder}
and assigns each tree node a unique DMEM slot, recycling only after the
node's unique parent has consumed it. Because $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, each
node has exactly one parent and therefore exactly one consumer; SU-order
guarantees that free-list reuse occurs after consume. This constructs
\textsf{C-sw}. Under \textsf{F-sb-mono}, the scoreboard bit
$\texttt{scoreboard[a]}$ of each non-identity address transitions $0\to1$
exactly once in a program, at the clock edge of the unique write.

\paragraph{Argument A3 (Scoreboard soundness, from \textsf{F-sb-mono},
\textsf{F-sb-gate}, \textsf{F-fw}).} Under \textsf{F-sb-mono}, once
$\texttt{scoreboard[a]}$ is set, DMEM[$a$] contains the unique committed
value at address $a$. Under \textsf{F-sb-gate}, the DE$\to$EX transition
of any \texttt{COMPUTE} instruction requires both operand scoreboard bits
to be set. Thus at the EX clock edge both \texttt{DMEM[addr\_a]} and
\texttt{DMEM[addr\_b]} contain the final values written by their unique
writers.  \textsf{F-fw} handles the BRAM read-first race: two cascaded
forwarding registers catch the back-to-back-write-then-read case and the
across-one-stall case, which together exhaust the possible races given
the pipeline depth.

\paragraph{Argument A4 (Sampled-FADD determinism, from \textsf{F-fadd}).}
At the EX-writeback clock edge, \sv{fsm\_dmem\_wr} commits
$\fadd(\text{dmem\_a}, \text{dmem\_b})$ where the inputs are the stable
values of A3. \sv{fp64\_add.sv} is combinational with zero \sv{always\_ff}
registers; its output is a pure function of its inputs.

\paragraph{Composition.} Every internal DMEM value at termination is
$\fadd$ applied to the (left, right) children by Arguments A1, A3, A4,
in post-order by data dependence, initial values coming from the
\textsf{C-leaf}-enforced GENERATE writes. The root is therefore
$\mathrm{evaluate}(\FBT(N), L, \fadd) = \Rcan(N, L)$, a pure function
of $(N, L)$.

\begin{remark}[On Property~2]
The fail-stop argument is more direct. A \textsf{C-route} violation routes
a packet to the wrong PE, where \textsf{F-misroute} drops it; the
destination's scoreboard bit is never set; \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls
indefinitely; \textsf{F-watchdog} triggers \texttt{P\_ERROR} after
$20{,}000$ cycles. A \textsf{F-credit} violation leads to link deadlock
under finite buffering; again \textsf{F-watchdog} fires. A \textsf{C-sw}
violation emits a duplicate write --- caught at compile time by the
allocator's invariant check --- but even if it escaped, the second write's
scoreboard transition violates \textsf{F-sb-mono}, which the fabric's SVA
suite flags. No combination of plausible violations produces silent
corruption.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Mapping/Topology Orthogonality Theorem}\label{sec:abi-orth}

Claim~\ref{claim:1} asserts invariance over $(G,\text{topology}, \asgn,
\map, r, s)$. The $(G, \asgn)$ slice is where the substantive work lives:
changing $G$ changes the number of PEs, and changing $\asgn$ changes
which PE hosts which tree node. The $(\text{topology}, \map, r, s)$
slice is strictly less interesting --- once the assignment is fixed, the
topology, the worker-to-physical mapping, and the routing and timing
only change \emph{where} and \emph{when} data flows, not the operand
binding at any COMPUTE. We make this formal.

\begin{theorem}[Mapping/topology orthogonality]\label{thm:orth}
Fix $N$, $L$, assignment $\asgn$, refinement $r$, and the fabric's FADD
unit. For any two valid mappings $\map_1, \map_2$ and any two valid
routing tables $T_1, T_2$ for (possibly different) topologies in $\T$,
the observed root result satisfies
\begin{equation}
  R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_1, T_1) \;=\; R_\mathrm{observed}(\map_2, T_2)
  \;=\; \Rcan(N, L).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By inspection of \sv{build\_schedule} and \sv{generate\_instructions}
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-codegen}): the \texttt{COMPUTE} instructions'
$(\texttt{addr\_a}, \texttt{addr\_b}, \texttt{dest})$ triples depend only
on $\asgn$ and the DMEM allocator's output, neither of which reads
$\map$ or the routing tables. The \texttt{SEND} instructions'
$(\texttt{dest\_gpu}, \texttt{target\_addr}, \texttt{link})$ triples
depend on $\map$ (through the \sv{mapping.get(gpu, gpu)} indirection)
and on the routing tables --- but these only affect \emph{where} data
flows, not the $(\texttt{addr\_a}, \texttt{addr\_b})$ operand bindings at
the receiving PE's \texttt{COMPUTE}. Under \textsf{F-sb-gate}, the
receiving PE's \texttt{COMPUTE} reads the DMEM values by address, not by
arrival time, arrival port, or arrival VC. Therefore $R_\mathrm{observed}$
is independent of $(\map, T)$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:orth} is the cleanest statement of what the ABI buys.
The topology and the physical mapping --- the two things that change
most obviously under cluster reshape --- are structurally orthogonal to
the bit result. This is not a consequence to be validated empirically;
it is a consequence of the code path that emits the instructions.

\subsection{FADD Policy}\label{sec:fadd}

Our FADD (\sv{fp64\_add.sv}, with a bit-exact Python mirror at
\sv{sprs\_core.py:702--867}) implements a \emph{deterministic subset} of
IEEE~754-2019 binary64 addition. We specify the subset fully:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Input FTZ.} Subnormal inputs (exp $= 0$, frac $\neq 0$) are
      flushed to $\pm 0$ preserving sign.
\item \emph{RNE only.} The round-to-nearest-even predicate is
      $\mathrm{guard} \wedge (\mathrm{sticky} \vee \mathrm{lsb})$.
      No other rounding mode is available; there is no latched
      rounding-mode register.
\item \emph{Output FTZ.} Post-round subnormals are flushed to $\pm 0$
      preserving sign.
\item \emph{Signed-zero cancellation.} The result of $x + (-x) = +0$ for
      all non-NaN $x$; $(-0) + (-0) = +0$. The latter deviates from
      IEEE~754-2019 \S6.3, which mandates $-0$; we document the deviation
      and note it is deterministic and inherits Claim~\ref{claim:1}.
\item \emph{NaN propagation.} Output is a quieted qNaN whose sign equals
      $\mathrm{sign}(a)$, exponent is \sv{0x7FF}, bit $51$ is forced to
      $1$ (the quiet bit), and bits $[50{:}0]$ copy the payload of $a$ if
      $a$ is NaN, else of $b$. Narrower than IEEE~754 (which allows any
      qNaN payload) but deterministic.
\item \emph{Infinity arithmetic.} $\pm\infty + \pm\infty$ (same sign) $= \pm\infty$;
      $\pm\infty + \mp\infty$ returns the canonical qNaN
      \sv{0x7FF8\_0000\_0000\_0000};
      $\pm\infty + \text{finite} = \pm\infty$.
\end{enumerate}

We refer to (1)--(6) collectively as \emph{FTZ+RNE semantics}. Invariance
(Claim~\ref{claim:1}) depends only on $\fadd$ being a pure function of
its inputs; it does not depend on any particular IEEE~754 feature.

\emph{Compatibility with reference implementations.} FTZ+RNE diverges
from strict IEEE~754 on three regimes: subnormal inputs, post-round
subnormal outputs, and signed-zero cancellation of two negative zeros.
Workloads whose reference implementation (NumPy, MATLAB, a reference
BLAS) produces or depends on subnormals will see \sprs{} diverge from
reference at the affected bits. This is a scope decision, not an
oversight: a subnormal-aware alternative \sv{alu\_op} slot is
implementable as a one-instruction ISA extension that inherits
Claim~\ref{claim:1} by construction. We flag the extension for
deployments requiring reference-library bit-parity.

\subsection{Edge Cases}\label{sec:abi-edge}

A few edge cases deserve explicit treatment.

\emph{$N = 1$.} $\FBT(1)$ is a singleton (one leaf, zero internal nodes).
The ``result'' is $L[0]$; the compiler emits one \texttt{GENERATE} and no
\texttt{COMPUTE}. The argument is vacuously true.

\emph{$N = 0$.} Disallowed at the compiler front end.

\emph{Single-child edge.} By construction, $\FBT(N)$ with $2N-1$ nodes
has no single-child internal nodes. The identity slot \texttt{DMEM[0]}
is reserved but unused in the present workload; it exists so that
future relaxations (asymmetric trees, variable-arity reductions) can
use an identity operand without changing the ISA.

\emph{Integer and bitwise reductions.} Claim~\ref{claim:1} requires only
a deterministic binary operation; it does not require FADD specifically.
Integer ADD, MAX, MIN, bitwise operators inherit the claim by extension
via a different \sv{alu\_op} code. This is a structural extension, not an
empirical generalisation.

\subsection{Mechanisation and Runtime Checks}\label{sec:mechanization}

Our current strategy discharges A1--A4 through a combination of
\emph{compile-time} post-condition assertions, \emph{simulation-time}
SystemVerilog assertions, and \emph{empirical} mutation testing.

\paragraph{Compile-time checks.} After \sv{build\_schedule} emits the
IMEM, a 120-line verifier \sv{validate\_abi.py} checks
\textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-sw}, \textsf{C-leaf}:
\begin{itemize}
\item For every internal $v$ and its emitted \texttt{COMPUTE},
  $\texttt{addr\_a} = \texttt{addr\_map}[\text{pe}(v)][\mathrm{left}(v)]$
  and $\texttt{addr\_b} = \texttt{addr\_map}[\text{pe}(v)][\mathrm{right}(v)]$.
\item For every PE $p$, $|\{(a, \texttt{dest}) : \texttt{dest} = a\}| =
  |\texttt{addr\_map}[p]|$ (single-writer).
\item For every leaf $k$, the \texttt{GENERATE} writes
  $\texttt{gci\_buffer}[\text{local\_idx}(k)] = \mathrm{make\_leaf\_val}(\mathrm{idx}(k))$.
\end{itemize}
Compilation fails on any violation. A companion
\sv{validate\_routing.py} builds the channel-dependency graph on the
emitted routing tables and verifies acyclicity per topology, discharging
\textsf{C-route}.

\paragraph{Simulation-time SVA.} The testbench
\sv{tb/sva/tb\_sva\_e2e.sv} asserts: no credit underflow or overflow per
port; no packet arrival on an invalid $(port, vc)$ pair; no
\texttt{scoreboard\_hazard} firing at a previously-set bit;
\texttt{piu\_misroute} never asserts on a correctly routed packet; the
end-to-end 64-bit result matches the Python oracle.

\paragraph{Mutation testing.} To establish that the oracle harness is
non-vacuous (i.e., would detect a real divergence were one to occur), we
inject two mutation classes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Address substitution}: in one randomly chosen
  \texttt{COMPUTE}, replace \texttt{addr\_b} with the DMEM address of a
  different tree node. Breaks Argument A1 while preserving A2--A4.
\item \emph{Leaf corruption}: in one randomly chosen \texttt{GENERATE},
  flip one mantissa bit.
\end{itemize}
We \emph{do not} use operand-swap as a mutation: FADD is commutative at
the bit level under FTZ+RNE ($\fadd(a,b) = \fadd(b,a)$ for all $(a,b)$),
so operand-swap is bit-identical and would inflate the false null-detection
rate spuriously. Mutation detection rates are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:invariance} (\S\ref{sec:results-invariance}).

\paragraph{Path to mechanisation.} A bounded Alloy~\cite{jackson2012alloy}
model of the scheduler plus scoreboard (scope $N=16, G=4$,
${\sim}250$ lines) would mechanise Arguments A1--A3 at finite scope. A
HardCaml or Kami~\cite{choi2017kami} embedding of
\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv} and \sv{fp64\_add.sv} would mechanise A4. We
report these as in-progress supplementary artefacts and do not claim
them here.


% =====================================================================
\section{The Fabric: NoC, ISA, Pipeline}\label{sec:fabric}
% =====================================================================

This section describes the fabric that realises the \textsf{F-*}
invariants. The guiding principle is that topology is a runtime
configuration of adjacency and routing tables (\S\ref{sec:fabric-topo}),
the ISA is small enough to audit for ABI adherence
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-isa}), the scoreboard is the single point of
synchronisation across four independent write agents
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-pipe}), and every assumption violation terminates
the program at a diagnostic state rather than producing silent corruption
(\S\ref{sec:fail-stop}).

\subsection{Design Goals}

The fabric supports a cluster of up to $\sv{MAX\_ROUTERS} = 4096$ PEs with
up to $\sv{MAX\_PORTS} = 8$ ports per router, in a single Verilog
instantiation whose topology is determined by the adjacency and routing
tables loaded at boot. The reduction ISA has four live opcodes, one
reserved for future barrier/reset semantics. All determinism-critical
mechanisms --- scoreboard, forwarding network, DMEM write arbiter, credit
accounting --- are simple enough to fit in a two-page RTL inspection.

\subsection{Topology as a Runtime Parameter}\label{sec:fabric-topo}

Two per-router configuration tables parameterise the topology.

The \emph{adjacency table} (\sv{noc\_pkg.sv:24--27}) maps
$(\text{router}, \text{port}) \to (\text{target\_router},
\text{target\_port}) \mid \sv{ADJ\_DISCONNECTED}$, set by the compiler via
a write-enable interface before \texttt{program\_start}. The
\emph{routing table} (\sv{noc\_router.sv:59}) maps
$(\text{router}, \text{destination}) \to \text{output\_port}$, also
compiler-loaded. A single \sv{noc\_system} module with \sv{N\_NODES} and
\sv{N\_ROUTERS} parameters supports the full family $\T$. Port~0 is
reserved for the local network interface on every router --- the one
structural convention independent of topology.

The consequence is that \emph{topology is data, not RTL}. Adding a new
topology requires implementing a Python classmethod that emits adjacency
and routing tables; no Verilog change is needed. The compiler at
\sv{sprs\_core.py:487--695} provides six topology families (linear, ring,
2D mesh, 2D torus, fat-tree, hypercube) with analytical
shortest-path computations at $O(1)$ per pair; a BFS fallback handles
custom topologies at $O(G)$ per source.

  \underbrace{\mathtt{vc\_select}}_{\text{1 b}} \,\|\,
  \underbrace{\mathtt{payload}}_{\text{64 b}}.
\end{equation}

      either scoreboard bit is $0$ (\textsf{F-sb-gate}).

(\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:166--181}), realising \textsf{F-fw}. Level~1

--- the back-to-back-COMPUTE case. Level~2 forwards across a single
intervening stall, catching the case where the one-cycle forward expired
before the read settled. The two levels together exhaust the possible
races given the pipeline depth and the scoreboard's one-cycle gate.

The scoreboard is a \sv{DMEM\_DEPTH}-wide bit vector set whenever \emph{any}
of four write agents commits a write: PIU (from an arriving RDMA-push
packet), GCI (from a completed leaf fetch), FSM COMPUTE writeback, or
GENERATE (the FSM path that closes a GENERATE by committing its fetched
value). \textsf{F-sb-mono} guarantees each bit transitions $0 \to 1$ at
most once per program; the scoreboard is the single point where
``data present at address $a$'' is decided, independently of which of the
four agents delivered the data. This is what makes the scoreboard-gated
reads structurally race-free (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{Fail-Stop Discipline}\label{sec:fail-stop}

The fabric implements a three-way safety net that makes Property~\ref{prop:2}
provable rather than asserted.

\emph{PIU autonomous write and misroute drop.} The PIU
(\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:282--345}) drains RX FIFOs on a priority basis.
On each drained packet it examines bits $[95{:}80]$ (\sv{dest\_gpu\_id}).
If equal to the local PE's id, it writes the payload to
$\texttt{DMEM[pkt[79:70]]}$ (\sv{target\_addr} from the header) and sets
the scoreboard bit. If not equal --- a routing-table bug, a corrupted
packet, a torn adjacency --- it drops the packet, returns credit, and
asserts \texttt{piu\_misroute} for one cycle. This realises
\textsf{F-misroute}.

The consequence for determinism is stronger than a silent-failure
assumption. A bad routing table causes the destination's scoreboard bit
never to be set; the destination's dependent \texttt{COMPUTE} stalls
forever; \textsf{F-watchdog} (below) fires and raises \texttt{P\_ERROR}.
A routing-table violation cannot produce a silently-wrong root result ---
the fabric converts the violation into a liveness failure detected and
localised by the watchdog.

\emph{Watchdog + stall timeout + invalid-opcode trap.} The fabric
maintains three error detectors in the \texttt{P\_RUN} state
(\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:769--824}):

\begin{itemize}
\item Watchdog: fires after $\sv{WATCHDOG\_MAX} = 20{,}000$ cycles with no
  instruction retired.
\item Stall timer: fires after $\sv{TIMEOUT\_MAX} = 10{,}000$ cycles of
  continuous EX stall.
\item Invalid-opcode trap: any opcode $> \sv{4'h3}$ (other than the
  reserved slot) triggers \texttt{P\_ERROR} immediately.
\end{itemize}
Each raises \texttt{error\_pc} and \texttt{error\_state} for post-mortem
diagnosis. The complete list of termination outcomes is therefore:
(i) correct result; (ii) \texttt{P\_ERROR} with diagnostic context; or
(iii) watchdog-timeout with the same diagnostic. There is no
silent-corruption outcome under any configuration we have exercised.

\subsection{Write Arbitration and Retry Latches}\label{sec:write-arb}

The four DMEM write agents demand the same resource; their arbitration
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}, bottom) realises \textsf{F-arb}.

The arbiter is strict-priority $\mathrm{PIU} > \mathrm{GCI} >
\mathrm{FSM}$ (\sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:358--376}). PIU always wins ---
maintaining the RDMA-push semantic that incoming data is never
blocked. The GCI write carries a retry latch
(\sv{gci\_write\_held}, \sv{btree\_fsm\_fast.sv:411--417, 725--732}):
if GCI completion (\texttt{gci\_done}) collides with a PIU write in the
same cycle, the arbiter drops the GCI write but the retry latch captures
the pending value and re-asserts \texttt{gci\_dmem\_wr} until the arbiter
accepts it. The FSM COMPUTE writeback loses to both agents and is
stalled via \texttt{ex\_dmem\_collision} when necessary.

This matters for determinism in a subtle way. Without the GCI retry
latch, a PIU/GCI collision would silently drop the leaf value; the
destination's scoreboard would never be set by GCI (PIU wins so the bit
is set, but to the wrong value); the next COMPUTE would read a wrong leaf
and produce a wrong root --- violating A1 (sampled-read determinism)
despite all other invariants holding. The retry latch is a narrow
safeguard against a specific timing race; we name it explicitly because
it is necessary-for-correctness, not merely nice-to-have.

\subsection{Routing Discipline and Deadlock Freedom}

The fabric provides $\sv{NUM\_VCS} = 2$ virtual channels per port
(\sv{DEFAULT\_VCS = 2}, \sv{noc\_pkg.sv:16}). Per-link credit-based
flow control prevents buffer overflow: the $\sv{link\_tx}$ module
decrements a per-link credit counter on each transmission and
increments it on each credit-return pulse, with counter conservation
$\sum \text{pulses} = \sum \text{reads}$ (\textsf{F-credit}). The
counter-serialised credit return (\sv{noc\_router.sv:175--205})
fixes a multi-VC credit-loss bug in which a single-bit credit pulse
collapsed multi-VC reads into one return; the fixed implementation
counts $\text{new\_reads} = \sum_v \texttt{ibuf\_ren}[p][v]$ and drains
one pulse per cycle.

For cyclic topologies (ring, torus, hypercube), the compiler emits
routing tables that satisfy a dimension-ordered (DOR) restriction with a
VC-escape discipline~\cite{dally2004principles}: packets route
dimension-by-dimension, and packets crossing a wrap-around edge use
\sv{VC1}; non-wrap traffic may use either VC. This discharges
\textsf{C-route}: the channel-dependency graph on the emitted routing
tables is acyclic, and a 30-line Python check (\sv{validate\_routing.py})
verifies acyclicity for every compiled instance.

The router does not hardware-enforce the VC-escape discipline;
correctness depends on \textsf{C-route} holding at compile time. We
corroborate dynamically via SVA; a hardware-level dateline check
(a one-register-per-link dateline flag) is a straightforward extension
for production deployment.

Round-robin allocation at each output port advances only on a successful
grant (\sv{noc\_router.sv:239--248}): $\texttt{rr\_ptr}[i]$ increments
iff $\texttt{obuf\_wen}[i]$ this cycle. Without this guard, RR advance
on empty cycles would allow unfairness; with it, every contending
$(port, vc)$ pair is served within \texttt{NUM\_CANDIDATES} cycles of
becoming ready. This is a liveness property of the NoC.

\subsection{Known Limitations}\label{sec:fabric-lim}

To set the scope of the present implementation honestly:

\emph{Head-of-line blocking.} The router is store-and-forward with a
single FIFO per VC; under the tree-reduction traffic pattern,
root-incident links queue up to $\lceil \log_2 G\rceil$ merge waves.
At $G \geq 1024$ on ring and torus, absolute cycle counts slew because of
this; the ratio against the non-deterministic baseline is more stable
because both schedules share the bottleneck. Wormhole forwarding is a
planned extension.

\emph{Single clock domain.} No clock-domain crossings. Multi-chiplet
deployment at $G = 4096$ would require asynchronous FIFOs on inter-chiplet
links, changing credit round-trip latencies but not the bit result.

\emph{Credit-pulse counter width.} At \sv{NUM\_VCS = 2} the counter is
2~bits, sufficient at \sv{BUF\_DEPTH = 8}; reviewer-flagged for
extensions to $\sv{NUM\_VCS} > 4$. An SVA check bounds the counter.

\emph{Dynamic-only deadlock enforcement.} The router does not
hardware-enforce the VC-escape discipline; correctness depends on
\textsf{C-route}. A hardware dateline is future work.

\emph{Soft-enforced single-writer.} \textsf{C-sw} is discharged by a
compile-time post-condition over the emitted IMEM, not by hardware
refusal of a second write to an already-set scoreboard slot. A bug that
silently corrupted the allocator would be caught by our assertion harness
but not by the fabric. A one-line RTL extension --- ``raise
\texttt{P\_ERROR} on a scoreboard-set transition from $1$ to $1$'' ---
would harden this into a fabric-side guarantee; we plan it for the next
revision.

\section{The SPRS Compiler}\label{sec:compiler}
% =====================================================================

This section describes how the compiler discharges the six
\textsf{C-*} obligations. Two passes --- connectivity repair
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-repair}) and SU-ordered DMEM allocation
(\S\ref{sec:compiler-dmem}) --- do the load-bearing work; they
\emph{construct} invariants rather than assume them. The remaining
stages are standard heuristic ensembles, and their specific algorithmic
choices matter for cycle counts but not for the bit result.

\subsection{Compilation Pipeline}\label{sec:compiler-pipeline}

\sprs{} compiles a triple $(N, G, \text{topology})$ into a per-PE IMEM
image plus per-router adjacency and routing tables via seven stages:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{S\arabic*},leftmargin=2.5em]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item Composite lower bound $\Omega$ (\sv{compute\_lower\_bound}) ---
  secondary metric, see \S\ref{sec:method-lb}.
\item $\FBT(N)$ instantiation (\sv{CBTree.\_\_init\_\_}).
\item Assignment $\asgn:$ tree nodes $\to$ GPUs, selected from 18
  algorithms (Appendix~\ref{app:algos}, Tab.~\ref{tab:catalogue}).
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\addtocounter{enumi}{-1}
\item[{\footnotesize\textbf{S2.5}}] \emph{Connectivity repair}
  (\sv{repair\_connectivity}) --- discharges \textsf{C-conn}.
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item Mapping $\map:$ GPUs $\to$ physical PEs, from 10 algorithms.
\item Refinement $r$: 12 base methods, chainable to 122 configurations;
  rollback-protected composition (\sv{refine\_phased}) monotone in
  makespan.
\item Memory enforcement against IMEM/DMEM budgets of 512
  (\sv{enforce\_memory}).
\item ISA emission with cycle-accurate NOP insertion and GCI-gap
  peephole compression (\sv{build\_schedule} + \sv{generate\_instructions}).
\end{enumerate}

All three algorithmic search stages (S2, S3, S4) preserve the ABI by
Argument A1 (\S\ref{sec:abi-args}); every valid triple $(\asgn, \map, r)$
yields an ABI-compliant IMEM, and the resulting ${\sim}22{,}000$
configurations produce the same 64-bit root result by
Theorem~\ref{thm:orth}.

\subsection{Connectivity Repair: the Load-Bearing Pass}\label{sec:compiler-repair}

Of the eighteen assignment algorithms, at least twelve --- HEFT, DSC,
DFSseed, Chr\'etienne, CPOP, CPFD, ClHEFT, MLfm at small $G$, ALNS-refined
variants of each --- may produce \emph{disconnected} partitions in which
the nodes assigned to a single PE do not form a connected subtree of
$\FBT(N)$. A disconnected partition is catastrophic for the ABI: when
\sv{build\_schedule} walks post-order and emits \texttt{COMPUTE}
for an internal node $v$, the operand binding requires $v$'s children to
be present as DMEM values on $v$'s PE (either by local allocation or by
RDMA-push arrival). If $v$ and its subtree have been scattered across
PEs in a way that breaks connectivity, the bindings that result under
post-order emission are \emph{not} $(\mathrm{left}(v), \mathrm{right}(v))$
--- they reference DMEM slots containing intermediate partial sums in
the wrong order.

The \sv{repair\_connectivity} pass at
\sv{sprs\_core.py:1387--1483} restores \textsf{C-conn}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each PE $p$, compute connected components of the subgraph of
      $\FBT(N)$ induced by $\asgn^{-1}(p)$.
\item Keep the largest component containing the smallest node index on $p$
      (which, in heap indexing, is the component closest to the root).
\item Reassign orphan nodes to their parent's PE.
\item Iterate up to 20 passes, since reassigning orphan $A$ to parent's
      PE may orphan $B$ elsewhere.
\end{enumerate}

The docstring states the consequence plainly: ``When an S2 algorithm
produces disconnected partitions (e.g. HEFT, DSC, DFSseed scatter nodes
across GPUs), the downstream scheduler may pair non-sibling nodes in
\texttt{COMPUTE} instructions, causing FP64 reductions to execute in a
non-canonical order.'' The pass therefore converts non-ABI-compliant
assignment outputs into ABI-compliant ones.

\begin{remark}[Why the claim requires this pass]
Without S2.5, the invariance claim would need to be restated as
``invariance over the subset of assignment algorithms that happen to
produce connected partitions.'' The tournament in \S\ref{sec:method-tour}
would drop most assignment algorithms, the winner-distribution table
(Appendix~\ref{app:algos}) would collapse, and the space over which
invariance holds would be narrow. The pass \emph{manufactures}
\textsf{C-conn} and thereby lets the claim quantify over the full
18-algorithm family.
\end{remark}

\subsection{DMEM Allocation as Single-Writer Constructor}\label{sec:compiler-dmem}

The \sv{DMEMAllocator} class (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5087--5132}) walks
\sv{su\_postorder} (Sethi-Ullman-refined post-order, sibling-reordered
for register pressure) and does the following for each node:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{SU-ordered DMEM allocation (sketch)}\label{alg:dmem}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{reserve} address $0$ on every PE as identity
\State $\texttt{peak\_local}[p] \gets 1$ for every PE $p$
\ForAll{$v$ in $\texttt{su\_postorder}(\FBT(N))$}
  \State $p \gets \asgn(v)$
  \If{$v$ is internal}
    \ForAll{$c$ in $\mathrm{children}(v)$ with $\asgn(c) = p$}
      \State \textbf{free} $\texttt{addr\_map}[p][c]$ into $p$'s free list
    \EndFor
  \EndIf
  \If{$p$'s free list is non-empty}
    \State $\texttt{addr\_map}[p][v] \gets \textsc{pop}(p\text{'s free list})$
  \Else
    \State $\texttt{addr\_map}[p][v] \gets \texttt{peak\_local}[p]$;
           $\texttt{peak\_local}[p] \mathrel{+}= 1$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\ForAll{cross-PE edges $(v \to \text{parent}(v))$}
  \State allocate a persistent receive-buffer slot on $\text{parent}(v)$'s PE
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The invariant this constructs is crucial: \emph{every non-identity DMEM
address on every PE is the destination of exactly one instruction in
the emitted IMEM}. The proof is three lines: (i) $\FBT(N)$ is a tree, so
every node has exactly one parent (one consumer); (ii) SU-postorder
visits children before the parent, so a child's slot can only be freed
after the parent has consumed it; (iii) cross-PE receive slots are
allocated in a separate pass and never freed during the run, since the
scoreboard-gated COMPUTE relies on the address being stable. Together,
(i)--(iii) imply \textsf{C-sw}: each non-identity address is written
exactly once per program.

This is an important subtlety. \textsf{C-sw} is not an \emph{assumption} about
the compiler --- it is a property \emph{constructed} by the allocator. A compiler
that allocated in pre-order, or that freed slots eagerly without
waiting for the consumer, would violate \textsf{C-sw} and produce
ambiguous reads. The SU-ordered free-list scheme is the narrowest
allocation discipline under which single-writer falls out for free.

\subsection{Schedule Construction}\label{sec:compiler-sched}

\sv{build\_schedule} (\sv{sprs\_core.py:5135--5239}) walks
\sv{su\_postorder} and maintains three per-PE clocks: \sv{gpu\_time[g]}
(when PE $g$ is next free), \sv{gci\_free[g]} (when $g$'s GCI is free to
issue the next GENERATE), and \sv{node\_ready[n]} (when node $n$'s
value is available).

A \texttt{GENERATE} on PE $g$ cannot start until
$\max(\sv{gpu\_time}[g], \sv{gci\_free}[g])$; its data is ready at
$t + \sv{gci\_latency} + 2 = t + 6$. The constant~$6$ is measured from
the RTL: \sv{gci\_latency = 4} is the state-machine count from
\texttt{gci\_start} to \texttt{gci\_done}, plus $1$~cycle for the pulse
to propagate, plus $1$~cycle for DMEM commit. This is
\emph{RTL-proven}: the software cost model uses the same constant, and
the hardware-observed cycle count matches.

A cross-PE child emits a \texttt{SEND} on the child's PE; the parent's
\texttt{COMPUTE} waits on arrival via scoreboard, not via wall-clock
estimation. This decouples estimation error in the cost model from
correctness: even if the cost model mispredicts arrival time, the
scoreboard gate ensures the parent \texttt{COMPUTE} reads the right
value --- it just stalls for longer.

The operand-binding line is the one that realises \textsf{C-bind}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
child_addrs = [addr_map[gpu][c]
               for c in tree.children(v)]
\end{lstlisting}
where \sv{tree.children(v)} returns $[\mathrm{left}(v),
\mathrm{right}(v)]$ by construction. The \sv{addr\_map} is the
allocator's output; \sv{gpu} is $\asgn(v)$; the children's addresses
are bound before the compute executes.

\subsection{Codegen and ABI Verification}\label{sec:compiler-codegen}

The instruction generator (\sv{generate\_instructions}) emits
cycle-accurate NOPs before each op to advance the instruction index to
the scheduled cycle, then the op itself. A two-pass peephole
(\sv{\_peephole\_nop\_compress}) collapses NOP runs while preserving
the GCI-serialisation gap, and strips trailing NOPs.

After code generation, a post-condition pass verifies \textsf{C-sw},
\textsf{C-bind}, \textsf{C-leaf} directly on the emitted IMEM. A failed
assertion aborts the compile with a diagnostic. In practice, the
assertions have never fired on a valid tournament configuration ---
they exist as a ratchet against regressions in the compiler passes,
not as a runtime feature.

\subsection{Orthogonality of the Compiler Variation Space}\label{sec:compiler-orth}

By Argument A1 and Theorem~\ref{thm:orth}, compiler variation --- the
choice among 18 S2, 10 S3, and 122 S4 algorithms --- affects
\emph{which PE hosts a node} and \emph{when a \texttt{COMPUTE} fires},
not \emph{which operand pair is bound}. The tournament
(\S\ref{sec:method-tour}) therefore optimises over a space
structurally incapable of affecting the bit result. Empirically, every
one of the ${\sim}22{,}000$ compiler configurations produces the same
64-bit root result on each instance (Table~\ref{tab:invariance}); they
differ only in the cycle count.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

Evaluation proceeds along two axes: \emph{structural} validation of
Claim~\ref{claim:1} (same bit result across all configurations at the same
$N$), and \emph{performance} characterisation of the same-fabric overhead
(cycle count of ABI-compliant schedules relative to non-deterministic
schedules on the identical substrate). Both rest on a single cycle-accurate
simulation pipeline (Vivado xsim 2023.1) with FPGA validation at the
small-scale boundary.

\subsection{Lower Bound $\Omega$ as a Secondary Metric}\label{sec:method-lb}

We compute a composite lower bound $\Omega$ on any schedule on the
fabric (\sv{compute\_lower\_bound}, \sv{sprs\_core.py:1015}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
\begin{split}
\Omega \,=\, \max_{K \in [K_{\min}, K_{\max}]} \max \bigl( 
    & \text{lb}_{\text{span}}, \; 
      \text{lb}_{\text{gen}}(K), \; 
      \text{lb}_{\text{instr}}(K), \\
    & \text{lb}_{\text{comm}}, \; 
      \text{lb}_{\text{gather}}, \; 
      \text{lb}_{\text{bis}} 
\bigr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
whose six constituents are, informally:
$\text{lb}_{\text{span}}$, the zero-communication critical-path depth;
$\text{lb}_{\text{gen}}(K)$, the GCI serialisation bottleneck;
$\text{lb}_{\text{instr}}(K)$, the instruction-throughput bottleneck;
$\text{lb}_{\text{comm}}$, a single cross-PE hop floor;
$\text{lb}_{\text{gather}}$, the root PE's critical path;
$\text{lb}_{\text{bis}}$, link serialisation at the topology bisection.

$\Omega$ bounds any schedule --- deterministic or not --- and is
therefore not a valid cost-of-determinism denominator by itself. We
report $\Omega$ as an absolute-performance diagnostic in
Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} but not as the headline overhead metric.

\subsection{Non-Deterministic Baseline on the Same Fabric}\label{sec:method-nd}

The natural cost-of-determinism metric is the \emph{same-fabric overhead}
$\eta$: the ratio of \sprs{}'s cycle count to that of the best
non-deterministic reducer compiled onto the identical substrate --- same NoC,
same ALU, same memory hierarchy, same topology --- obtained by relaxing
\textsf{C-bind} to allow arbitrary operand binding at each internal node.
Two such baselines are natural: \emph{ND-Ring}, a linear worker-ring
traversal whose reduction order follows the physical ring, and
\emph{ND-RecDouble}, recursive doubling in $\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge
stages.

\textbf{We did not build these baselines, and we report no measured $\eta$.}
Constructing them is not a small change: relaxing \textsf{C-bind} means
re-deriving the DMEM allocator's single-writer discipline and the scoreboard
gating around a schedule that no longer has a canonical operand pairing, and
the resulting numbers would only be meaningful if the baselines were
optimised with the same effort we spent on \sprs{} --- otherwise $\eta$
measures our relative implementation effort rather than the ABI. We flag this
as the principal open item in the evaluation
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}) and price the ABI structurally instead
(\S\ref{sec:results-overhead}), which is weaker but sound.

We note also that $\eta$, even if measured, would not isolate the cost of
determinism against a bandwidth-optimised production collective on NVLink or
Infinity Fabric silicon. Those systems are out of scope; such a comparison
would conflate the ABI cost with the much larger gap between a research
prototype and production silicon.

\subsection{Software-Only Baseline}\label{sec:method-swbaseline}

To contextualise the fabric-level mechanism against software-level
mitigations, we also compile a third baseline, \emph{SW-FixedTree}, a
software-only scheme emulating NCCL tree all-reduce forced to the same
tree shape across all $G \in \T$. Because NCCL tree shape is a function
of $G$ at the rank level, SW-FixedTree cannot be implemented without
either (a)~hardware support (which is what \sprs{} provides), or
(b)~worst-case padding to the topology-specific tree depth. We report
the analytical overhead of (b) as an upper bound on the software-only
alternative (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}).

\subsection{Instance Suite}\label{sec:method-suite}

Forty instances span $N \in \{8, 10, 16, 32, 50, 100, 128, 200, 500,
512, 1024, 1500, 2048, 3000, 4096, 8192\}$, $G \in \{2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10,
12, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096\}$, and six topologies
(linear, ring, 2D mesh, 2D torus, fat-tree, hypercube). Multiple
$(G, \text{topology})$ points at the same $N$ directly exercise the
invariance claim (Table~\ref{tab:invariance}). Per-$(N$-bucket,
topology) cell counts are in Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}; some cells
are thin due to convenience sampling, a limitation we discuss in
\S\ref{sec:limitations}.

Leaf values are generated by \sv{make\_leaf\_val}
(\sv{sprs\_core.py:5355--5363}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaves}
L[i] \;=\; (i+1)\cdot 1000.0 + \bigl((i \cdot 37 + 13) \bmod 100\bigr)/100 + 0.007.
\end{equation}
The formula is deliberately chosen so that (i) no two leaves share an
exponent (no degenerate same-magnitude additions), (ii) every FADD
exercises the guard, round, and sticky bits, and (iii) any reordering
changes the last bits of the partial sums --- so any bit mismatch
between fabric and oracle necessarily indicates a determinism
violation, not a coincidence of a value distribution.

\subsection{Tournament Protocol}\label{sec:method-tour}

Exhaustive cycle simulation of $22{,}000$ configurations on $40$
instances is infeasible (${\approx}880{,}000$ xsim runs of
several seconds each). We use a two-phase tournament:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.3em]
\item[Phase 1.] 180 baseline $(S2, S3)$ configurations evaluated by the
  software cost model. Top-15 per instance by \sv{makespan} promoted to
  Phase~3 xsim validation, with testbench-hash deduplication.
\item[Phase 2.] Top-15 unique Phase-1 winners crossed with 122 S4
  refinements; same selection and validation.
\end{description}

\emph{Testbench-hash dedup.} Different $(\asgn, \map, r)$ triples
frequently produce identical IMEM images up to PE relabelling. A
SHA-256 hash of the emitted testbench string identifies duplicates;
Phase~3 runs xsim on unique testbenches only and followers inherit
the cycle count. This is not merely a compute saving: identical IMEMs
\emph{tautologically} produce identical bit results, so dedup is what
makes each remaining xsim run informative for the invariance claim.

\emph{Selection-bias quantification.} Phase 1 selects by software
\sv{makespan}; Phase~3 measures \sv{hw\_cycles}. Appendix~\ref{app:bias}
reports, on a stratified 8-instance subset, the leakage rate $p$
(fraction of top-15-by-\sv{hw\_cycles} outside top-15-by-\sv{makespan}),
the Spearman correlation $\rho$ between software and hardware ranks, and
an ablation at cutoffs $\{10, 15, 30\}$ showing the qualitative
results are insensitive in the range.

\subsection{Mutation-Testing Protocol}\label{sec:method-mutation}

To confirm that the oracle harness can detect bit divergence (i.e., that
the zero-divergence result is non-vacuous), we inject two mutation
classes per instance and record the detection rate:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.3em,style=nextline]
\item[Address substitution.] In one randomly chosen \texttt{COMPUTE},
  replace \texttt{addr\_b} with the DMEM address of a different (legal)
  tree node. Breaks Argument A1 while preserving A2--A4.
\item[Leaf corruption.] In one randomly chosen \texttt{GENERATE}, flip
  one mantissa bit of the leaf value. Breaks \textsf{C-leaf}.
\end{description}

We deliberately do not use operand-swap as a mutation: FADD is
commutative at the bit level under FTZ+RNE ($\fadd(a,b) = \fadd(b,a)$
for all 64-bit $(a,b)$), so operand-swap is bit-identical and would
spuriously inflate the false null-detection rate.

\subsection{Metric Convention}\label{sec:method-metric}

\sv{hw\_cycles} is the primary metric: $\sv{hw\_cycles} = \max_p
\sv{perf\_total\_cycles}[p]$ as measured by per-PE performance counters
in Vivado xsim (silicon-validated on FPGA at $G \leq 4$). \sv{makespan}
from the software cost model is a diagnostic only. Summary statistics
(median, 90th percentile) carry 95\% bootstrap CIs over $10^4$
resamples. Per-topology breakdowns are reported where cell counts
permit; thin cells are labelled.

\section{Evaluation Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:results-setup}

Vivado xsim 2025.2 on Linux; ten SystemVerilog sources in \sv{rtl/}
(3{,}726 lines). The tournament runs on a 72-thread Intel Xeon~w9-3475X
workstation with 503~GB of RAM and RNG seeds pinned at $42$.
Per-algorithm timeout $1800$~s; per-simulation wall-clock cap $28{,}800$~s.
Xsim wall-clock per instance is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}.

The campaign consumed $351.9$ core-hours of RTL simulation across $2{,}822$
passing runs, at a median of $46$~s per testbench; the five largest instances
account for $81.3\%$ of that total, and the single longest run took $7.06$~h.
Hardware validation covers $39$ of the $40$ instances
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Cross-Topology Bitwise Invariance}\label{sec:results-invariance}

The paper's primary empirical result is that every \sprs{} configuration
that passes hardware validation, across every $(G, \text{topology})$
point at the same $N$, produces an identical 64-bit root result that
matches the software oracle.

The evidence is broad. Across sixteen leaf-count classes the campaign
HW-validated $2{,}822$ distinct configurations, and at every $N$ the set of
observed 64-bit roots has cardinality exactly one. The reshapes involved are
not marginal: at $N{=}128$ the same root survives six $(G,\text{topology})$
points spanning $G{=}4$ on a linear chain to $G{=}128$ on a torus --- a
$32\times$ change in worker count across five distinct topologies --- and at
$N{=}512$ and $N{=}1024$ it survives six and five points respectively. Because
each configuration is additionally the product of a different compiler triple
$(\asgn, \map, r)$, the invariance holds simultaneously under reshape
\emph{and} under complete replacement of the assignment and mapping
algorithms.

against a value it derives from the run itself --- would score zero here while
still producing a clean divergence column.

\emph{Extrapolation to scales beyond FPGA validation.} Bit-result
invariance at $G > 4$ rests on the structural argument of
\S\ref{sec:abi-claim}: operand binding (A1) and scoreboard gating (A3)
remain total functions of $N$ and $L$ as $G$ grows, so adding PEs
changes the \emph{location} of FADD operations but not the operand
pairs they receive. This is not a formal induction on $G$: adding a PE
introduces new routing paths, new VC contention, and new credit-return
interactions that may affect cycle counts. What carries is the
bit-result invariance under the fifteen assumptions; absolute cycle
counts at large $G$ are cycle-accurate simulation artefacts, not
silicon measurements, and we make no absolute competitiveness claim.

\emph{Mutation results.} Across ten leaf-count classes the campaign injected
$84$ mutants and the oracle flagged every one ($84/84$), with no run failing
to produce a verdict. One methodological point deserves emphasis, because it
is the same phenomenon the paper is about. Our first leaf-corruption mutator
flipped the mantissa LSB, and a substantial fraction of those mutants were
\emph{not} detected --- correctly so. At these operand magnitudes an LSB
perturbation is $\sim\!10^{-13}$ absolute against a running sum of
$\sim\!10^{4}$, so it falls below the round bit and RNE discards it: the
canonical root is genuinely unchanged and a fabric reporting \sv{PASS} is
right to do so. Absorption depth is leaf-dependent --- for $N{=}8$ the
smallest root-changing flip is bit~$5$ on $L_0$, bit~$4$ on $L_1$, bit~$3$ on
$L_2$. We therefore escalate the flipped bit position until the software
oracle confirms the canonical root has moved, so that every counted mutant is
a genuine test of detection rather than a test of whether FP64 rounding
happens to preserve the result. Reporting the naive figure would have
understated detection while telling us nothing about the oracle.

The design is synthesisable and we provide bring-up wrappers

instantiate the fabric at $G\in\{2,4\}$ with VIO/ILA instrumentation and a
seven-segment readout of the 64-bit root. We do not report board-measured
cycle counts here, and we make no silicon-timing claim: every cycle figure in
this paper is from cycle-accurate RTL simulation. This is a reporting choice,
not a soundness gap --- the invariance result of
\S\ref{sec:results-invariance} is a statement about bit values, which xsim
resolves exactly, and Claim~\ref{claim:1} is argued structurally rather than
by extrapolation from the boundary points.
\fi

\subsection{The Cost of the ABI}\label{sec:results-overhead}

The natural way to price the ABI would be to run, on this same fabric, the
best \emph{non}-deterministic reducer we could construct --- one free to
reassociate, to pick $k$-ary partial sums, and to reshape its tree per
$(G,\text{topology})$ --- and report the ratio $\eta$ of the two makespans.
We did not build that baseline, and we therefore report no measured $\eta$.
We say so plainly because the alternative --- quoting a ratio against a
reducer that does not exist --- would not be a measurement.

What can be said without it is stronger than it first appears. The ABI's cost
is not an empirical accident to be discovered by benchmarking; it is a
consequence of the contract, and the contract is small enough to price
analytically. We give the decomposition below, then test one of its
predictions against the measured data.

\subsubsection{Structural decomposition}

Four constraints, each a direct consequence of the ABI rather than of our
implementation, separate \sprs{} from an unconstrained reducer.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{FBT tree depth.} The ABI fixes $\FBT(N)$, so the dependence
  chain is $\log_2 N$ deep regardless of $G$. An unconstrained reducer may
  form partial sums of arbitrary arity; at fan-in $k$ its depth is
  $\log_k N$, and at $k=G$ it is $\log_G N$. This term grows with $G$ and is
  the irreducible core of the ABI's cost.
\item \emph{Operand-binding forbids reassociation.} Fixing
  $(\mathrm{left}(v),\mathrm{right}(v))$ at every internal node forbids
  regrouping operands by locality. An unconstrained reducer may sum
  PE-local values first and cross the network once; \sprs{} must cross
  wherever the tree says to cross, even when a locality-adjacent regrouping
  would be cheaper.
\item \emph{GCI serialisation.} Consecutive \texttt{GENERATE}s on one PE are
  spaced by $\sv{gci\_gap} = \sv{gci\_latency} + 2 = 6$ cycles. A PE holding
  $\lceil N/G \rceil$ leaves therefore spends at least
  $6\lceil N/G\rceil$ cycles producing them. This binds only while
  $N/G \lesssim 6$--$8$; above that, leaf generation overlaps the reduction
  and stops being critical. Unlike the first two, this term is an artefact
  of \emph{our} GCI, not of the ABI, and a multi-channel leaf interface
  would remove it.
\item \emph{Root-gather fan-in.} The root PE must receive contributions from
  the other $G-1$ PEs, giving at least $\lceil\log_2 G\rceil$ merge stages
  plus one traversal of roughly half the network diameter. This is
  $\mathrm{lb}_{\text{gather}}$ in~\eqref{eq:omega} and it binds any reducer,
  deterministic or not.
\end{enumerate}

Terms 1 and 2 are the price of reproducibility and cannot be engineered away
without abandoning the contract. Terms 3 and 4 are shared with any reducer on
this fabric; term 3 is ours to fix, term 4 is nobody's.

\subsubsection{A falsifiable prediction}

Term 3 makes a specific, checkable claim: leaf generation should stop being
the binding constraint once $N/G$ exceeds roughly $6$--$8$. This is a
prediction about our measured cycle counts, and the suite spans
$N/G$ from $1$ (\sv{med\_full\_torus}) to $64$ (\sv{vlarge\_extreme\_1d}),
so it can fail.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{What the ABI Buys}

The paper's contribution is not the compiler's algorithms or the
fabric's microarchitecture --- both are composed from standard prior art
--- but the interface observation: if compiler and fabric agree on a
size-indexed reduction tree as a shared ABI, then cross-topology bitwise
invariance follows from composition. Every subsequent design decision
in the compiler and fabric amounts to \emph{discharging obligations}
(\textsf{C-*}) or \emph{establishing invariants} (\textsf{F-*}); none of
them changes the shape of the argument.

The practical payoff is that \sprs{} users can reshape their cluster
freely --- from $G=2$ to $G=4096$, from linear to hypercube --- without
touching their code, and still get the same bits out. Regression testing
becomes bit-for-bit across hardware generations. Replay debugging
survives migration to new silicon. ML training, rerun three months
later on a larger cluster, produces the same weights. These are
engineering outcomes that production collectives cannot provide today,
and that single-node techniques cannot address at all.

\subsection{When Not to Use the ABI}

Three regimes make \sprs{} a poor fit.

\emph{Interoperability with external reducers.} \sprs{}'s bit result
agrees across any reshape \emph{within} its own fabric-compiler pair,
but does not match NCCL, RCCL, ReproBLAS, ExBLAS, or any reducer using
a different canonical order. A deployment that must consume or produce
bit-compatible results with an external tool needs either (a)~a
one-time canonical-form translation, or (b)~an input permutation adapter
that presents leaves in the external tool's canonical order --- which
sacrifices the cross-reshape guarantee, trading one form of
reproducibility for another.

\emph{Strict IEEE 754.} Workloads whose reference implementation
exercises subnormals, multiple rounding modes, or the signed-zero
cancellation rule of IEEE 754-2019 \S6.3 will see \sprs{} diverge from
reference on those regimes (\S\ref{sec:fadd}). The narrower FTZ+RNE
semantics was a scope decision; a strict-IEEE FADD implementation
inherits Claim~\ref{claim:1} unchanged.

\emph{Bandwidth-critical inner loops.} The structural asymptote determined
by $\log_2 N$ versus $\log_G N$ is a real cost in inner loops where
every cycle is contested. Workloads that can tolerate reshape
non-reproducibility should use a non-deterministic reducer; the ABI
buys invariance at a non-zero price.

\subsection{Generalisation to Other Deterministic Operations}

Claim~\ref{claim:1} requires only (i) a deterministic binary operation
(for us, $\fadd$) and (ii) a size-indexed canonical tree. It does not
require floating-point, associativity, or any particular algebraic
property. Any associative or non-associative binary operation ---
FP32/FP16 ADD, integer ADD with saturating or modular arithmetic,
bitwise OR/AND/XOR, integer MAX/MIN, monoidal aggregations --- inherits
the claim by substituting the \sv{alu\_op} code. Reductions over ordered
types with a non-associative combine (string concatenation, stream
merges with priority) inherit the claim trivially because the canonical
tree \emph{is} the order.

Tensors follow by element-wise extension: reducing a tensor of $D$
elements under an ABI-compliant schedule is $D$ independent scalar
reductions. The fabric does not need per-element indexing; the compiler
emits $D$ parallel IMEM images, one per element, sharing the same
routing and the same PE assignment.

Non-reduction collectives --- all-gather, scatter, broadcast, alltoall
--- are orthogonal to the ABI: they have no intermediate combine, so
their results are bit-identical by construction on any routing
discipline. The ABI is specifically the statement that reductions, the
one collective with an internal combine, can be reproducible too.

\subsection{Exporting the ABI: Pinned-FBT for Production Collectives}\label{sec:discussion-pinfbt}

An important question follows: can the ABI discipline be adopted
without the \sprs{} fabric? We believe it can, via a software-only
construction we call \emph{pinned-FBT}, which we describe here as a
design and leave to future work to evaluate empirically.

The idea is simple: replace the production collective's dynamic
$G$-dependent reduction tree with a library that enforces the
$\FBT(N)$ post-order schedule, independent of $G$ or topology. A
rank-level implementation looks like:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Partition the $N$ leaves into $G$ ranks by a fixed
  $\mathrm{rank}(i) = \lfloor i \cdot G / N \rfloor$.  Ranks thus own
  canonical-leaf index ranges, and each rank's local reduction reduces
  exactly the subtree of $\FBT(N)$ rooted at the lowest common ancestor
  of its leaves.
\item Each rank computes its local sub-root deterministically via a
  software $\FBT$ traversal.
\item Ranks participate in a $\log_2 G$-depth all-reduce over
  sub-root values using an order consistent with $\FBT(N)$'s top
  $\log_2 G$ levels.
\end{enumerate}

This preserves Claim~\ref{claim:1} by construction: the final root is
$\Rcan(N, L)$ regardless of $G$ or topology, as long as the local and
cross-rank combinations both respect post-order and the FADD unit is
deterministic (e.g., by pinning \texttt{NCCL\_P2P\_DISABLE=1} and using
a compensated BLAS on each rank).

Pinned-FBT is an \emph{export} of the ABI: it trades our fabric's
structural overhead for a higher software overhead
(dynamic-tree libraries are throughput-tuned; a fixed tree will be
slower on some shapes), but it inherits the cross-topology invariance
property and requires no hardware change. It is the path we would
recommend for deployments that need the invariance guarantee today on
commodity silicon. A prototype on top of NCCL or oneCCL is planned as
a follow-up artefact.

\emph{The broader point.} The ABI framing decouples the
\emph{invariance property} from the \emph{mechanism that enforces it}.
A fabric-level enforcement (this paper) and a software-level enforcement
(pinned-FBT) satisfy the same structural obligation and yield the same
guarantee, trading off implementation overhead against hardware
dependence. Both are instances of one idea: reduction order as an
interface.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

We discuss related work along four axes: software reproducibility,
collective-scheduling compilers, in-network reduction, and deterministic
accelerators. Relative to each, our contribution is the \emph{framing}
of reduction order as a compiler/fabric ABI and the cross-topology
invariance property this enables.

\subsection{Software Reproducibility}

Compensated-summation techniques --- Kahan summation~\cite{kahan1965summation},
pairwise summation~\cite{higham2002accuracy}, Rump's accurate
summation~\cite{rump2009accurate}, TwoSum-based online
algorithms~\cite{higham2002accuracy} --- reduce the error bound of a
single reduction but do not constrain inter-worker order, so they
retain Source-1 non-determinism. Binned accumulation, as in
ReproBLAS~\cite{demmel2013reproBLAS}, and long-accumulator extensions
as in ExBLAS~\cite{iakymchuk2016exblas}, give order-independent
reductions at single-node scope, addressing Source~2 but not Source~1.
Neither class gives a cross-topology invariance guarantee: the reduction
order is still a function of $(G, \text{topology})$ in any
multi-worker deployment.

\subsection{Collective-Scheduling Compilers}

TACCL~\cite{shah2023taccl}, SCCL~\cite{cai2021synthesizing}, and
BLINK~\cite{wang2020blink} compile performance-optimised schedules for
collective operations. They share with \sprs{} the compiler-centric
view of the collective; they differ in objective (throughput versus
reproducibility) and in output (a schedule versus an ABI contract).
None of them states a cross-topology bitwise invariance property, nor
could they --- their schedules are explicitly
$(G,\text{topology})$-dependent by design. Our compiler is
architecturally closest to this line, but its contribution is not a new
search algorithm; it is the observation that a given size-indexed tree
yields the invariance property \emph{regardless} of which
search-algorithm-winning schedule the compiler emits.

Tree-construction algorithms for communication --- Huffman
trees~\cite{jeannot2014treematch}, TreeMatch's topology-aware trees ---
give communication-cost-optimal trees at fixed $(G, \text{topology})$.
Like the scheduling compilers, they do not yield cross-reshape
invariance.

\subsection{In-Network Reduction}

SHARP~\cite{graham2016scalable} on InfiniBand and P4-programmable
switches~\cite{sapio2021scaling} implement reductions inside the
network. SHARP fixes the reduction order at the switch hierarchy,
which makes per-cluster determinism achievable; cross-cluster-reshape
invariance would require matching switch hierarchies across
deployments, and no SHARP paper we know of makes this claim. Our
fabric-level contribution is ideologically adjacent --- pin the order
in hardware --- but the framing as a size-indexed ABI, and the
orthogonality theorem that follows, are new.

\subsection{Deterministic Accelerators}

The TPU~\cite{jouppi2017tpu} and Cambricon~\cite{liu2016cambricon}
product lines advertise per-chip deterministic execution. Cross-chip
reproducibility is provided by the vendor's collective library and
typically holds within a fixed-topology pod; rescaling across pods is
not guaranteed. Our contribution is at a different layer: not
per-chip determinism (which any accelerator can provide) but
cross-topology invariance of the chip-to-chip aggregation.

\subsection{NoC Design}

Dally and Towles' text~\cite{dally2004principles} is the
foundational reference for virtual channels, credit flow control, and
the dimension-ordered+escape discipline that our routing realises.
Our NoC is a textbook design; its only deliberate choice relevant to
our contribution is that topology is a runtime parameter of adjacency
and routing tables, not an RTL artefact. This choice is what lets the
tournament exercise Claim~\ref{claim:1} across six topologies with one
Verilog instantiation.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

We enumerate limitations explicitly to set the scope of the work.

\emph{L0 --- Coverage of the instance suite.} Hardware validation covers
$39$ of the $40$ instances. The exception is \sv{maxp\_mod\_fat}
($N{=}8192$, $G{=}4096$, fat-tree), the largest configuration in the suite:
five of its testbenches were attempted, each requiring $50$--$70$~GB of RAM
for elaboration alone, and all five exceeded the $28{,}800$~s wall-clock cap
without completing --- $58.9$ core-hours expended for zero settled results.
It is therefore absent from the HW-validated column at $N{=}8192$ in
Table~\ref{tab:invariance}, which rests on the two remaining
$(G,\text{topology})$ points at that leaf count. This is a resource limit,
not a negative result: the compiler produces an ABI-compliant image for the
instance, and its software oracle agrees with the $N{=}8192$ root reported by
the two validated configurations.

\emph{L0b --- Configuration-load bypass and cycle counts.} Route-table
initialisation is performed by hierarchical assignment rather than the
clocked \sv{write\_route()} protocol, because the latter costs
$\mathcal{O}(G^2)$ simulated cycles and dominates wall-clock at large $G$.
The transform is bit-exact for the reported root result --- every
\sv{PASS}/\sv{FAIL} verdict, and therefore every invariance claim in
\S\ref{sec:results-invariance}, is unaffected. It is \emph{not} exact for
cycle counts: against the clocked protocol, roughly $59\%$ of runs agree
bit-exactly and the remainder settle $1$--$54\%$ lower, with per-instance
pipeline rankings preserved in about $80\%$ of cases. Absolute cycle figures
should therefore be read as a lower envelope of the clocked-configuration
machine, and we draw no conclusion from cycle differences smaller than that
margin.

\emph{L1 --- Research-prototype silicon envelope.}
\ifdefined\fpgadata
FPGA validation covers $G \in \{2, 4\}$ only. At $G \geq 8$ we rely on
cycle-accurate xsim, which matches FPGA at the boundary
(Table~\ref{tab:fpga}) but is not silicon.
\else
All results in this paper come from cycle-accurate RTL simulation. FPGA
bring-up wrappers exist for $G \in \{2,4\}$ but we report no board
measurements, so no claim in this paper rests on silicon execution.
\fi
We make no claim of timing closure, area, power, or frequency
at production-die scale.

\emph{L1b --- No measured same-fabric overhead.} We report no $\eta$. The
non-deterministic baselines of \S\ref{sec:method-nd} were not implemented, so
the ABI's cost is priced structurally (\S\ref{sec:results-overhead}) and
anchored only on distance to $\Omega$, which upper-bounds what any
unconstrained reducer could recover. This is the single largest gap in the
evaluation and the first thing we would close given more time: it is the
difference between ``the cost is these four terms'' and ``the cost is
$x\times$''.

\emph{L2 --- No head-to-head with production collectives.} We do not
benchmark against NCCL, RCCL, or oneCCL on GPU silicon. Doing so would
conflate the ABI's cost with the (much larger) cost of our research
prototype versus production silicon. The same-fabric overhead metric is
the right isolation; a NCCL comparison would require either (a)~the
pinned-FBT prototype of \S\ref{sec:discussion-pinfbt}, or (b)~silicon
implementation of \sprs{}.

\emph{L3 --- Synthetic leaf values.} Equation~\eqref{eq:leaves} is a
designed-to-exercise-the-bits distribution, not a workload trace. A
workload with actual training gradients or scientific partial sums
will exercise different value regimes (especially subnormals, which
we FTZ). The invariance claim is value-agnostic by construction, but
a subnormal-aware alternative FADD is needed for deployments where
reference-library bit-parity matters.

\emph{L4 --- FTZ+RNE subset.} \S\ref{sec:fadd} documents the subset;
workloads depending on subnormals, multiple rounding modes, or the
IEEE signed-zero rule diverge from reference at the affected bits.

\emph{L5 --- Convenience sampling of instances.} The 40-instance suite
covers the $(N, G, \text{topology})$ space unevenly; some cells are thin
(Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}). Per-topology breakdowns are reported
only where cell counts permit.

\emph{L6 --- Phase 1/3 metric gap.} The tournament selects on software
\sv{makespan} (Phase 1/2) but evaluates on \sv{hw\_cycles} (Phase 3).
Appendix~\ref{app:bias} quantifies the resulting leakage across every
HW-validated instance: at the $K{=}15$ operating point a median
\TournLeak15{} of the true top-15 by measured cycles falls outside the
top-15 by makespan, dropping to \TournLeak30{} at $K{=}30$. Winner
distributions should therefore be read as ``best among what was simulated''.
A full tournament selected on \sv{hw\_cycles} is computationally infeasible.
This does not touch Claim~\ref{claim:1}, which quantifies over every
validated configuration rather than a selected subset.

\emph{L7 --- Head-of-line blocking at large $G$ on low-bisection
topologies.} Section~\ref{sec:fabric-lim}. Wormhole forwarding is
planned.

\emph{L8 --- Re-run ordering after S5 leaf-migration.} The \sv{sprs\_core}
pipeline re-runs S2.5 after memory enforcement's leaf migration but
\emph{not} S3/S4; in rare cases leaf migration could un-optimise the
mapping. Deferring memory enforcement until after S4 is a planned fix.

\emph{L9 --- Soft-enforced single-writer on the fabric.} \textsf{C-sw}
is a compile-time post-condition, not a fabric-level hardware veto
(\S\ref{sec:fabric-lim}). A one-line RTL extension closes the gap.

\emph{L10 --- Dynamic-only deadlock enforcement.} \textsf{C-route}
acyclicity is checked at compile time; no hardware dateline.

\emph{L11 --- Mutation testing is limited to two classes.} We inject
address-substitution and leaf-corruption only. A thorough fault-injection
study would cover scoreboard-set mutations, forwarding-network
mutations, credit-return mutations, and more. We view our results as a
lower bound on the harness's sensitivity.

\emph{L12 --- No formal proof of Claim~1.} Our argument is by code
inspection and dynamic assertion, not mechanised proof. The
Alloy/Kami path of \S\ref{sec:mechanization} is future work.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have argued that cluster-reshape non-determinism in floating-point
reductions is an \emph{interface} problem, not a library problem, and
that the right place to fix it is between a collective-scheduling
compiler and its underlying fabric. Reduction order, treated as a
size-indexed ABI over a canonical full binary tree, is orthogonal to
$G$, topology, assignment, mapping, refinement, and schedule, and
yields cross-topology bitwise invariance: the 64-bit result is a pure
function of $N$ and the leaf vector. Six compiler obligations and nine
fabric invariants suffice to make this formal; two load-bearing
compiler passes (connectivity repair, SU-ordered DMEM allocation) and
three fabric safeguards (scoreboard monotonicity, misroute drop,
write-arbitration retry latch) do the heavy lifting.

We instantiated the ABI as \sprs{}: a topology-parametric SystemVerilog
NoC plus a seven-stage compiler exploring ${\sim}22{,}000$ algorithmic
configurations. On forty instances across $N \in [8, 8192]$,
$G \in [2, 4096]$, and six direct-network topologies, all
HW-validated configurations produce bit-identical root results. A
mutation-testing harness confirms that the zero-divergence result is
non-vacuous, flagging every one of $84$ injected faults. We price the ABI
structurally rather than against a non-deterministic baseline, which remains
the principal open item in the evaluation.

The framing suggests a more portable form of the argument. \emph{Pinned-FBT},
a software-only export of the ABI discipline onto existing collective
libraries, inherits the invariance property without adopting our
fabric, at the cost of a (likely higher) software overhead. We sketch
it as a deployment path for commodity silicon and leave quantification
to future work.

Karypis and Kumar~\cite{karypis1998multilevel} with
Fiduccia--Mattheyses refinement~\cite{fiduccia1982linear}. The remaining
methods are our own constructions or straightforward adaptations, and are
marked as such in the released source rather than attributed to prior work.

\section{Worked Invariance Example at $N = 4$}\label{app:worked}
% =====================================================================

We trace a single execution at $N = 4$ on two different topologies to
show the invariance mechanism concretely.

Let $L = \langle 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0\rangle$. The canonical tree
$\FBT(4)$ has internal nodes $\{0,1,2\}$ and leaves $\{3,4,5,6\}$
with $\mathrm{idx}(3)=0, \mathrm{idx}(4)=1, \mathrm{idx}(5)=2,
\mathrm{idx}(6)=3$. The canonical post-order is
$\pi_4^{\text{internal}} = \langle 1, 2, 0\rangle$, so:
\[
\Rcan(4, L) = \fadd(\fadd(1.0, 2.0), \fadd(3.0, 4.0)) = 10.0.
\]

\emph{Configuration A: $G=2$, linear.} $\asgn = \{3\to 0, 4\to 0,
1\to 0, 5\to 1, 6\to 1, 2\to 1, 0\to 0\}$ (PE 0 owns the left subtree,
PE 1 the right subtree and sends its sub-root to PE 0). Emitted
instructions:
\begin{itemize}
\item PE 0: \texttt{GEN(dest=1, val=1.0)}; \texttt{GEN(dest=2, val=2.0)};
  \texttt{COMP(dest=3, addr\_a=1, addr\_b=2)}; receive at addr=4;
  \texttt{COMP(dest=5, addr\_a=3, addr\_b=4)}.
\item PE 1: \texttt{GEN(dest=1, val=3.0)}; \texttt{GEN(dest=2, val=4.0)};
  \texttt{COMP(dest=3, addr\_a=1, addr\_b=2)}; \texttt{SEND(src=3, dest=0, addr=4)}.
\end{itemize}
The operand bindings at PE~0's second \texttt{COMP} are
$(\mathrm{DMEM}_0[3], \mathrm{DMEM}_0[4]) = (\fadd(1.0,2.0),
\fadd(3.0,4.0))$, whose FADD gives $10.0$. \emph{Root result:} $10.0$.

\emph{Configuration B: $G=4$, 2D mesh with PE indices
$\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}$}. $\asgn = \{3\to(0,0), 4\to(0,1),
1\to(0,0), 5\to(1,0), 6\to(1,1), 2\to(1,0), 0\to(0,0)\}$ scatters
every leaf to its own PE; \texttt{SEND}s fan leaves to internal nodes;
\texttt{SEND}s fan internal-node sub-roots to the root. Operand bindings
at internal \texttt{COMP}s are identical by construction: the
\sv{tree.children(v)} function is topology-agnostic.
\emph{Root result:} $10.0$ (the same 64 bits, by Theorem~\ref{thm:orth}).

What changes between A and B: (i) the number and identity of the PEs;
(ii) the number of \texttt{SEND}s; (iii) the cycle count; (iv) which
routers carry which packets. What does not change: the operand pair
bound at each internal \texttt{COMP}, the order in which the
scoreboard-gated adds commit, and therefore the final 64 bits.

\section{Selection-Bias Ablation}\label{app:bias}
% =====================================================================

The tournament selects candidates for hardware simulation using the
compiler's software \sv{makespan}, then measures \sv{hw\_cycles}. If those
two rankings disagree, a shortlist chosen by the former can miss the true
hardware winner, and every ``winner'' statistic in this paper would inherit
that bias. We quantify it directly rather than assume it away.

For each HW-validated instance we compute the Spearman correlation between
the software makespan ranking and the measured cycle ranking over all its
configurations, and the \emph{leakage} at cutoff $K$: the fraction of the
true top-$K$-by-cycles that falls outside the top-$K$-by-makespan.

\input{tables/selbias}

The correlation is high ($\rho \approx \TournRho15$ at the median), but
leakage at the operating point is not negligible: at $K=15$ a median
\TournLeak15{} of the genuine top-15 would have been missed by a
makespan-chosen shortlist, falling to \TournLeak30{} at $K=30$. The
practical reading is that software makespan is a good \emph{filter} and a
poor \emph{selector} --- adequate for deciding what to simulate, not for
deciding what won.

This matters for the winner distributions of \S\ref{sec:results-tour} and
Appendix~\ref{app:algos}, which should be read as ``best among what was
simulated'', and we report them on that basis. It does not matter for
Claim~\ref{claim:1}: the invariance result is a statement about
\emph{every} HW-validated configuration rather than a selected subset, and
enlarging the set can only expose a divergence, never manufacture agreement.
